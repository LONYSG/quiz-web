'''Kabosu''' (カボス or 臭橙; binomial name: ''Citrus sphaerocarpa'') is a [[citrus]] fruit of an evergreen [[broad-leaf tree]] in the family [[Rutaceae]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.specialtyproduce.com/produce/Kabosu_Citrus_11772.php|title=Kabosu Citrus|website=www.specialtyproduce.com|access-date=2017-01-10}}</ref> It is popular in Japan, especially [[Ōita Prefecture]],<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |title=WHAT IS KABOSU {{!}} 大分県カボス振興協議会 |url=https://oitakabosu.com/en-about/ |access-date=2024-10-17 |website=OITA KABOSU |language=en}}</ref>  where its juice is used to improve the taste of many dishes, especially cooked fish, [[sashimi]], and [[hot pot]] dishes.

==Characteristics==
[[File:kabosu.jpg|thumb|right|150px|Cross-section of a kabosu, with two whole ones for comparison]]
Kabosu is a citrus fruit closely related to [[yuzu]].  Its juice has the sharpness of lemon, and it is used instead of vinegar in some Japanese dishes. It grows on a flowering tree with sharp thorns. The fruit is harvested when still green, but if left to ripen it turns yellow. It is often confused with similar citrus such as [[sudachi]], but can easily be distinguished by the apex of the fruit where the pistil has fallen off, which is a slightly raised [[torus]] shape.{{Cn|date=September 2023}}

==Origin==
Kabosu is thought to be an [[ichang papeda]] – [[bitter orange]] hybrid.{{Citation needed|date=October 2024}} It was not until the mid-[[Shōwa era|Showa period]] that "kabosu" was first mentioned in literature. A legend from [[Usuki, Ōita|Usuki]], [[Ōita Prefecture|Ōita]] says that a kabosu seed or sapling was brought to Ōita from [[Kyoto]] in the [[Edo period]] by a doctor named Sogen, beginning cultivation in the prefecture.<ref name=":0" /> 

However, as there are kabosu trees that can be found in Ōita Prefecture that are older than those found anywhere else in Japan, kabosu are now thought to be native to the prefecture.<ref name=":0" /> In Usuki, there are many 200-year-old trees with some thought to be about 300 years old.<ref>{{cite web |title=The origin of Kabosu |url=http://www.oitakabosu.com/information/ |access-date=16 August 2014 |website=Ōita Kabosu - Official site |publisher=The Ōita Kabosu promotion council |archive-date=19 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200119102947/http://www.oitakabosu.com/information/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

During the 1960s, cultivation of kabosu in Ōita increased and shipments of the fruit to other [[Prefectures of Japan|prefectures]] began.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |title=かぼすについて {{!}} 大分県カボス振興協議会 |url=https://oitakabosu.com/about/ |access-date=2024-10-17 |website=OITA KABOSU |language=ja}}</ref> Production was promoted by the prefecture during the 1970s and sales and shipments continued to grow. This trend continued through the 1980s and 90s, making kabosu known nationwide as an Ōita ''[[meibutsu]]'' (local speciality).<ref name=":1" />

==Usage==
Kabosu [[juice]] is rich in [[sourness]], with a unique [[fragrance]]. It is used with [[sashimi]], grilled fish, [[ponzu]] for [[hot pot]], and as a vinegar alternative for Japanese dishes. In Ōita Prefecture it is also used with [[miso soup]], [[noodles]], and [[shōchū]], by adding the juice for flavoring. Squeezing vertically cut radial quarters with the peel side down prevents the seeds from entering the dish or cup while adding the juice. Kabosu juice is used in a wide range of products including [[condiments]], [[juices]], [[non-alcoholic beverage]]s, [[frozen dessert]]s, [[snack foods]], [[wagashi]], [[pastries]], and [[alcoholic beverages]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Pask |first1=Katie |title=We try Oita Prefecture’s citrus-y creation in Starbucks Japan’s 47 Jimoto Frappuccinos |url=https://soranews24.com/2021/07/04/we-try-oita-prefectures-citrus-y-creation-in-starbucks-japans-47-jimoto-frappuccinos/ |access-date=12 July 2024 |work=SoraNews24 |date=July 4, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=カボス加工品 |url=http://www.oitakabosu.com/purchase/processing/ |website=大分県カボス振興協議会 |access-date=12 July 2024 |archive-date=19 December 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191219002215/http://www.oitakabosu.com/purchase/processing/ |url-status=bot: unknown }}</ref>

When mixed in fish feed, the [[polyphenol]]s in kabosu prevent discoloration and odor in fish meat for longer time periods. [[Japanese amberjack]] ''(buri)'' and [[Summer flounder]] ''(hirame)'' grown using this feed are marketed as [[:ja:かぼすブリ|Kabosu Buri]] and [[:ja:かぼすヒラメ|Kabosu Hirame]] in Ōita Prefecture.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20120416204310/http://www.oita-press.co.jp/localNews/2009_126213622969.html Kabosu mixed feed delays discoloration of Japanese amberjack meat] - Ōita Press 30 December 2009</ref><ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20120303120920/http://www.oita-press.co.jp/localNews/2010_129159762438.html Kabosu induced feed improves taste of Japanese amberjack and fluke] - Ōita Press 12 June 2010</ref>

==Production==
[[File:W kabosu1111.jpg|thumb|Kabosu on tree]]
In 2018, Ōita Prefecture accounted for about 99% of kabosu production in Japan.<ref name=":1" /> It is produced in most areas of the prefecture, particularly [[Usuki, Ōita|Usuki]], [[Taketa, Ōita|Taketa]], [[Bungo-Ōno]], and [[Bungotakada, Ōita|Bungotakada]].<ref name=":0" /> National Japanese production in 2007 was 5,185 tons. Prefecture-specific production volumes that year were 5,019 tons in [[Ōita Prefecture]], 144 tons in [[Aichi Prefecture]], and 17 tons in [[Miyazaki Prefecture]], and volume in the main producing district of Ōita Prefecture was 97% of national production.<ref>{{cite web|title=2007 annual specialty fruit production white paper|url=http://www.e-stat.go.jp/SG1/estat/List.do?lid=000001059460|website=e-stat.go.jp|publisher=Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications Statistics Bureau of Japan|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref> There are good and bad years for Kabosu production; 2009 was a good year and the volume in Ōita Prefecture was about 6,587 tons.<ref>{{cite web|title=2009 Kabosu production in Ōita|url=http://www.e-stat.go.jp/SG1/estat/GL02020101.do?method=xlsDownload&fileId=000006771238&releaseCount=3|website=e-stat.go.jp|publisher=Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications Statistics Bureau of Japan|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref> The annual production in Ōita Prefecture was 3,623 tons in 2010,<ref>{{cite web|title=2010 Kabosu production in Ōita|url=http://www.e-stat.go.jp/SG1/estat/GL02020101.do?method=xlsDownload&fileId=000006771238&releaseCount=3|website=e-stat.go.jp|publisher=Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications Statistics Bureau of Japan|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref> and 5,273 tons in 2011.<ref>{{cite web|title=Statistics for Ōita Prefecture|url=http://www.stat.go.jp/naruhodo/c3loc44.htm|website=stat.go.jp|publisher=Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications Statistics Bureau of Japan|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=2011 Kabosu production in Ōita|url=http://www.e-stat.go.jp/SG1/estat/GL02020101.do?method=xlsDownload&fileId=000006771238&releaseCount=3|website=e-stat.go.jp|publisher=Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications Statistics Bureau of Japan|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref> 

Kabosu is in season from August to March, but stored kabosu or kabosu grown in [[Greenhouse|greenhouses]] allow for the fruit to be available year-round.<ref name=":0" />

==Character==
A kabosu-motif mascot character called [[:ja:カボたん|Kabotan]] was created for the [[:ja:全国都市緑化フェア|National Greening Fair]] held in Ōita in 2003. The Ōita Kabosu  promotion council chose this character as the mascot for "Ōita Kabosu" after the fair.<ref name=Kabotan>{{cite web|title=Kabotan|url=http://www.oitakabosu.com/kabotan/|website=Ōita Kabosu - Official Site|publisher=The Ōita Kabosu promotion council|access-date=15 August 2014}}</ref> In 2005, Kabotan's use was extended to regional development in general in Ōita Prefecture, even beyond Kabosu production.<ref>[http://www.pref.oita.jp/10400/gov_news/h16/01/27_kabo.html Application for trademark registration of "Kabotan"] {{webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130617150049/http://www.pref.oita.jp/10400/gov_news/h16/01/27_kabo.html |date=2013-06-17 }} - Ōita Prefecture</ref><ref name="Kabotan usage">{{cite web|title=2001 Kabosu memory|url=http://www.oitakabosu.com/archives/topics/2011.html|website=Ōita Kabosu|publisher=Ōita Kabosu - Official site|access-date=15 August 2014|archive-date=5 April 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160405170302/http://www.oitakabosu.com/archives/topics/2011.html|url-status=dead}}</ref>

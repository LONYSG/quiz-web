'''Spotify'''{{efn|{{IPAc-en|ˈ|s|p|ɒ|t|ɪ|f|aɪ}}; {{IPA|sv|ˈspɔ̂tːɪfaj|lang}}}} is a Swedish [[freemium]] [[music streaming service]] provider founded in April 2006 by [[Daniel Ek]] and [[Martin Lorentzon]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=14 October 2016 |title=Spotify UK revenues surge to almost £190m as mobile subscriptions take off |url=http://www.theguardian.com/media/2016/oct/14/spotify-uk-revenues-surge-to-almost-190m-as-mobile-subscriptions-take-off |access-date=6 August 2021 |website=The Guardian |language=en |archive-date=17 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220717195247/https://www.theguardian.com/media/2016/oct/14/spotify-uk-revenues-surge-to-almost-190m-as-mobile-subscriptions-take-off |url-status=live}}</ref> {{As of|2026|03}}, it is one of the largest providers of music streaming services, with over 788 million monthly [[active users]] comprising 306 million paying subscribers.<ref name="About Spotify">{{Cite web |date=22 October 2026 |title=About Spotify |url=https://newsroom.spotify.com/company-info/ |access-date=22 October 2026 |website=Spotify}}</ref> Spotify is listed on the [[New York Stock Exchange]] in the form of [[American depositary receipt]]s.

Spotify offers [[Digital rights management|DRM]]-protected audio content, including over 100 million songs, 8 million [[podcast]] titles, and 800,000 [[audiobook]] titles from record labels and media companies.<ref name="About Spotify" /> Operating as a [[freemium]] service, the basic features are free with advertisements and limited control, while additional features, such as offline listening and commercial-free listening, are offered via paid [[Subscription business model|subscriptions]]. Users can search for music based on artist, album, or [[Music genre|genre]], and can create, edit, and share [[playlist]]s. It offers some [[social media]] features such as messaging, creating profiles, following friends, shared playlists, and creating listening parties called "Jams".

As of December 2022, Spotify is available in most of Europe, as well as Africa, the [[Americas]], Asia, and [[Oceania]], with availability in a total of 184 markets.<ref name="About Spotify" /> Its users and subscribers are based largely in the [[United States]] and Europe, jointly accounting for around 53% of users and 67% of revenue.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Spotify Q3 2022 investor presentation |url=https://s29.q4cdn.com/175625835/files/doc_financials/2022/q3/Q3-2022-Shareholder-Deck-FINAL-LOCKED.pdf |access-date=7 January 2023 |archive-date=23 November 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221123020252/https://s29.q4cdn.com/175625835/files/doc_financials/2022/q3/Q3-2022-Shareholder-Deck-FINAL-LOCKED.pdf |url-status=live}}</ref> It has no presence in [[mainland China]], where the market is dominated by [[QQ Music]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Zeb |first=Engr Khan |date=5 August 2025 |title=Does Spotify Work in China? |url=https://avenuear.com/2025/08/04/does-spotify-work-in-china/ |access-date=28 October 2025 |website=AvenueAR |language=en-US}}</ref> The service is available on most devices, including [[Microsoft Windows|Windows]], [[macOS]], and [[Linux]] computers, [[iOS]] and [[Android (operating system)|Android]] smartphones and tablets, [[Home automation|smart home]] devices such as the [[Amazon Echo]] and [[Google Nest]] lines of products, and [[digital media player]]s like [[Roku]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Spotify Music |url=https://channelstore.roku.com/details/19977/spotify |access-date=12 November 2018 |website=Roku Channel Store |archive-date=28 September 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200928012717/https://channelstore.roku.com/details/19977/spotify |url-status=dead}}</ref> As of December 2023, Spotify was the [[List of most-visited websites|47th most-visited website in the world]] with 24.78% of its traffic coming from the United States followed by Brazil with 6.51% according to data provided by [[Semrush]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Top websites|url=https://www.semrush.com/trending-websites/global/all|website=Semrush|access-date=25 December 2023|archive-date=19 December 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231219181830/https://www.semrush.com/trending-websites/global/all|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.semrush.com/website/spotify.com/overview/ |title=spotify.com|website=[[Semrush]]|access-date=25 December 2023|archive-date=25 December 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231225141959/https://www.semrush.com/website/spotify.com/overview/ |url-status=live}}</ref> As of 2022, Spotify is the current sponsor of Spanish football club [[FC Barcelona]], with music artists like [[Drake (musician)|Drake]], [[Travis Scott]], and [[Ed Sheeran]] collaborating with the club by changing their shirts into their artist logo, sometimes used for album promotions.

Unlike physical or download sales, which pay artists a fixed price per song or album sold, Spotify pays [[Royalty payment#Music|royalties]] based on the number of artist streams as a proportion of total songs streamed. It distributes approximately 70% of its total revenue to rights holders (often record labels), who then pay artists based on individual agreements.<ref name=":22">{{Cite news |last=Sehgal |first=Kabir |date=26 January 2018 |title=Spotify and Apple Music should become record labels so musicians can make a fair living |publisher=CNBC |url=https://www.cnbc.com/2018/01/26/how-spotify-apple-music-can-pay-musicians-more-commentary.html |access-date=21 November 2018 |archive-date=24 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200224043025/https://www.cnbc.com/2018/01/26/how-spotify-apple-music-can-pay-musicians-more-commentary.html |url-status=live}}</ref> While certain musicians laud the service for offering a lawful option to combat piracy and for remunerating artists each time their music is played, others have voiced objections to Spotify's royalty structure and its effect on record sales.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Swanson |first=Kate |year=2013 |title=A Case Study on Spotify: Exploring Perceptions of the Music Streaming Service |url=http://www.meiea.org/Journal/Vol13/Swanson |journal=Journal of the Music and Entertainment Industry Educators Association |volume=13 |issue=1 |pages=207–230 |doi=10.25101/13.10 |doi-access=free |access-date=5 November 2023 |archive-date=1 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231201024516/https://www.meiea.org/Journal/Vol13/Swanson |url-status=live |issn = 1559-7334}}</ref>

== History ==
{{Main|History of Spotify}}
Spotify was founded in April 2006 in [[Stockholm]], Sweden, by [[Daniel Ek]] and [[Martin Lorentzon]]. Conceived as a legal alternative to music piracy, the service launched in several European markets in October 2008 and in the United States in July 2011.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2 March 2018 |title=The story of Spotify: Sweden's controversial king of music streaming |url=https://www.thelocal.se/20180302/the-story-of-spotify-swedens-controversial-king-of-music-streaming |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200602210017/https://www.thelocal.se/20180302/the-story-of-spotify-swedens-controversial-king-of-music-streaming |archive-date=2 June 2020 |access-date=31 May 2020 |work=The Local Sweden}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Spotify — Spotify: A Visual History |url=https://newsroom.spotify.com/spotify-timeline/ |access-date=14 May 2026 |website=Spotify |language=en-US}}</ref>

During the 2010s, Spotify expanded internationally and grew from a music-streaming service into a broader audio platform through podcasting, audiobooks and acquisitions. It became publicly traded on the [[New York Stock Exchange]] in April 2018 through a [[direct public offering]].<ref name="Spotify 23bn3">{{Cite news |date=1 March 2018 |title=Is Spotify really worth $23bn? |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/business-43235317 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180228231416/http://www.bbc.com/news/business-43235317 |archive-date=28 February 2018 |access-date=1 March 2018 |work=BBC News}}</ref>

In the 2020s, Spotify continued to expand its podcast, audiobook and creator services while facing disputes over app-store policies, licensing and royalty distribution. In 2024, it reported 252 million subscribers and 640 million monthly active users; by the end of 2025, these figures had risen to 290 million Premium subscribers and 751 million monthly active users.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Spotify |date=12 November 2024 |title=Spotify Reports Third Quarter 2024 Earnings |url=https://newsroom.spotify.com/2024-11-12/spotify-reports-third-quarter-2024-earnings/ |access-date=13 November 2024 |website=Spotify Newsroom}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=10 February 2026 |title=Spotify Reports Fourth Quarter 2025 Earnings |url=https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-10/spotify-q4-2025-earnings/ |access-date=18 August 2026 |website=Spotify Newsroom}}</ref>

== Unionization ==
Spotify recognizes [[trade union]]s at its US podcasting subsidiaries [[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]] and Spotify Studios since 2019. In Germany, a [[works council]] was established in 2023. Swedish trade unions have unsuccessfully attempted to [[collective bargaining|bargain collectively]] with Spotify since 2023.

=== Germany ===
Spotify [[GmbH]] employees in Berlin established an [[electoral board (works council)|electoral board]] in February 2023,<ref>{{Cite web |date=6 February 2023 |title=Bei Spotify wird ein Betriebsrat gewählt |trans-title=Spotify is electing a Works Council |url=https://mmm.verdi.de/gewerkschaft/bei-spotify-wird-ein-betriebsrat-gewaehlt-86837/ |access-date=24 June 2024 |website=[[ver.di|[Menschen Machen Medien]] |language=de-DE |archive-date=2 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241002004906/https://mmm.verdi.de/gewerkschaft/bei-spotify-wird-ein-betriebsrat-gewaehlt-86837/ |url-status=live}}</ref> which prepared the election for the [[works council]] in April.<ref>{{Cite tweet |number=1649410165978939393 |user=TechWorkersBER |title=This is not exactly a @SpotifyDE album release...🥳 Berlin @Spotify employees formed a Works Council today! 🎶 Three months of campaigning with @verdi_tech 💪 |author=[[Tech Workers Coalition]] |access-date=24 June 2024}}</ref>{{Primary source inline|date=June 2024}}

=== Sweden ===
Spotify [[Aktiebolag|AB]] does not recognize any trade unions or have any [[collective agreement]]s in Sweden. Spotify ended joint-negotiations with the three trade unions [[Unionen]], Akavia, and [[Engineers of Sweden]] (both affiliates of [[Swedish Confederation of Professional Associations|SACO]]) in August 2023. The three unions petitioned Spotify to negotiate back in May. 90% of Swedish workers are [[Collective agreement coverage|covered by collective agreements]]. In tech companies bargaining coverage is less common. Swedish labor disputes are also happening at [[Tesla and trade unions#Sweden|Tesla]] and [[Klarna]] {{As of|2024|lc=y}}.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lund |first=Sandra |date=27 October 2023 |title=Tech companies disrupt the Swedish model |url=https://www.arbetsvarlden.se/tech-companies-disrupt-the-swedish-model/ |access-date=24 June 2024 |website=[[Arbetsvärlden]] |archive-date=24 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240624150232/https://www.arbetsvarlden.se/tech-companies-disrupt-the-swedish-model/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=9 August 2023 |title=Spotify pulls out of negotiations with Swedish trade unions |url=https://www.thelocal.se/20230809/spotify-pulls-out-of-negotiations-with-swedish-trade-unions |work=[[The Local]] |access-date=24 June 2024 |archive-date=24 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240624144728/https://www.thelocal.se/20230809/spotify-pulls-out-of-negotiations-with-swedish-trade-unions |url-status=live}}</ref>

In November 2022, Henry Catalini Smith, a Spotify engineer in [[Malmö]], set up the channel #kollektivavtal in the internal company [[Slack Technologies|Slack]], which means "[[collective agreement]]" in Swedish. The channel grew to 2,000 participants. 700 employees have since joined Unionen, with another 100 each joining Engineers of Sweden and Akavia.<ref>{{Cite news |date=14 April 2023 |title=Interview: 'Spotify is going to find that a union deal is a competitive advantage' |url=https://www.thelocal.se/20230414/interview-spotify-is-going-to-find-that-a-union-deal-is-a-competitive-advantage |work=[[The Local]]}}</ref> Catalini Smith no longer works at Spotify.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lund |first=Sandra |date=15 February 2024 |title=The CBA struggle at Spotify traumatized him |url=https://www.arbetsvarlden.se/the-cba-struggle-at-spotify-traumatized-him/ |access-date=24 June 2024 |website=[[Arbetsvärlden]] |archive-date=2 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241002004908/https://www.arbetsvarlden.se/the-cba-struggle-at-spotify-traumatized-him/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== United States ===
[[Writers Guild of America East|Writers Guild of America, East]] represents two union affiliates at Spotify Studios and [[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Kaloi |first=Stephanie |date=15 March 2024 |title=WGA East Members at Spotify and The Ringer Ratify New Contracts |url=https://www.thewrap.com/wga-east-members-at-spotify-and-the-ringer-ratify-new-contracts/ |access-date=24 June 2024 |website=[[TheWrap]] |language=en-US |archive-date=24 June 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240624144729/https://www.thewrap.com/wga-east-members-at-spotify-and-the-ringer-ratify-new-contracts/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

The United Musicians and Allied Workers campaigns for a fairer redistribution and compensation system for musicians.<ref name="UMAW">{{Cite news |last=Selinger |first=Julia |date=20 March 2024 |title=Musicians want streamers to pay a living wage. Can this bill make that happen? |url=https://www.fastcompany.com/91063051/new-bill-living-wage-musicians-tlaib-umaw |work=[[Fast Company]] |access-date=24 June 2024 |archive-date=2 October 2024 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20241002004908/https://www.fastcompany.com/91063051/new-bill-living-wage-musicians-tlaib-umaw |url-status=live}}</ref> The United Musicians and Allied Workers (UMAW) was established in 2020 during the [[COVID-19 pandemic]]. One year later, in 31 cities worldwide, 27,500 musicians joined UMAW's campaign #JusticeAtSpotify to demand a compensation of one cent per audio [[Streaming media|stream]]. == Business model ==
[[File:Spotify HK Stockholm.jpg|thumb|Spotify offices in Stockholm, 2024]]
Spotify operates under a [[freemium]] business model (basic services are free, while additional features are offered via paid subscriptions). Spotify generates revenue by selling premium streaming subscriptions to users and advertising placements to third parties. Some of the premium options users may choose from include individual, duo, family, and student.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Spotify Premium |url=http://www.spotify.com/us/premium/ |access-date=14 February 2022 |website=Spotify |language=en-US |archive-date=13 February 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220213121532/https://www.spotify.com/us/premium/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

In December 2013, the company launched a new website, "Spotify for Artists", explaining its business model and revenue data. Spotify gets its content from major record labels as well as [[Unsigned artist|independent artists]] and pays [[copyright]] holders [[Royalty payment|royalties]] for streaming music. The company pays about 70% of its total revenue to rights holders.<ref name="Spotify business"/> Of that amount, about 58.5% of its total revenue goes to the owners of [[sound recording copyright]]s, and the other 12% goes to the owners of [[musical composition]]s. In the United States, this is split between 6% mechanical royalties (paid via the [[Mechanical Licensing Collective]]) and 6% performance royalties (paid via [[Performance rights organisation|performing rights organizations]] or PROs).<ref name="manatt-2016">{{Cite web |url=https://www.manatt.com/Manatt/media/Media/PDF/US-Streaming-Royalties-Explained.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180203080959/https://www.manatt.com/Manatt/media/Media/PDF/US-Streaming-Royalties-Explained.pdf |archive-date=3 February 2018 |year=2016 |title=U.S. Music Streaming Royalties Explained: a.k.a. How Do Artists Get $$$ from Spotify and Apple? |website=[[Manatt, Phelps & Phillips]]}}</ref><ref name="spotify-mlc">{{cite web |url=https://artists.spotify.com/en/blog/collecting-mechanical-royalties-can-be-tricky-the-mlc-is-here-to-fix-that |title=Collecting Mechanical Royalties Can Be Tricky. The MLC Is Here to Fix That |date=21 March 2022 |website=Spotify for Artists |access-date=5 October 2025}}</ref>{{efn|[[Manatt, Phelps & Phillips]] estimated in 2016 that about 58.5% of Spotify's total revenue goes to master royalties, 6% to mechanical royalties, and 6.12% to performance royalties for compositions. The total percentage that goes to rights holders is 70.62%, which exceeds 70%.<ref name="manatt-2016"/>}}

Spotify for Artists states that the company does not have a fixed per-play rate; instead, it considers factors such as the user's home country and the individual artist's royalty rate. Rights holders received an average per-play payout between $.000029 and $.0084.<ref name="Spotify business">{{Cite web |title=How is Spotify contributing to the music business? |url=http://www.spotifyartists.com/spotify-explained/ |archive-url=https://wayback.archive-it.org/all/20141103193456/http://www.spotifyartists.com/spotify-explained/ |archive-date=3 November 2014 |access-date=7 November 2016 |website=Spotify}}</ref>

Today, royalties on all streaming services, including Spotify are paid on a per user basis not per stream as this allows the artists who users listen to the most to receive the largest percentage of the payouts. In 2013, Spotify revealed that it paid artists an average of $0.007 per stream. ''[[Music Week]]'' editor Tim Ingham commented that while the figure may "initially seem alarming", he noted: "Unlike buying a CD or download, streaming is not a one-off payment. Hundreds of millions of streams of tracks are happening every day, which quickly multiplies the potential revenues on offer—and is a constant long-term source of income for artists."<ref>{{Cite web |date=4 December 2013 |title=Spotify reveals artists earn $0.007 per stream |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-25217353 |access-date=22 January 2017 |website=BBC News |archive-date=6 February 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206123918/http://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-25217353 |url-status=live}}</ref> According to [[Ben Sisario]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'', approximately 13,000 out of seven million artists (0.19%) on Spotify generated $50,000 ({{Inflation|index=US-GDP|value=50000|start_year=2020|r=-1|fmt=eq}}) or more in payments in 2020.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Ovide |first=Shira |date=22 March 2021 |title=Streaming Saved Music. Artists Hate It. |language=en-US |work=The New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/22/technology/streaming-music-economics.html |url-access=limited |access-date=26 March 2021 |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20211228/https://www.nytimes.com/2021/03/22/technology/streaming-music-economics.html |archive-date=28 December 2021}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

In November 2023, Spotify announced a new royalty model taking effect in 2024, aiming to reduce the amount of "fraudulent" royalties collected from "functional" non-music tracks with short lengths (such as environmental sounds and [[white noise]]). Under the model, all royalties go to songs with at least 1,001 listens. Meaning that tracks must reach at least 1,000 listens in 12 months as well as a minimum number of unique listeners to become eligible for sound recording royalties, "functional" tracks will require a longer amount of play time to count as a listen, and distributors will face reprimands if their content is responsible for generating "fraudulent" royalties. This eligibility rule only applies to royalties for sound recordings, not musical compositions.<ref name="spotify-track-eligibility">{{cite web |url=https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/track-monetization-eligibility/ |title=Track monetization eligibility |website=Spotify for Artists |access-date=5 October 2025}}</ref> The changes faced a mixed reaction from the music industry, who believed that it would be detrimental to emerging musicians, but would make a larger share of total royalty payments available to musicians.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Aswad |first=Jem |date=6 November 2023 |title=What Spotify's New Royalty Model Really Means |url=https://variety.com/2023/music/news/spotify-new-royalty-model-really-means-1235781554/ |access-date=15 December 2023 |website=Variety |language=en-US |archive-date=15 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231215033131/https://variety.com/2023/music/news/spotify-new-royalty-model-really-means-1235781554/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2 November 2023 |title=Spotify's New Royalty Model Explained by an Expert |url=https://consequence.net/2023/11/spotify-royalty-model-op-ed/ |access-date=15 December 2023 |website=Consequence of Sound |language=en-US |archive-date=15 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231215033131/https://consequence.net/2023/11/spotify-royalty-model-op-ed/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=5 November 2023 |title=Confirmed: From 2024, tracks on Spotify will have to be played 1,000 times before they start earning money |url=https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/confirmed-next-year-tracks-on-spotify-1000-plays/ |access-date=15 December 2023 |website=Music Business Worldwide |language=en-US |archive-date=15 December 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231215033131/https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/confirmed-next-year-tracks-on-spotify-1000-plays/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Downloads ===
In March 2009, Spotify began offering [[music download]]s in the United Kingdom, France, and Spain. In September 2018, Spotify announced "Upload Beta", allowing artists to upload directly to the platform instead of going through a [[music industry|distributor]] or record label.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Deahl |first=Dani |date=20 September 2018 |title=Spotify will now let artists directly upload their music to the platform |url=https://www.theverge.com/2018/9/20/17879840/spotify-artist-direct-upload-independent-music |access-date=10 April 2019 |website=The Verge |archive-date=10 April 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190410092646/https://www.theverge.com/2018/9/20/17879840/spotify-artist-direct-upload-independent-music |url-status=live}}</ref> The feature was rolled out to a small number of US-based artists by invitation only. === Industry initiatives ===
In June 2017, ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' reported that Spotify would announce "Secret Genius", a new initiative aimed at highlighting songwriters and producers, and the effect those people have on the music industry and the artists' careers. The project, which would feature awards, "Songshops" songwriting workshops, curated playlists, and podcasts, is an effort to "shine a light on these people behind the scenes who play such a big role in some of the most important moments of our lives. When the general public hears a song, they automatically associate it with the artist who sings it, not the people behind the scenes who make it happen, so we thought the title Secret Genius was appropriate", Spotify's former Global Head of Creator Services [[Troy Carter (talent manager)|Troy Carter]] told ''Variety'' the first awards ceremony would take place in late 2017,{{update inline|date=March 2019}} and was intended to honour "the top songwriters, producers and publishers in the industry as well as up-and-coming talent". Additionally, as part of "The Ambassador Program", 13 songwriters would each host a Songshop workshop, in which their peers would collaboratively attempt to create a hit song, with the first workshop taking place in Los Angeles in June 2017.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Aswad |first=Jem |date=8 June 2017 |title=Spotify Launches 'Secret Genius' Program to Celebrate Songwriters (EXCLUSIVE) |url=https://variety.com/2017/music/news/exclusive-spotify-launches-secret-genius-for-songwriters-1202458229/ |access-date=9 June 2017 |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |archive-date=8 June 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170608135636/http://variety.com/2017/music/news/exclusive-spotify-launches-secret-genius-for-songwriters-1202458229/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Stations by Spotify ===
On 31 January 2018, Spotify started testing a new [[Pandora (service)|Pandora]]-styled standalone app called Stations by Spotify for Australian Android users.<ref>{{Cite news |title=Spotify's new Stations app is all about playlists without any of the faff |work=Alphr |url=http://www.alphr.com/spotify/1008362/spotify-stations-app-playlists |access-date=25 May 2018 |archive-date=25 May 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180525205237/http://www.alphr.com/spotify/1008362/spotify-stations-app-playlists |url-status=dead}}</ref> It featured 62 music channels, each devoted to a particular genre. Spotify itself has two channels named after its playlists that link directly to the users' profile: "Release Radar" and "Discover Weekly". The aim was to help users to listen to the music they want without [[information overload]] or spending time building their own playlists. At launch, the skipping feature was not featured to "reinforce the feel of radio", but it was quietly added later and with no limits. Songs can be "loved" but cannot be "hated". If a song is "loved", a custom radio channel will be created based on it, and when there are at least 15 of these songs, a "My Favourites" channel is unlocked.

The standalone app was made available to all iOS and Android users in the United States since 4 June 2019.<ref>[https://www.theverge.com/2019/6/4/18651865/spotify-stations-ios-release-date-news-features-australia-playlists Spotify Stations is now available in the US] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210116231700/https://www.theverge.com/2019/6/4/18651865/spotify-stations-ios-release-date-news-features-australia-playlists/ |date=16 January 2021}}&nbsp;– ''[[The Verge]]'' (published 4 June 2019)</ref>

Spotify announced the app would be shut down on 16 May 2022. The company said users would be able to login into the main Spotify app with their Stations account and transfer their stations into Spotify.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lawler |first=Richard |date=6 May 2022 |title=Spotify will shut down its radio-like listening app Stations on May 16th |url=https://www.theverge.com/2022/5/6/23060239/spotify-stations-experiment-may-16th-radio-pandora |access-date=7 May 2022 |website=The Verge |archive-date=6 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220506202340/https://www.theverge.com/2022/5/6/23060239/spotify-stations-experiment-may-16th-radio-pandora |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Listening limitations ===
Spotify has experimented with different limitations to users' listening on the Free service tier.

In April 2011, Spotify announced via a blog post that it would drastically cut the amount of music that free members could access, effective 1 May 2011. The post stated that all free members would be limited to ten hours of music streaming per month, and in addition, individual tracks were limited to five plays. New users were exempt from these changes for six months.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Butcher |first=Mike |date=14 April 2011 |title=Spotify takes the axe to its free service&nbsp;– can it now claim to slash music piracy? |url=https://techcrunch.com/2011/04/14/spotify-takes-the-axe-to-its-free-service-can-it-now-claim-to-slash-music-piracy/ |access-date=2 June 2017 |website=[[TechCrunch]] |archive-date=14 June 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170614085717/https://techcrunch.com/2011/04/14/spotify-takes-the-axe-to-its-free-service-can-it-now-claim-to-slash-music-piracy/ |url-status=live}}</ref> In March 2013, the five-play individual track limit was removed for users in the United Kingdom, and media reports stated that users in the United States, Australia, and New Zealand never had the limit in the first place.<ref>{{Cite web |last=O'Hear |first=Steve |date=19 March 2013 |title=Spotify Ditches Its 5 Play Limit For Spotify Free Users In The UK |url=https://techcrunch.com/2013/03/19/spotify-freetards/ |access-date=2 June 2017 |website=[[TechCrunch]] |archive-date=4 July 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170704015953/https://techcrunch.com/2013/03/19/spotify-freetards/ |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Richmond |first=Shane |date=19 March 2013 |title=Spotify removes five-play song cap |url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/technology/news/9940432/Spotify-removes-five-play-song-cap.html |url-access=subscription |url-status=live |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/archive/20220110/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/technology/news/9940432/Spotify-removes-five-play-song-cap.html |archive-date=10 January 2022 |access-date=2 June 2017 |website=[[The Daily Telegraph]]}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

Spotify is [[Proprietary software|proprietary]] and uses [[digital rights management]] (DRM) controls.<ref name="DRM">{{Cite web |last=Orlowski |first=Andrew |date=31 August 2009 |title=Spotify, DRM and the celestial jukebox |url=https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2009/08/31/spotify-drm-and-the-celestial-jukebox/1362574 |access-date=8 November 2016 |website=[[The Register]] |archive-date=6 February 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170206124200/http://www.theregister.co.uk/2009/08/31/spotify_crypto |url-status=live}}</ref> Spotify's [[Contractual term|terms and conditions]] do not permit users to [[Reverse engineering|reverse-engineer]] the application.

Spotify allows users to add local audio files for music not in its catalog into the user's library through Spotify's desktop application, and then allows users to synchronize those music files to Spotify's mobile apps or other computers over the same [[Wi-Fi]] network as the primary computer by creating a Spotify playlist, and adding those local audio files to the playlist. Audio files must either be in the .mp3, .mp4 (.mp4 files that have video streams are not supported), or .m4p media formats. This feature is available only for Premium subscribers.

Spotify has a median playback latency of 265 ms (including local [[cache (computing)|cache]]).<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Kreitz |first1=Gunnar |last2=Niemelä |first2=Fredrik |title=Spotify&nbsp;– Large Scale, Low Latency, P2P Music-on-Demand Streaming |url=http://www.csc.kth.se/~gkreitz/spotify-p2p10/spotify-p2p10.pdf |access-date=11 April 2026 |publisher=[[Royal Institute of Technology]] |archive-date=4 March 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304012754/http://www.csc.kth.se/~gkreitz/spotify-p2p10/spotify-p2p10.pdf |url-status=dead}}</ref>

In April 2014, Spotify moved away from the [[peer-to-peer]] (P2P) system it had used to distribute music to users. Previously, a desktop user would listen to music from one of three sources: a cached file on the computer, one of Spotify's servers, or from other subscribers through the P2P system. P2P, a well-established Internet distribution system, served as an alternative that reduced Spotify's server resources and costs. However, Spotify ended the P2P setup in 2014, with Spotify's Alison Bonny telling [[TorrentFreak]]: "We're gradually phasing out the use of our desktop P2P technology which has helped our users enjoy their music both speedily and seamlessly. We're now at a stage where we can power music delivery through our growing number of servers and ensure our users continue to receive a best-in-class service."<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ernesto |date=16 April 2014 |title=Spotify Starts Shutting Down Its Massive P2P Network |url=https://torrentfreak.com/spotify-starts-shutting-down-its-massive-p2p-network-140416/ |access-date=21 November 2016 |website=[[TorrentFreak]] |archive-date=23 October 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161023211534/https://torrentfreak.com/spotify-starts-shutting-down-its-massive-p2p-network-140416/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

Originally, Spotify had its own servers; however, in 2016, most of its infrastructure was migrated to [[Google Cloud Platform|Google Cloud]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Toor |first=Amar |date=24 February 2016 |title=Spotify is moving its data to Google's servers |url=https://www.theverge.com/2016/2/24/11104446/spotify-google-cloud-platform |access-date=6 July 2022 |website=The Verge |language=en |archive-date=6 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220706195928/https://www.theverge.com/2016/2/24/11104446/spotify-google-cloud-platform |url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Social media ===

Artists through Spotify can sell tickets to their music venue,<ref name="auto3"/> promote their merchandise,<ref name="auto3"/> add their videos as [[Story (social media)|stories]] through Spotify clips,<ref name="Perez"/> and promote new releases of their work through countdown pages.<ref name="Perez"/> Spotify also has a 'blend' feature in which two users' music preferences are mixed in a shared playlist.<ref name="Admin"/>  Spotify users can also 'jam' sessions where users can create and contribute live music playing sessions with their Spotify friends.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rawat |first=Ritesh |date=2 October 2023 |title=What Is Jam in Spotify and How to Use It |url=https://techwiser.com/what-is-jam-in-spotify-and-how-to-use-it/ |access-date=9 October 2025 |website=TechWiser |language=en-US}}</ref> Spotify Wrapped had generated two billion impressions worldwide, and more ninety million Spotify users share their Spotify Wrapped statistics online, especially through social media.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Strategism: Unwrapping Spotify Wrapped |url=https://www.opensourceceo.com/p/strategism-spotify-wrapped |access-date=2 November 2025 |website=Open Source CEO by Bill Kerr |language=en}}</ref>

Artists through social media platforms, especially [[TikTok]], often got their popularity boost through Spotify.<ref name="soundon.global">{{Cite web |title=How Do Spotify Cover Playlists Influence TikTok Song Trends? |url=https://www.soundon.global/forum/spotify-cover-tiktok-trends?lang=en |access-date=31 October 2025 |website=Soundwave}}</ref> For example, [[Lil Nas X]]'s "[[Old Town Road]]" and [[Doja Cat]]'s "[[Say So]]" started their popularity through TikTok but through Spotify playlists had their popularity  accelerated.<ref name="soundon.global"/> This furthering of Doja Cat's popularity through Spotify helped with Doja Cat's "[[Paint the Town Red (Doja Cat song)|Paint the Town Red]]" on 21 August 2023 becoming the #1 song on Spotify. This, in turn, made it the first time that a female solo rap artist achieved such a top spot in Spotify.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Galindo |first=Thomas |title=Doja Cat's "Paint The Town Red" Makes Spotify History |url=https://thegradecountry.com/2023/08/21/doja-cats-paint-the-town-red-makes-spotify-history/ |access-date=2 November 2025 |website=100.9 The Grade {{!}} Classic Country Hits |language=en}}</ref>

== Reception ==
{{Main|Criticism of Spotify}}

Spotify has attracted significant criticism since its 2008 launch.<ref name="Launch">{{Cite web |date=7 October 2008 |title=We've only just begun! |url=https://news.spotify.com/us/2008/10/07/weve-only-just-begun/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171211213512/https://news.spotify.com/us/2008/10/07/weve-only-just-begun/ |archive-date=11 December 2017 |access-date=7 November 2016 |website=Spotify}}</ref> The primary point of criticism centers around what artists, music creators, and the media have described as "unsustainable" compensation.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Plaugic |first=Lizzie |date=7 December 2015 |title=Spotify's Year in Music shows just how little we pay artists for their music |url=https://www.theverge.com/2015/12/7/9861372/spotify-year-in-review-artist-payment-royalties |access-date=22 January 2017 |website=[[The Verge]]}}</ref> Unlike physical sales or legal downloads (both of which were the main medium of listening to music at the time), which pay artists a fixed amount per song or album sold, Spotify pays royalties based on its "market share": the number of streams for their songs as a proportion of total songs streamed on the service. Spotify distributes approximately 70% of its total revenue to rights-holders, who will then pay artists based on their individual agreements.<ref name="Spotify business" /> Worldwide, 30,000 musicians have joined the organization UnionOfMusicians (UMAW).<ref name="auto2">{{Cite web |title=Has Streaming Saved the Music Industry?&nbsp;– Tracks&nbsp;– Watch the full show |url=https://www.arte.tv/en/videos/100280-007-A/has-streaming-saved-the-music-industry/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211129150425/https://www.arte.tv/en/videos/100280-007-A/has-streaming-saved-the-music-industry/ |archive-date=29 November 2021 |access-date=28 October 2021 |website=ARTE}}</ref> UMAW organized protests in 31 cities in March 2021 and its campaign #JusticeAtSpotify is demanding more transparency and a compensation of one cent per stream.<ref name="auto2" />

Spotify has been criticised by artists and producers including [[Taylor Swift]] and [[Thom Yorke]], who have argued that Spotify does not fairly compensate musicians, and both withdrew their music from the service.<ref>{{Cite web |date=15 July 2013 |title=Thom Yorke pulls albums from Spotify |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-23313445 |access-date=18 January 2017 |website=BBC === Fake artists ===
{{Main|Controversy over fake artists on Spotify}}

In 2021, a conspiracy theory began to take hold that Spotify was filling its playlists in some genres (including jazz, chill and "peaceful piano") with stock music attributed to a handful of little-known musicians, mostly Swedish, in an effort to meet demand for background music. In 2022, an investigation by the Swedish daily ''[[Dagens Nyheter]]'' found that around twenty songwriters were behind the work of more than five hundred "artists", and that thousands of their tracks were on Spotify and had been streamed millions of times, benefitting those artists versus other artists who might otherwise have supplied the background or ambient music.  In the 2025 book ''[[Mood Machine]]'', journalist Liz Pelly speculated that Spotify allowed these artists to put their music on Spotify. In doing so, if users chose to listen to one set of these artists' music, it effectively reduced the number of plays for other musicians. Pelly advocated for a move away from the current royalty model that says the artists who people listen to the most should make the most money toward a more generalized approach where the decision for who gets paid rests with industry or government bodies, not audiences.<ref>{{Cite web |title=The Ugly Truth About Spotify Is Finally Revealed |last=Gioia |first=Ted |work=The Honest Broker |access-date=25 December 2024 |url= https://www.honest-broker.com/p/the-ugly-truth-about-spotify-is-finally}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Pelly |first=Liz |title=The Ghosts in the Machine |magazine=Harper's Magazine |date=19 December 2024 |access-date=25 December 2024 |url= https://harpers.org/archive/2025/01/the-ghosts-in-the-machine-liz-pelly-spotify-musicians/}}</ref>

=== Privacy concerns ===
Spotify does not sell user data but did begin selling broader trend data, often coined "streaming intelligence" to marketing firms in 2016, allowing for the data to be available directly to clients.<ref name=":3">{{Cite book |last=Pelly |first=Liz |title=Mood Machine: The Rise of Spotify and the Costs of the Perfect Playlist |publisher=One Signal Publishers/Atria Books |year=2025 |isbn=978-1-6680-8350-5 |location=New York, NY |page= |language=English}}</ref> According to journalist and author Liz Pelly, Spotify, "under the guise of AI-powered recommendations has developed a surveillance apparatus driven by emotion profiling and pseudoscience."<ref name=":3" />

In July 2025, a leak containing the listening data of several politicians, journalists, and businesspeople, titled the Panama Playlists, was released online.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Lopatto |first=Elizabeth |date=30 July 2025 |title=Spotify's terrible privacy settings just leaked Palmer Luckey's bops and bangers |url=https://www.theverge.com/privacy/716386/spotifys-terrible-privacy-settings-just-leaked-palmer-luckeys-bops-and-bangers |access-date=31 July 2025 |website=The Verge |language=en-US}}</ref><ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Rogers |first=Reece |title=What JD Vance, Pam Bondi, and Sam Altman Can't Stop Listening to, According to the 'Panama Playlists' |url=https://www.wired.com/story/i-listened-to-the-alleged-spotify-playlists-of-trump-officials-for-five-hours-heres-what-i-learned/ |access-date=31 July 2025 |magazine=Wired |language=en-US |issn=1059-1028}}</ref>

In [[typography]], an '''asterism''', {{char|⁂}}, is a [[typographic symbol]] consisting of three [[asterisk]]s placed in a [[triangle]], which is used for a variety of purposes. The name originates from the [[astronomy|astronomical]] term for a [[Asterism (astronomy)|group of stars]].<ref name="Humez">From the Greek ''astēr'' (star) Alexander Humez, Nicholas D. Humez (2008). ''On the Dot: The Speck That Changed the World'', p. 72 & 186n. {{ISBN|978-0-19-532499-0}}.</ref>

The asterism was originally used as a type of [[dinkus]] in typography, though increasingly rarely.<ref name="D13" /> It can also be used to mean "untitled" or author or title withheld{{snd}} as seen, for example, in some editions of ''[[Album for the Young]]'' by composer [[Robert Schumann]] ([[№]] 21, 26, and 30).<ref>Taruskin, Richard (2005). ''The Oxford history of western music, Volume 3'', p. 311. {{ISBN|978-0-19-516979-9}}.</ref> 

In [[meteorology]], an asterism in a [[station model]] plot indicates moderate snowfall.<ref>{{Cite book|title=Essentials of meteorology: an invitation to the atmosphere|last=Ahrens|first=C. Donald|date=2011|publisher=Brooks/Cole|isbn=9780840049339|edition=6th|location=Belmont, CA|pages=461|oclc = 651905769}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.wpc.ncep.noaa.gov/html/stationplot.shtml|title=Station Model Information for Weather Observations|website=National Weather Service Weather Prediction Center|access-date=2019-07-16}}</ref>{{Clarify|date=May 2026 |reason=Is this actually the same symbol as the standard typographical symbol, or just similar? ==Dinkus==
{{main article|Dinkus}}
[[File:James Joyce, Ulysses, 1ed 2pr, p240.jpg|thumb|alt=Asterisms in use|Asterisms used as dinkuses in the [[James Joyce]] novel  ''[[Ulysses (novel)|Ulysses]]'', the "Wandering Rocks" chapter, from the 1922 edition.<ref>{{cite book |title=Ulysses |first=James |last=Joyce | page=240 |url=https://archive.org/details/ulysses00joyc_1/page/240/mode/1up |via=[[Archive.org]]  |date= 1922 |publisher = Egoist Press, John Rodker | location=London, Paris}}</ref> The 1961 edition used a hollow [[Star (grapheme)#Five points|white star]] (☆), and the 1984 edition used a row of three asterisks.]]

A dinkus is a typographical device to divide text, such as at [[Section (typography)#Flourished section breaks|section breaks]]. Its purpose is to "indicate minor breaks in text",<ref>{{cite book| author= Hudson, Robert | date=2010 | title =The Christian Writer's Manual of Style| page= 396 | isbn=978-0-310-86136-2}}</ref> to call attention to a passage, or to separate sub-chapters in a book. An asterism used this way is thus a type of dinkus: nowadays this usage of the symbol is nearly obsolete.<ref name="D13">Radim Peško, Louis Lüthi (2007). ''Dot Dot Dot 13'', p. 193. Stuart Bailey, Peter Bilak, eds. {{ISBN|978-90-77620-07-6}}.</ref> More commonly used dinkuses are three [[interpunct|dots]] or three asterisks in a horizontal row.<ref>{{cite book| author= Lundmark, Torbjorn |title = Quirky Qwerty: the story of the keyboard @ your fingertips |page = 120 | date =2002 | publisher = University of New South Wales |isbn= 9780868404363}}</ref><ref name=Crystal>{{cite book|title=Making a Point: The Pernickety Story of English Punctuation | author = David Crystal |isbn=9781781253519 |publisher= London Profile Books | date=2016}}</ref>

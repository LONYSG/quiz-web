A '''dead heat''' is a rare situation in various [[racing]] sports in which the performances of competitors are judged to be so close that no difference between them can be resolved. The result is declared a [[Tie (draw)|tie]] and the competitors are awarded a joint ranking. Dead heats can occur in both head-to-head races and competitions where competitors race sequentially and are ranked by finishing time.

[[Photo finish]]es have been a long-standing method of resolving outcomes too ambiguous to be distinguished by the naked eye. Improvements in technology, including digital super-slow motion replay and pressure-sensitive digital timers, have increased precision in resolving dead heats.  Consequently, dead heats are declared less often than they once were.

==Etymology==
[[Image:GGF Race7d.jpg|thumb|A dead heat in [[horse racing]]]]
The [[Oxford English Dictionary]] attributes the term to [[horse racing]]. Meets formerly had the same horses run several "heats" in a day, with victors being decided by the total number of wins. A heat which had no clear single winner was discounted from these tallies and was therefore "dead".<ref name="tlohr" />

==Occurrence==
[[File:Triple dead-heat.jpg|thumb|An extremely rare [[photo finish]] triple dead heat, recorded in a 1953 [[harness racing|harness race]] at New Jersey's [[Freehold Raceway]]]]
Dead heats are very rare, and situations with three (or more) competitors in a dead heat are exceptionally so. The frequency of dead heats varies between sports, depending on the typical variance in performances and the precision of the technology available. The use of the [[photo finish]], first introduced in horse racing in second quarter of the 20th century, notably decreased the number of dead heats.<ref name=tlohr>{{cite book|last1=Hammond|first1=Gerald|title=The Language of Horse Racing|date=2016|publisher=Routledge|isbn=9781135965020|pages=62–63|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=kbEFDAAAQBAJ&pg=PA62|language=en}}</ref>  Both of the two recorded quadruple dead heats in horse racing occurred in England in the 1850s.<ref>{{cite news |author=Mazeppa |url=https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/OW18940125.2.150.6 |title=Talk of the Day |newspaper=Otago Witness |issue=2083 |date=25 January 1894 |quote=At the Hoo races, held in Gorhambury Park, Hertfordshire, on April 26, 1851, four horses ran a dead heat for a race entitled the Omnibus or Open Hunters' Stakes—''viz.'', Defaulter, The Squire of Malton, Reindeer, and Pulcherrima. In the deciding heat Reindeer and Pulcherrima were each backed at 6 to 4, but Defaulter won by half a length. Again in a sweepstakes at the Newmarket - Houghton meeting (October 22, 1855) five horses ran, and four of them—''viz.'', Overreach, The Unexpected, Gamester, and Lady Golightly—could not be separated by the judge, the other horse, King of the Gipsies, being only half a length behind the quartet.}}</ref>

[[Swimming (sport)|Swimming]] has a relatively high number of dead heats because, under [[FINA]] rules (which includes [[Swimming at the Summer Olympics|Olympic events]]), positions are based on race timings which are limited in precision to hundredths of a second; this is despite the availability of technology that could provide further precision.<ref name="deadspin">{{cite web|last1=Burke|first1=Timothy|title=This Is Why There Are So Many Ties In Swimming|url=https://deadspin.com/this-is-why-there-are-so-many-ties-in-swimming-1785234795|website=Deadspin|access-date=24 February 2018}}</ref><ref name="FINA_timing">{{cite web|title=SW 11 TIMING |url=http://www.fina.org/content/sw-11-timing|publisher=Fédération internationale de natation|access-date=24 February 2018|language=en}}</ref>
The reason for this is that the length of lanes can vary by up to {{convert|3|cm}}, with lower-precision timing compensating for the possible differences between the distances competitors have travelled.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Ward-Henninger|first1=Colin|title=Rio Olympics: Here's the reason why there are so many ties in swimming|url=https://www.cbssports.com/olympics/news/rio-olympics-heres-the-reason-why-there-are-so-many-ties-in-swimming/|access-date=20 February 2018|work=CBS Sports|date=13 August 2016|language=en}}</ref>

Occasionally racers will try to deliberately engineer a dead heat. During the mid-1940s, twin distance runners [[H. Ross Hume]] and [[Robert H. Hume]] became known as the "dead heat twins" for their practice of finishing their races hand-in-hand in intentional efforts to share victory.<ref>{{cite news|first=Jimmy|last=Jordan|title=Illinois Beats Michigan for Big Ten Track Crown: Three Illini Stars Win Six Events Among Them to Aid Most Stunning Upset in Years|newspaper=Council Bluffs Nonpareil|agency=Associated Press|date=27 May 1945}}</ref> At the [[2002 United States Grand Prix]] in Indianapolis, [[Scuderia Ferrari|Ferrari]] teammates [[Rubens Barrichello]] and [[Michael Schumacher]] attempted to tie for first place, but Barrichello was adjudicated to have won by 0.011 seconds.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Clarke|first1=Liz|title=Barrichello Wins Grand Prix With Schumacher's Assist|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/2002/09/30/barrichello-wins-grand-prix-with-schumachers-assist/59af8338-5208-4685-9d3b-890741417355/|access-date=21 February 2018|work=Washington Post|date=30 September 2002}}</ref>

In Formula One qualifying, dead heats have occurred only twice since times were recorded to the thousands of a seconds. At the [[1997 European Grand Prix]] in Jerez, [[Michael Schumacher]], [[Jacques Villeneuve]], and [[Heinz-Harald Frentzen]] all posted the same qualifying time. The positions were then determined based on who posted the time first, which resulted in Villeneuve acquiring pole position.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.statsf1.com/en/1997/europe/qualification.aspx|title=Europe 1997 – Qualifications |website=STATS F1|language=en|access-date=15 October 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181015192526/http://www.statsf1.com/en/1997/europe/qualification.aspx|archive-date=15 October 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> 27 years later, the [[2024 Canadian Grand Prix]] saw [[George Russell (racing driver)|George Russell]] and [[Max Verstappen]] both posting the same time, but Russell, having set the time first, received the pole position.<ref name="Starting grid">{{cite web|url=https://www.formula1.com/en/results.html/2024/races/1237/canada/starting-grid.html|title=Formula 1 AWS Grand Prix du Canada 2024 – Starting Grid|website=Formula 1|date=8 June 2024|access-date=8 June 2024}}</ref>

==Outcome==
If a dead heat is declared, all tied competitors are considered to have jointly achieved the superior position (unless a tie-breaking method is used to separate them).<ref>See Mazeppa, ''op. cit.''</ref> This does not affect awards for subsequent finishers. For example, in the final of the [[Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's 100 metre freestyle|Women's 100 metre freestyle]] at the [[2016 Summer Olympics]], [[Penny Oleksiak]] and [[Simone Manuel]] finished in a dead heat for first place. Both were awarded gold medals, no silver medal was awarded, and the next finisher, [[Sarah Sjöström]], received bronze.<ref name="Swimming example">{{cite web|title=Double gold medal joy as Oleksiak and Manuel finish in dead heat|url=https://www.olympic.org/news/double-gold-medal-joy-as-oleksiak-and-manuel-finish-in-dead-heat|website=International Olympic Committee|access-date=20 February 2018|language=en|date=11 August 2016}}</ref>

Prizes for the tied competitors may be divided. The rules of [[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile|FIA]]-organised championships, for example, specify that a dead heat in a championship would result in all the [[List of Formula One World Championship points scoring systems|World Championship points]] for the tied positions being added together and divided equally between the tied competitors.<ref>e.g. from the regulations of [[Formula 3]]: {{Cite web |last=Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile |author-link=Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile |title=2024 FIA FORMULA 3 CHAMPIONSHIP SPORTING REGULATIONS |url=https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2024_fia_f3_sporting_regulations_v3_11-06-2024.pdf |access-date=2024-09-02 |quote=Points awarded for all the positions of drivers who tie will be added together and shared equally}}</ref> For example, at the [[2024 Monza Formula 2 round]] where [[Dennis Hauger]] and [[Gabriel Bortoleto]] both finished in 8th place in the sprint race, scoring 0.5 points each.<ref name="FIA">{{cite book|title=FIA F2 Race 1 (sprint) final classification|date=31 August 2024|publisher=FIA|page=4|url=https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/decision-document/2024%20Monza%20Event%20-%20F2%20Race%201%20(Sprint)%20-%20final%20classification.pdf|access-date=31 August 2024}}</ref> Complications can occur if the reward cannot be divided or duplicated: at the Women's 100 meters at the [[2012 United States Olympic Trials (track and field)|2012 United States Olympic Track Trials]], [[Jeneba Tarmoh]] and [[Allyson Felix]] finished in a dead heat for the third and final place in the [[United States at the 2012 Summer Olympics|US Olympic team]], with there being no provision in the rules to resolve the situation (a head-to-head run-off was proposed, but Tarmoh eventually conceded the place).<ref>{{cite news|agency=Associated Press|title=Jeneba Tarmoh concedes In [[Grand Prix motorcycle racing]], joint rankings are resolved by using fastest lap times as a tiebreaker.<ref name="FIM_regs">{{cite book|title=FIM WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP GRAND PRIX REGULATIONS|date=17 February 2018|publisher=[[Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme]]|page=61|url=http://www.fim-live.com/en/sport/regulations-and-documents/grand-prix/|access-date=22 February 2018|quote=In case of ties, the riders concerned will be ranked in the order of the best lap time made during the race.}}</ref> This rule resulted in [[Héctor Faubel]] winning the 125cc classification of the [[2011 German motorcycle Grand Prix]] after a photo finish could not separate him and [[Johann Zarco]].<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.motogp.com/en/news/2011/125+report+race+sachsenring|title=Faubel victorious after photo finish|work=MotoGP.com|publisher=[[Dorna Sports]]|date=17 July 2011|access-date=22 February 2018|archive-date=3 November 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111103075743/http://www.motogp.com/en/news/2011/125+report+race+sachsenring|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Special provision is made for dead heats in the rules of [[sports betting]]: punters' stakes are divided proportionally by the number of tied competitors.<ref name="betfairhelp">{{cite web|title=Exchange: What happens if there is a dead heat?|url=https://en-betfair.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/403/~/exchange%3A-what-happens-if-there-is-a-dead-heat%3F|website=en-betfair.custhelp.com|access-date=20 February 2018}}{{Dead link|date=November 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}</ref>

{{short description|Tennis term}}
In [[tennis]], a '''bagel''' is when the set ends with a score of 6–0.<ref>{{Cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2013/09/05/sports/tennis/serena-williams-perfects-the-double-bagel.html |title=A true champion, Williams perfects the double bagel |date=Sep 4, 2013 |work=New York Times}}</ref> An extremely rare type of bagel, where no point is lost, is called a [[golden set]]. Most bagel sets occur in the early rounds of tennis tournaments where the favorites play lower-ranked players, such as [[lucky loser|lucky losers]] or [[Wild card (sports)|wild cards]].

== Etymology ==
The term refers to the similarity between the shape of a zero and the shape of a [[bagel]]. The tennis term was coined by players [[Harold Solomon]] and [[Eddie Dibbs]], and popularized by commentator [[Bud Collins]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Bodo |first1=Peter |title=Bagel, Anyone? |url=https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/bagel-anyone |website=Tennis.com |access-date=4 September 2022}}</ref>

== Surface disparity ==
Statistics of the men's singles [[Grand Slam (tennis)|Grand Slam]] tournaments from 2000 to 2016 are as follows: at [[Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]] (grass), 127 bagels were made; at [[French Open]] (clay), 267; at the [[US Open Tennis Championship]] (hardcourt), 275, and at the [[Australian Open]] (hardcourt), 238.{{citation needed|date=July 2017}}. [[Björn Borg]] (five-time Wimbledon champion and six-time French Open champion) recorded twenty 6–0 sets at the French Open, and only five at Wimbledon.

== Double bagel==

=== Women's singles ===
For women in Grand Slam tournaments, a double bagel result is possible as the matches are best of three sets. In the Open Era, there has been a women's singles Grand Slam tournament match with a double bagel every year except for in 1968 and 2005.{{citation needed|date=July 2017}} The most double bagels were in the seasons of 1974 and 1993, when eight matches had a result of 6–0, 6–0.{{citation needed|date=July 2017}}

==Trivia==
* In 1910 in [[Brussels]], [[Max Decugis]] beat [[Tony Wilding]] 3–6, 0–6, 7–5, 6–0, 6–0. Wilding was leading 6–3, 6–0, 5–0, until Decugis won the last 19 consecutive games.
* In 1948, [[Don Budge]] won the first round match against George Hudson 6–0, 6–0, 6–0 at the [[U.S. Pro Tennis Championships draws, 1946–1967|US Professional Championship]], and then against Jerome Adler 6–0, 6–0, 6–1 in the second round.
* In 1969, in the [[1969 Wimbledon Championships – Men's singles|second round of Wimbledon]], top seed [[Rod Laver]] began with the loss of the first two sets to the unseeded Indian [[Premjit Lall]]. However, the match ended in his favor with a score of 3–6, 4–6, 6–3, 6–0, 6–0.

** 1938 [[1938 U.S. National Championships – Men's singles|U.S. National Championships]]: [[Gilbert Hunt]] – [[Bobby Riggs]] 6–2, 0–6, 9–7, 0–6, 6–4<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1938/09/13/archives/miss-jacobs-and-riggs-beaten-in-national-tennis-upsets-gilbert-hunt.html|title=Miss Jacobs and Riggs Beaten in National Tennis Upsets|website=New York Times|date=1938-09-13}}</ref>
** 1960 [[Italian Open (tennis)|Italian Open]]: [[Barry MacKay (tennis)|Barry MacKay]] - [[Luis Ayala (tennis)|Luis Ayala]] 7–5, 7–5, 0–6, 0–6, 6–1

** 1981 [[1981 US Open – Men's singles|US Open]]: [[José Luis Clerc]] – [[Mel Purcell]] 6–3, 0–6, 0–6, 6–4, 6–3

* [[Suzanne Lenglen]] won nine tournaments in which she did not lose a single game in all matches.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Little|first1=Alan|title=Suzanne Lenglen : Tennis Idol of the Twenties|date=2007|publisher=Wimbledon Lawn Tennis Museum|location=London|isbn=9780906741436|pages=135–196|edition=2nd rev.}}</ref>

* [[Björn Borg]] won 116 bagel sets during his career.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://tennisplanet.wordpress.com/2007/02/27/most-bagels-or-6-0-sets |title=Most bagels or 6–0 sets. |website=Tennisplanet.wordpress.com |date=2007-02-27 |accessdate=2017-06-23}}</ref>
* Three women's finals in the history of the Grand Slam tournaments have ended with a double-bagel:<ref>{{cite news|last= Herman |first= Robin |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1988/06/05/sports/tennis-graf-shuts-out-zvereva-to-gain-french-open-title.html |title= TENNIS – Graf Shuts Out Zvereva to Gain French Open Title |website= [[The New York Times]] |date=1988-06-05 |accessdate=2017-06-23}}</ref>

* Swiss tennis player [[Martina Hingis]] achieved her first Olympic bagel in [[Tennis at the 1996 Summer Olympics – Women's singles|singles]] in 1996, and the second [[Tennis at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's doubles|in doubles]] twenty years later.
* Spanish tennis player [[Conchita Martínez]] scored the first double bagel against a player inside the top ten in Hamburg in 1995 when she defeated [[Magdalena Maleeva]].{{citation needed|date=January 2026}}
* Polish tennis player Iga Świątek scored the first double bagel in a [[WTA 1000 tournaments|WTA 1000]] final, defeating top ten Czech player and former world No. 1, [[Karolína Plíšková]], in [[2021 Italian Open – Women's singles|Rome]].

'''Confit''' ({{IPAc-en|k|ɒ|n|f|i}}, {{IPA|fr|kɔ̃fi}}; {{langx|oc|confisat}}) (from [[French language|French]] 'to preserve')<ref name="IP">{{cite book | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=3RsgAQAAQBAJ | title=Irish Pantry: Traditional Breads, Preserves, and Goodies to Feed the Ones You Love | publisher=Running Press of the Perseus Books Group | author=McMeel, Noel | year=2013 | location=Orange-Onion Confit | pages=44 | isbn=978-0762445523}}</ref><ref name="Chaucer">{{cite book | url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Bts-AAAAYAAJ | title=Chaucer: The tale of the Man of Lawe, The Pardoneres Tale, The Second Nones Tale, The Chanouns Yemannes Tale | publisher=Macmiillan and Co. | author=Skeat, Walter William | author-link=Walter William Skeat | year=2015 | pages=222 | isbn=1330317475}}</ref> is a food that is cooked in fat{{Citation needed|date=July 2021|reason=Clearly false}} as a method of [[food preservation|preservation]].<ref name="IP" />

A confit is cooked in fat at a low temperature, typically {{convert|200|F|C|-1|order=flip}} or less.

For meat, this method requires the meat to be salted. After salting and cooking in fat, confit can last for several months or years when sealed and stored in a cool, dark place. Confit is a specialty of southwestern [[France]].{{fact|date=November 2023}}

==Etymology==
The word comes from French ''confire'' 'to preserve', which in turn comes from [[Latin]] ''conficere '''to do, to produce, to make, to prepare'.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.cnrtl.fr/etymologie/confit|title=CONFIT : Etymologie de CONFIT|website=www.cnrtl.fr|language=fr|access-date=2017-11-20}}</ref> The French verb was first applied in medieval times to fruits cooked and preserved in sugar.{{fact|date=November 2023}}

==Fruit confit==
Fruit confit is [[candied fruit]] (whole fruit, or pieces thereof) preserved in [[sugar]]. The fruit must be fully infused{{Citation needed|date=July 2021|reason=I don't think so}} with sugar to its core; larger fruit takes considerably longer than smaller ones to candy. Thus, while small fruit such as [[cherry|cherries]] are ''confits'' whole, it is quite rare to see whole large fruit, such as [[melon]] ''confits'' since the time and energy involved in producing large ''fruit confits'' makes them quite expensive.{{fact|date=November 2023}}

==Meat confit==
[[File:Essen Ente 02 (RaBoe).jpg|thumb|Duck leg confit]]
[[Image:Fete Huma 2006 p1140018.jpg|thumb|Canned duck confit and [[cassoulet]]]]
Confit of [[Goose as food|goose]] (''confit d'oie'') or [[Duck as food|duck]] (''[[Duck confit|confit de canard]]'') is usually prepared from the legs of the bird. The meat is salted, seasoned with herbs, and slowly cooked submerged in its own rendered fat at {{convert|85|C|F|disp=}} or less. The meat is put in containers with its fat. Turkey and pork{{Citation needed|date=July 2021|reason=What pork?}} may be treated in the same manner. Meat confit is a specialty of the southwest of France ([[Toulouse]], [[Dordogne]], and other areas) and is used in dishes such as [[cassoulet]]. Confit preparations originated as a means of preserving meat before refrigeration and canning.{{fact|date=November 2023}}

In restaurants, confit is usually served after further preparation.{{Citation needed|date=July 2021|reason="usually" is insufficient}} Whole confit leg is baked to crisp the skin or added to a casserole-type dish. Confit duck leg is used to make [[rillettes]].{{fact|date=November 2023}}

=== History ===
The traditional meat for confit includes waterfowl such as goose and duck, and pork. Duck [[gizzard]]s are also prepared as confit.

Confit is common in regions of France where goose fat is used for cooking, rather than olive oil. Goose confit is associated with the [[Béarn]] and [[Basque Country (greater region)|Basque]] regions with their specialties of cassoulet and [[garbure]], made with confit and beans. [[Saintonge (region)|Saintonge]] and [[Brantôme, Dordogne|Brantôme]] feature duck confit, often with potatoes and [[Tuber (genus)|truffle]]s.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2020-03-11 |title=How to Easily Make Duck Confit at Home |author-first1=Sam|author-last1=Slaughter|url=https://www.themanual.com/food-and-drink/how-to-make-duck-confit/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250320180001/https://www.themanual.com/food-and-drink/how-to-make-duck-confit/ |archive-date=2025-03-20 |access-date=2026-10-14 |work=The Manual |language=en-US}}</ref>

Non-waterfowl meats are also treated to the confit process. The French refer only to duck and goose confits as true confits; other meats poached in duck or goose fat are considered ''en confit'' ("in confit"). For example, chicken cooked in goose fat is called ''poulet en confit''.<ref>Plummer, Paul. ''Sensual Preservation: The Art of Confit''.</ref> Pork is often confit and shredded to create [[rillettes]].{{fact|date=November 2023}}

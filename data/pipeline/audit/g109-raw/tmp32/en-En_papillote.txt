'''''En papillote''''' ({{IPA|fr|ɑ̃ papijɔt}}; French for "enveloped in paper"<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.larousse.fr/dictionnaires/francais/papillote/57784|title = Définitions : Papillote - Dictionnaire de français Larousse}}</ref>), or ''al cartoccio'' in Italian, is a method of [[cooking]] in which the food is put into a folded pouch or parcel and then [[baking|baked]]. This method is most often used to cook [[fish]] or vegetables, but lamb and [[poultry]] can also be cooked ''en papillote''. It is a combination cooking method of [[baking]] and [[steaming]].<ref name="NYtimes" /> This method of cooking has been popular since the 17th century in France.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-03-13 |title=Recipe for classic Salmon en Papillote - The Good Life France |url=https://thegoodlifefrance.com/recipe-for-salmon-en-papillote/ |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=thegoodlifefrance.com |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Method ==
The parcel is typically made from folded [[Parchment paper (baking)|parchment paper]], but other material, such as a [[paper bag]] or [[aluminum foil]], may be used. The parcel holds in moisture to steam the food.<ref name="NYtimes">{{cite news|last=Hesser|first=Amanda|title=The Envelope Please: Cooking En Papillote|url=https://www.nytimes.com/1999/05/19/dining/the-envelope-please-cooking-en-papillote.html?pagewanted=all&src=pm|access-date=28 November 2012|newspaper=New York Times|date=May 19, 1999}}</ref> The pocket is created by overlapping circles of paper or foil and folding them tightly around the food to create a seal. 

The moisture may be from the food itself or from an added moisture source, such as [[water]], [[wine]], or [[stock (food)|stock]]. The choice of [[herb]]s, [[seasoning]]s and [[spice]]s depends on the particular [[recipe]] being prepared.

The parcel can be opened at the table to allow people to smell the aroma when it opens.

==Regional and local variations==

===Asia===

Indonesian cuisine has a variation on ''en papillote'' using leaves to steam food.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kumbara |first=Ade Gusti |date=2023-06-27 |title=Unveiling the Delights of Pepes Ikan: A Step-by-Step Guide to a Traditional Indonesian Dish |url=https://medium.com/@ashtonatticus2023/unveiling-the-delights-of-pepes-ikan-a-step-by-step-guide-to-a-traditional-indonesian-dish-da516663b27e |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=Medium |language=en}}</ref> With large leaves (e.g. [[banana]], ''[[Xanthosoma]]'', or [[cassava]])<ref>{{Cite web |author=Cammelyain |date=2017-10-15 |title=Cultivation of banana plants in Indonesia and its benefits for humans |url=https://steemit.com/science/@cammelya/cultivation-of-banana-plants-in-indonesia-and-its-benefits-for-humans-20171015t181429678z |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=Steemit |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Taylor |first=Michael |title=Indonesia's cassava push leaves bitter taste in Borneo rainforest |url=https://www.context.news/nature/indonesias-cassava-push-leaves-bitter-taste-in-borneo-rainforest |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=context.news |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=PROSEA - Plant Resources of e |url=https://prosea.prota4u.org/view.aspx?id=3245 |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=prosea.prota4u.org}}</ref> widely available across the archipelago, Indonesians have long used them in food preparation. The leaves are used to wrap food before cooking it either by steaming or grilling.<ref>{{Cite journal |last=Wijaya |first=Serli |date=2019-09-18 |title=Indonesian food culture mapping: a starter contribution to promote Indonesian culinary tourism |journal=Journal of Ethnic Foods |volume=6 |issue=1 |pages=9 |doi=10.1186/s42779-019-0009-3 |doi-access=free |issn=2352-619X}}</ref> The Indonesian method requires no additional moisture, and in some dishes, the leaf wrappings may also be eaten. Popular Indonesian dishes that employ this cooking method include ''[[pepes]]'', ''[[botok]]'', ''[[buntil]]'', and ''[[otak-otak]]'' (variations of this dish are also commonly available in neighboring Singapore and Malaysia).

A Japanese variant called ''foil yaku'' or ''hoiru yaku'' (ホイル焼き), which consists of using aluminium foil to both cook and serve. Commonly Mushrooms, onion and fish, seasoned with butter, miso and soy-sauce.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Miso butter Mushrooms in Foil |url=https://www.justonecookbook.com/miso-butter-mushrooms-in-foil/ |access-date=2026-11-07 |website=justonecookbook |language=en}}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Foil Yaki is the Best |url=https://tastecooking.com/foil-yaki-best/}}</ref>

Chinese [[beggar's chicken]]  is encased in mud or clay before being heated directly on a fire.  The legend surrounding its origination was to prevent any aroma from escaping while it was being cooked.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Beggar's Chicken |url=https://en.chinaculture.org/focus/focus/2010expo_en/2010-04/14/content_376283.htm |access-date=2024-05-15 |website=chinaculture.org}}</ref>

=== New Orleans ===
''Pompano en papillote'', [[pompano]] fish baked ''en papillote'' with vegetables and shrimp, crab or oyster meat, is a specialty of [[Louisiana Creole cuisine]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Bienvenu|first=Marcelle|title=Pompano is traditional en papillote, but other fish work as well|url=https://www.nola.com/entertainment_life/eat-drink/article_657ac52c-66b0-5f4d-8788-1e4bcc1162cc.html|access-date=2020-12-31|website=NOLA.com|date=23 August 2012 |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Chef Folse's Pompano en Papillote|url=https://www.wafb.com/story/29336418/chef-folses-pompano-en-papillote|access-date=2020-12-31|website=[[WAFB]]|date=17 June 2015 |language=en-US}}</ref> It was invented at [[Antoine's Restaurant]] in [[New Orleans]].<ref>{{Cite web|title=Antoine's Restaurant|url=http://www.atlasobscura.com/places/antoines-restaurant|access-date=2020-12-31|website=Atlas Obscura|language=en}}</ref>

=== Greece ===
The [[Greek cuisine|Greek]] dish [[Kleftiko]] is prepared by slow-braising meat and potatoes in a paper parcel.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Cloake |first=Felicity |date=2016-08-18 |title=How to cook the perfect kleftiko |url=https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/wordofmouth/2016/aug/18/how-to-cook-the-perfect-kleftiko |access-date=2026-09-14 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

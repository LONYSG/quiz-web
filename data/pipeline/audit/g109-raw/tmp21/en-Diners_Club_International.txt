'''Diners Club International Ltd.''' ('''DCI'''), founded as '''Diners Club''', is a [[payment network]] owned by [[Capital One]]. Diners Club and its franchises are accepted in over 200 countries and territories. Card issuers operating in approximately 45 countries have exclusive individual licenses to issue cards on the Diners Club network in a given country.

The company was formed in 1950 by Frank X. McNamara, Ralph Schneider (1909–1964),<ref>{{Cite news |date=1964-11-03 |title=Ralph E. Schneider Dead at 55; Diners' Club Founder and Head; Lawyer Pursued Credit Idea With Client — Business Made Him Millionaire |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1964/11/03/archives/ralph-e-schneider-dead-at-55-diners-club-founder-and-head-lawyer.html |access-date=2024-04-29 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> [[Matty Simmons]], and [[Alfred S. Bloomingdale]]. It was the first independent [[payment card]] company in the world, successfully establishing travel and entertainment (T&E) credit cards as a viable business.<ref>{{cite web |title=Diners Club Review |url=http://casinosbanking.com/diners-club-overview.htm |access-date=2011-05-26 |work=CasinosBanking |date=<!--undated--> |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.-->}}</ref> [[Citibank]] bought Diners Club in 1981, then sold it to [[Discover Financial]] in 2008. [[Capital One]] then acquired Discover Financial in 2025.

== History ==
[[File:Ralph Schneider, bust portrait, facing front (1958, by Herman Hiller, LCCN 00649704) (cropped).tif|thumb|Portrait of Diners Club co-founder Ralph Schneider, pictured in 1958]]
The idea for Diners Club was conceived at the Majors Cabin Grill restaurant in New York City in 1949.<ref name="Klaffke2003">{{cite book |first=Pamela |last=Klaffke |title=Spree: A Cultural History of Shopping |url=https://archive.org/details/spree00pame |url-access=registration |access-date=8 February 2013 |date=1 October 2003 |publisher=Arsenal Pulp Press |isbn=978-1-55152-143-5 |pages=[https://archive.org/details/spree00pame/page/22 22]–24}}</ref> Diners Club cofounder Frank McNamara was dining with clients and realized he had left his wallet in another suit.<ref name="one">{{cite book |title=How to Survive & Thrive in the Merchant Services Industry |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=WoF42gu04dIC&pg=PT36 |access-date=7 January 2013 |date=June 2003 |publisher=Survive & Thrive |isbn=978-0-9741884-0-9 |pages=37–38 |first=Marc J. |last=Beauchamp}}</ref> His wife paid the bill, and McNamara thought of a multipurpose charge card as a way to avoid similar embarrassments in the future.<ref name="EvansSchmalensee2005"/> He discussed the idea with the restaurant owner at the table,<ref name="Levine2008"/> and the following day with his lawyer Ralph Schneider and friend [[Alfred S. Bloomingdale]].<ref name="Levine2008">{{cite book |first=Jonathan |last=Levine |title=Credit where it is Due: A Social History of Consumer Credit in America |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=MBdHMJkppLoC&pg=PA184 |access-date=8 February 2013 |year=2008 |isbn=978-0-549-81953-0 |pages=184–205 }}{{Dead link|date=April 2023 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> McNamara returned to the same restaurant the following February, in 1950, and paid for his meal using a cardboard charge card and a signature.<ref name="deserve">{{cite news |date=November 19, 2006 |first=David |last=Bear |title=Who Deserves the Credit for Credit Cards? |url=http://www.post-gazette.com/stories/sectionfront/life/who-deserves-the-credit-for-credit-cards-460111/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160107020250/http://www.post-gazette.com/travel/2006/11/19/Who-deserves-the-credit-for-credit-cards/stories/200611190203 |url-status=dead |archive-date=January 7, 2016 |access-date=February 6, 2013 |newspaper=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette}}</ref><ref name="Liberman2011">{{cite book |first=Sherri |last=Liberman |title=American Food by the Decades |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=sLSDQRV3XUMC&pg=PA131 |access-date=7 January 2013 |date=31 August 2011 |publisher=ABC-CLIO |isbn=978-0-313-37698-6 |pages=131–132}}</ref> The story became well known.<ref name="EvansSchmalensee2005" /><ref name="Schnaars2002" /> Diners Club official history refers to this meal as "The First Supper", and is credited by historians as the beginnings of contemporary credit.<ref name="Hyman2011">{{cite book |first=Louis |last=Hyman |title=Debtor Nation: The History of America in Red Ink |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=M5xk1rKh8ncC&pg=PA98 |access-date=8 February 2013 |date=January 12, 2011 |publisher=Princeton University Press |isbn=978-0-691-14068-1 |pages=98}}</ref> Various versions of the story differ about whether it was a lunch or dinner at which McNamara forgot his wallet,<ref name="EvansSchmalensee2005" /> and whether the bill was paid on loan or McNamara waited for his wife to drive his wallet to him.<ref name="stl">{{cite news |newspaper=Chicago Sun-Times |date=March 13, 2000 |first=Neil |last=Steinberg |page=6 |title=The Card that Started it All}}</ref><ref name="Sobey1999">{{cite book |first=Edwin J. C. |last=Sobey |title=Young Inventors at Work!: Learning Science by Doing Science |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=B8D-_PqexDIC&pg=PA100 |access-date=8 February 2013 |date=1 March 1999 |publisher=Good Year Books |isbn=978-0-673-57735-1 |pages=100}}</ref> According to Diners Club press agent Matty Simmons, that never happened and the story was invented.<ref>The Credit Card Catastrophe by Matty Simmons first printing, page 26</ref> Some journalists later credited Alfred Bloomingdale with the idea for Diners Club.<ref name="EvansSchmalensee2005" />

McNamara and his attorney, Ralph Schneider, founded Diners Club International on February{{nbsp}}8, 1950,<ref name="RobertsBerg2012">{{cite book |first1=Bryan |last1=Roberts |first2=Natalie |last2=Berg |name-list-style=amp|title=Walmart: Key Insights and Practical Lessons from the World's Largest Retailer |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=TLC2LiaU6x8C&pg=PA8 |access-date=7 January 2013 |date=3 April 2012 |publisher=Kogan Page Publishers |isbn=978-0-7494-6274-1 |pages=8}}</ref> with $1.5&nbsp;million in initial capital.<ref name="EvansSchmalensee2005">{{cite book |first1=David Sparks |last1=Evans |first2=Richard |last2=Schmalensee |name-list-style=amp|title=Paying With Plastic: The Digital Revolution In Buying And Borrowing |url=https://archive.org/details/payingwithplasti0000evan |url-access=registration |access-date=January 7, 2013 |year=2005 |publisher=MIT Press |isbn=978-0-262-55058-1 |pages=[https://archive.org/details/payingwithplasti0000evan/page/54 54]}}</ref> Alfred Bloomingdale joined briefly, then started a competing venture in California before merging his California-based Dine and Sign with Diners Club.<ref name="Schnaars2002">{{cite book |first=Steven P. |last=Schnaars |title=Managing Imitation Strategies |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=7q9oGyKzS2QC&pg=PA81 |access-date=January 7, 2013 |date=April 29, 2002 |publisher=Simon and Schuster |isbn=978-0-7432-4265-3 |pages=81}}</ref> McNamara's original conception was to make a card that could be used as a means of payment in restaurants around New York City; however, he later expanded its usage to other types of establishments, including hotels, car rentals, and flower shops. The company started building its customer base by offering their cards to prominent businessmen. Shortly afterward, [[Matty Simmons]], the company's first press agent, started advertising the card in newspapers, magazines, and by sending personal mail to potential customers.<ref>{{cite book|author=Simmons, Matty|title=''The Credit Card Catastrophe: The 20th Century Phenomenon that Changed the World''|publisher=Barricade Books|year=1995|pages=26, 29, 106}}</ref> Diners Club International was named for being a "club of diners" that would allow patrons to settle their bill at the end of each month through their credit account.<ref name="Clair2011">{{cite book |first=Michael |last=St. Clair |title=So Much, So Fast, So Little Time: Coming to Terms with Rapid Change and Its Consequences |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Xm_o7oDhzAIC&pg=PA88 |access-date=January 7, 2013 |date=August 31, 2011 |publisher=ABC-CLIO |isbn=978-0-313-39276-4 |pages=88}}</ref> When the card was first introduced, Diners Club listed 27{{nbsp}}participating restaurants, and 200{{nbsp}}of the founders' friends and acquaintances used it.<ref name="Clair2011"/>

Diners Club had 20,000{{nbsp}}members by the end of 1950<ref name="LindopCapua2009">{{cite book |first1=Edmund |last1=Lindop |first2=Sarah |last2=De Capua |name-list-style=amp|title=America in the 1950s |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=JdAQXQjnKJUC&pg=PA47 |access-date=January 7, 2013 |date=September 1, 2009 |publisher=Twenty-First Century Books |isbn=978-0-8225-7642-6 |pages=47}}</ref> and 42,000 by the end of 1951.<ref>{{cite news |title=Credit Card at 50 |first=Marcy |last=Gordon |newspaper=Gainesville Sun |agency=Associated Press |via=Google News |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1320&dat=20000308&id=JoZRAAAAIBAJ&pg=4969,1891011 |date=March 8, 2000 |access-date=February 8, 2013 |page=5B}}</ref> At the time, the company was charging participating establishments 7% and billed cardholders $5{{nbsp}}a year.<ref name=lej-1961dec15>{{cite news |title=How to get a $1,000,000 Idea |agency=Associated Press |newspaper=Lewiston Evening Journal |via=Google News |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?id=mHQpAAAAIBAJ&pg=3406,6913333&dq=diners-club&hl=en |date=December 15, 1951 |access-date=January 7, 2013 |first=Hal |last=Boyle |page=4}}</ref> In 1952, McNamara sold his interest in Diners Club to his partners for $200,000.<ref name="HolperPaul">{{cite book |first1=Paul |last1=Holper |first2=Simon |last2=Torok |name-list-style=amp|title=Inventing Millions |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=k6S2i7xgox0C&pg=PA58 |access-date=February 8, 2013 |publisher=Orient Paperbacks |isbn=978-81-222-0458-2 |pages=57–58 |date=2008}}</ref> The first plastic Diners Club card was introduced in 1961;<ref name="Russia">{{cite news |title=Diners Club History |newspaper=Kommersant Money |date=December 13, 2010 |access-date=February 23, 2013 |url=https://ittah3orccej25mswg4qekpa6i-adwhj77lcyoafdy-www-kommersant-ru.translate.goog/doc/1556158 |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.--> |archive-date=January 12, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210112023923/https://ittah3orccej25mswg4qekpa6i-adwhj77lcyoafdy-www-kommersant-ru.translate.goog/doc/1556158 |url-status=dead }}</ref> by the mid-1960s, Diners Club had 1.3&nbsp;million cardholders.<ref name="Voorhees2004">{{cite book |first=Donald A. |last=Voorhees |title=Why Do Donuts Have Holes?: Fascinating Facts About What We Eat And Drink |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=9h7fcZFYWVcC&pg=PA209 |access-date=February 7, 2013 |date=October 1, 2004 |publisher=Citadel Press |isbn=978-0-8065-2551-8 |pages=209}}</ref>

Towards the end of the 1960s, Diners Club faced competition from banks that issued [[revolving credit]] cards through [[Bank of America]]'s BankAmericard (renamed [[Visa Inc.|Visa]]), and Interbank Master Charge (renamed [[MasterCard]]). 

In 1981, [[Citibank]], a unit of [[Citicorp]], bought Diners Club International, including the franchisor that holds rights to the Diners Club trademark. Despite this, a majority of the franchises abroad remain independently owned.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.nytimes.com/1981/06/23/business/citicorp-acquisition-of-diners-club-unit.html | title=Citicorp Acquisition of Diners Club Unit | work=[[The New York Times]] | date=23 June 1981}}</ref> 

In July 2008, [[Discover Financial]] acquired the Diners Club International network from Citigroup for $165{{nbsp}}million.<ref name=completes>{{cite news |url=https://archive.nytimes.com/dealbook.nytimes.com/2008/07/02/discover-completes-diners-club-acquisition/ | title=Discover Completes Diners Club Acquisition | work=[[The New York Times]] | date=July 2, 2008}}</ref> [[Capital One]] then acquired Discover Financial in 2025 in a $35.3{{nbsp}}billion all-stock deal.<ref>{{cite news |first1=Hakyung |last1=Kim |first2=Christine |last2=Wang |url=https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/19/capital-one-acquiring-discover-financial-services-report-says.html |title=Capital One to acquire Discover Financial Services in $35.3 billion all-stock deal | work=CNBC|date=February 19, 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |first1=Chris |last1=Prentice |first2=Saeed |last2=Azhar |url=https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/us-banking-regulators-ok-capital-one-discover-deal-2025-04-18/ |title=Capital One, Discover deal approved by US bank regulators |work=Reuters |date=April 18, 2025}}</ref>

In June 2022, [[National Australia Bank]] (NAB) acquired Citigroup's Consumer Banking business in Australia, including the Australian branch of Diners Club.<ref>{{Cite press release | url=https://www.citigroup.com/global/news/press-release/2022/citi-completes-sale-of-australian-consumer-business-to-national-australia-bank | title=Citi Completes Sale Of Australian Consumer Business To ... | publisher=[[Citigroup]] | date=June 1, 2022}}</ref> The Diners Club Corporate Card, Citi Mastercard Corporate Executive Companion Card, Diners Club Business Card and the Citi Mastercard Business Companion Card previously marketed under the Citibank brand were brought under the NAB brand as a result of the acquisition.

=== Europe ===
Diners Club has franchises in some European countries, such as Austria, Germany, North Macedonia, Slovenia, Spain, and Switzerland for example. Those franchises generally only issue Diners Club cards to residents of their respective countries.

However, since January 2025, ISX Financial has become a pan-European Diners Club franchisee, issuing the [https://www.flykk.it/ Flykk] Diners Club debit card in several European markets.

=== India ===
Diners Club was the first to introduce credit cards in India. Kali Mody is credited with opening the first Diners franchise in the country in 1961, which started an invitation-only credit card issuance.<ref>{{cite web |first=SUDEEP | last=CHAKRAVARTI |title=With market finally looking good, host of credit card issuers are readying for take-off |url=https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/special-report/story/19940615-with-market-finally-looking-good-host-of-credit-card-issuers-are-readying-for-take-off-809261-1994-06-15 |website=India Today |date=15 June 1994 |language=en}}</ref> Its cards have been offered by [[Citibank India]], followed by [[HDFC Bank]] which took over in 2011.

In 2021, the [[Reserve Bank of India]] (RBI) barred the company (along with [[American Express]]) from issuing new cards for failing to follow norms about storing data within the country. The ban was lifted later in the year after it complied with the RBI guidelines.<ref>{{cite web |title=India cenbank lifts ban on Diners Club issuing new cards after six months |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-cenbank-lifts-ban-diners-club-issuing-new-cards-2021-11-09/ |website=Reuters |language=en |date=9 November 2021}}</ref>

===South Korea===
In South Korea, Diners Club started its business around the 1980s. However, in 1993, [[Citibank Korea]] sold Diners Club Korea to Daewoo Group, and after the acquisition by [[Daewoo]] Group, the parent company of [[Daewoo Motors]], Diners Club Korea ran a successful business with sales of over 400 billion won. Diners Club Korea went through strict card issuance screening and solidified its position as a premium credit card in Korea. The number of Diners Club member stores increased more than tenfold, and the number of members reached hundreds of thousands, and the business was successful. However, Daewoo Group, which had acquired Diners Club, sold Diners Club Korea to [[Hyundai Motor Company]] after experiencing financial difficulties and starting a workout. Later, Diners Club Korea's name was changed to [[Hyundai Card]], and [[Hyundai Motor Company]] began issuing [[Visa Inc.|Visa]] and [[MasterCard]] credit cards other than Diners Club to start a credit card business in earnest. Hyundai Card continued to maintain the exclusive license of Diners Club, but stopped issuing the card and discontinued the license contract with Diners Club because there was no benefit compared to the license fee. As a result, [[Hyundai Card]] was able to issue [[American Express]] cards and signed an exclusive license for American Express Card individual cards in Korea. Since then, Diners Club has signed a license contract with Korean credit card companies [[BC Card]] and [[Woori Bank|Woori Card]] to issue Diners Club cards in Korea. In addition, Diners Club, which previously used Hyundai Card's payment network, began using BC Card's payment network in 2018. BC Card is a credit card company established by various Korean banks such as [[Citibank Korea]] and [[Woori Bank]]. Because it has contracts with almost all stores in Korea, Diners Club cards can be used at almost all stores and restaurants in Korea.<ref>{{cite news |title=The reason why Hyundai Card terminated the contract with Diners Club, which was the parent company |url=https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/finance/2019/11/07/0015 |access-date=22 August 2023 |agency=Associated Press |newspaper=Biz Watch |date=7 November 2019 |page=21 |author=Hyunchang Kong}}</ref>

===North American franchise===
====MasterCard alliance====
In 2004, Diners Club announced an agreement with [[Mastercard]].{{Citation needed|date=November 2023}} Since 2004, all Diners Club cards issued in the United States and Canada (by Citibank until 2009,{{Citation needed|date=November 2023}} by BMO since) feature a Mastercard logo and 16-digit account number on the front and can be used wherever Mastercard is accepted. They are processed through the Mastercard network.

Initially, cards from other countries continued to bear a 14-digit account number on the front, with the Mastercard logo on the back. However, since the takeover of Diners Club International by Discover Financial Services, international cards have had the Discover logo on the back and the Diners Club International network is part of the Discover network. Since then, the only connection between Diners Club in North America and Diners Club International is BMO's continuous licensed use of the Diners Club brands, including Carte Blanche, on Mastercard credit cards.

====Carte Blanche====
Carte Blanche began in 1958 when the [[Hilton Hotels]] travel & entertainment card was renamed. Hilton sold Carte Blanche to [[Citibank|First National City Bank]] in 1966. Regulatory challenges forced First National City Bank to sell Carte Blanche to [[Avco]] in 1968. In 1978, Citicorp (parent company of First National City Bank which was renamed Citibank) reacquired Carte Blanche without regulatory opposition.<ref>{{cite news |title=Justice Won't Fight Carte Blanche Buy |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1129&dat=19780403&id=TkwNAAAAIBAJ&pg=3486,243369 |access-date=14 April 2014 |agency=Associated Press |newspaper=Pittsburgh Post-Gazette |via=Google News Archive |date=3 April 1978 |page=21 |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.-->}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |title=Carte Blanche Credit Cards |url=https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1946&dat=19581115&id=7YRhAAAAIBAJ&pg=5423,2946276 |access-date=14 April 2014 |newspaper=Montreal Gazette |via=Google News Archive |date=15 November 1958 |page=37 |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.-->}}</ref> The 1960s- and 1970s-era Carte Blanche cards were considered more prestigious worldwide than their competition, the [[American Express]] and Diners Club cards, though its small cardmember base hindered its success. In 1981, Citicorp acquired the Diners Club card, and by the mid-1990s the Carte Blanche card was being phased out in favor of Diners Club. Parent company [[Citigroup]] was formed in 1998 with the merger of Citicorp and the Travelers Group. Citigroup issued a premium Diners Club card in 2000, naming it the Diners Club Carte Blanche card. It was an upper-level charge card on par with the American Express [[Platinum Card]].

The Carte Blanche card in North America, now a branded Mastercard issued by BMO under its Diners Club International brand license, carried a US$300 annual fee as of April 2015 and offers an extensive menu of perks.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.dinersclubus.com/home/professional-cards/profcards-l2/carte-blanche-card |title=Carte Blanche |publisher=Diners Club International |date=<!--undated--> |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.-->}}</ref>

It marketed the card under the combined name for a period of time as the "Diners Club/enRoute Card".<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1992/03/27/business/company-news-air-canada-sells-credit-card-unit.html |title=Air Canada Sells Credit Card Unit New York Times |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=March 27, 1992 |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.-->}}</ref> The enRoute business was valued at over $300&nbsp;million at the time of acquisition.

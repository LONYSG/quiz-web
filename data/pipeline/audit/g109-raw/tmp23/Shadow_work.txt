'''Shadow work''' is a concept in [[economics]] identifying tasks performed by consumers, as a complement of industrial labor and services, as [[unpaid labor]]. It includes assembling of [[Flat pack|goods as components]], [[self-checkout]], and self-service at gas stations. This contrasts with the well-established view of [[unpaid labor]] done for self-subsistence or to give to others, encompassing all tasks individuals do without compensation.

The term was coined by [[Ivan Illich]] in his 1980 article<ref name="illich">Illich, Ivan. ''Shadow - Work'' Philosophica 26,1980 (2), Pp. 7-46.</ref> and in his 1981 book<ref>Illich, Ivan. ''Shadow Work.'' Salem, New Hampshire and London: Marion Boyars,  1981.</ref> of the same title.<ref name="nyt">{{cite news |author=Craig Lambert |title=Our Unpaid, Extra Shadow Work |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2011/10/30/opinion/sunday/our-unpaid-extra-shadow-work.html?ref=opinion |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |date=October 29, 2011 |accessdate=2011-10-25 }}</ref>

Craig Lambert, a former editor of ''[[Harvard Magazine]]'' wrote about the new trend towards unpaid "shadow work" in 2011 and followed up his research in a book called ''Shadow Work: The Unpaid, Unseen Jobs That Fill Your Day'' in 2015. In it, he itemizes many of the unpaid tasks ordinary people do now that others used to do, such as [[filling station attendant|pumping gasoline]], [[bagger|bagging groceries]], [[Travel agency|making travel arrangements]], and [[Airport check-in|checking in baggage at airports]]. He includes the rise of technology and robotics as forces leading to the growth of shadow work, and also includes such factors as [[crowdsourcing]] and parental over-engagement in their children's lives. He argues that shifting tasks to consumers takes away from their time and reduces the amount of casual social interaction in people's lives. It also limits the number of opportunities for low-skilled [[entry-level]] work (such as pumping gas).<ref>{{cite news|last1=Liebetrau|first1=Eric|title=Book Review: 'Shadow Work' by Craig Lambert|url=https://www.bostonglobe.com/arts/books/2015/05/21/book-review-shadow-work-the-unpaid-unseen-jobs-that-fill-your-day-craig-lambert/Ea1MPA9o0CujES4gCOMmzK/story.html|accessdate=3 November 2015|work=Boston Globe|publisher=Boston Globe Media Partners, LLC|date=May 21, 2015}}</ref>

'''''Burning''''' ({{Korean|hangul=버닝}}) is a 2018 South Korean [[psychological thriller]]<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-10-09|title=New Haruki Murakami Adaptation 'Burning' Is A Feverish Psychological Thriller|url=https://adelaidefilmfestival.org/2018/10/new-haruki-murakami-adaptation-burning-is-a-feverish-psychological-thriller/|access-date=2020-06-08|website=Adelaide Film Festival|archive-date=2020-06-08|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200608093704/https://adelaidefilmfestival.org/2018/10/new-haruki-murakami-adaptation-burning-is-a-feverish-psychological-thriller/|url-status=dead}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=Burning review - an explosive psychological thriller|url=https://theartsdesk.com/film/burning-review-explosive-psychological-thriller|access-date=2020-06-08|website=theartsdesk.com|date=2 February 2019|archive-date=2022-06-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220602063639/https://theartsdesk.com/film/burning-review-explosive-psychological-thriller|url-status=live}}</ref> film co-written, produced, and directed by [[Lee Chang-dong]]. The film is based on the short story "Barn Burning" from ''[[The Elephant Vanishes]]'' by [[Haruki Murakami]], with elements inspired by [[William Faulkner]]'s [[Barn Burning|story of the same name]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Brzeski |first1=Patrick |title=HKIFF Interview: South Korea's Lee Chang-dong on the Many Mysteries of 'Burning' |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/oscars-interview-lee-chang-dong-burning-1167869/ |access-date=17 July 2021 |work=The Hollywood Reporter |date=December 10, 2018}}</ref> It stars [[Yoo Ah-in]], [[Steven Yeun]], and [[Jeon Jong-seo]]. The plot depicts a young deliveryman, Jong-su (Yoo), who runs into his childhood friend, Hae-mi (Jeon). They soon meet an enigmatic young man named Ben (Yeun), whom Jong-su becomes suspicious of, and he begins to believe Hae-mi is in danger.

The first film by Lee after a hiatus of eight years, the film premiered on May 16, 2018, at the [[2018 Cannes Film Festival]], where it competed for the {{Lang|fr|[[Palme d'Or]]|italic=no}}; it ended up receiving the [[FIPRESCI|FIPRESCI International Critics' Prize]] at the festival. It was released the following day in South Korea and on October 26, 2018, in the United States.

The film received widespread critical acclaim,<ref>{{cite journal |author-last=Boman |author-first=Björn |date=December 2020 |title=From ''Oldboy'' to ''Burning'': ''Han'' in South Korean films |editor-last=Valsiner |editor-first=Jaan |editor-link=Jaan Valsiner |journal=[[Culture & Psychology]] |publisher=[[SAGE Publications]] |volume=26 |issue=4 |pages=919–932 |doi=10.1177/1354067X20922146 |doi-access=free |eissn=1461-7056 |issn=1354-067X }}</ref> with praise for its sense of unease, ambiguous narrative, and performances. It was selected as the [[List of South Korean submissions for the Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|South Korean entry]] for [[Academy Award for Best International Feature Film|Best Foreign Language Film]] at the [[91st Academy Awards]]; although it was not nominated, it became the first Korean film to make it to the final nine-film shortlist. It received [[List of accolades received by Burning|numerous other accolades]], and was included on several critics' "top ten" lists for the year 2018, notably those of ''[[The New York Times]]'', ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'', and the [[Associated Press]]. It has been regarded by some critics as one of the best films of the 21st century,<ref>{{Cite web|title=100 best films of the 21st century according to critics|url=https://stacker.com/stories/1812/100-best-films-21st-century-according-critics|access-date=2021-07-08|website=Stacker|language=en|archive-date=2021-07-13|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210713115708/https://stacker.com/stories/1812/100-best-films-21st-century-according-critics|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|title=The 100 best films of the 21st century|url=http://www.theguardian.com/film/2019/sep/13/100-best-films-movies-of-the-21st-century|date=2019-09-13|access-date=2021-07-08|website=the Guardian|language=en}}</ref> and it was voted as the best Korean film of all time on Korean Screen, from a poll of over 150 critics from 28 countries,<ref>{{Cite web|title=100 Greatest Korean Films Ever {{!}} koreanscreen.com|url=https://www.koreanscreen.com/100-greatest-korean-films-how-made|access-date=2021-07-08|website=Korean Screen|language=en|archive-date=2022-09-02|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220902123551/https://www.koreanscreen.com/100-greatest-korean-films-how-made|url-status=live}}</ref> displacing movies like ''[[Parasite (2019 film)|Parasite]]'' (2019), ''[[Oldboy (2003 film)|Oldboy]]'' (2003) and ''[[The Housemaid (1960 film)|The Housemaid]]'' (1960).

== Plot<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be between 400 to 700 words. -->==
Lee Jong-su, an aspiring young novelist, performs odd jobs in [[Seoul]]. One day he runs into Shin Hae-mi, a childhood neighbor and classmate, at a promotion at which he is making a delivery. Jong-su initially does not remember her, but Hae-mi tells him she had plastic surgery. Jong-su gives her the pink watch that he won at the promotion. Later, she tells him about her upcoming trip to Africa and asks him to feed her cat, Boil, while she is away. Before Hae-mi's departure, Jong-su's father, a cattle farmer in [[Paju]], becomes entangled in disagreeable legal affairs, and Jong-su has to return to the farm. Jong-su visits Hae-mi's apartment, where he receives instructions about feeding the cat. They then have sex.

After Hae-mi departs, Jong-su dutifully feeds her cat, although he never sees it. He does, however, presume that a cat is there because he finds feces in the litter box. He begins habitually masturbating in Hae-mi's apartment. One day Hae-mi calls, saying she had become stranded at [[Jomo Kenyatta International Airport|Nairobi Airport]] for three days after a bombing nearby. When Jong-su comes to pick her up, she arrives with Ben, a man whom she met and bonded with during the crisis. The three go out for dinner, where Hae-mi cries and confesses that she wants to disappear. Ben is wealthy, but does not give a clear answer when asked what kind of work he does.

At Jong-su's farm, the trio smokes cannabis and Hae-mi dances topless. After Hae-mi has fallen asleep on the sofa, Ben confesses that every two months, he burns an abandoned greenhouse as a hobby. He notes that Jong-su's rural neighborhood is full of greenhouses. When asked when his next burning will take place, Ben claims it will be very soon and close to Jong-su's house. Jong-su chastises Hae-mi for disrobing in front of other men. She quietly gets into Ben's car and they drive off.

Jong-su keeps watch around the neighborhood to see if any greenhouses burn down, but none do. One afternoon, in front of an intact greenhouse that he is inspecting, he receives a call from Hae-mi, which cuts off after a few seconds of ambiguous noises. Jong-su becomes worried as she does not answer any of his calls, and begins to investigate after her phone number becomes disconnected. He convinces the landlady to let him into Hae-mi's apartment so that he can feed her cat. Hae-mi's apartment is unnaturally clean; her suitcase remains, and all signs of the cat are gone. Jong-su begins staking out Ben's [[Gangnam]] apartment and following him to see where he goes. When he sees Ben's [[Porsche]] parked outside a restaurant, he goes inside to confront him. Ben insists that he did burn down a greenhouse near Jong-su's house. A young woman approaches the table, apologizing to Ben for being late. As the three of them leave the restaurant, Jong-su asks Ben if he has heard from Hae-mi and whether she has gone on a trip. Ben says he has not heard from her, and he doubts she has gone on a trip because she could not afford it. Ben says Jong-su was the only person she trusted and that it made him jealous for the first time in his life.

One day, Ben finds Jong-su waiting outside his place and invites him up to his apartment. Ben has a new cat, which he claims is a rescued stray. In the restroom, Jong-su finds a pink watch, similar to the one he gave Hae-mi, hidden in a drawer containing other pieces of women's jewelry. When Ben's cat runs out of the apartment, Jong-su finds that it answers to the name "Boil." Jong-su silently leaves the dinner party Ben is hosting, despite Ben's pleas for him to stay.

Jong-su asks to meet Ben in the countryside, claiming he is with Hae-mi. Ben sees that Hae-mi is not there and asks Jong-su where she is; Jong-su stabs him to death. He then douses Ben's car and body in gasoline and sets them on fire, tossing his blood-soaked clothes in as well. He stumbles naked to his truck and drives off.

== Release ==
[[File:Cannes 2018 23.jpg|thumb|Cast and director at the [[2018 Cannes Film Festival]].]]

Lee's 2007 film ''[[Secret Sunshine]]'' and his 2010 film ''[[Poetry (film)|Poetry]]'' both premiered as in-competition entries at the [[Cannes Film Festival]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Lee Chang-dong's latest film, 'Burning,' billed as possible Cannes lineup|url=http://english.yonhapnews.co.kr/news/2018/03/25/0200000000AEN20180325004500315.htm|publisher=[[Yonhap News Agency]]|access-date=10 April 2018|date=25 March 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title='Burning' Teaser: Filmmaker Lee Chang-Dong Is Back With His First Film In 7 Years|url=https://theplaylist.net/burning-teaser-20180405/|website=The Playlist|access-date=10 April 2018|date=5 April 2018|archive-date=10 April 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180410135332/https://theplaylist.net/burning-teaser-20180405/|url-status=live}}</ref> In April 2018, ''Burning'' was among the lineup of in-competition entries announced for the 2018 Cannes Film Festival.<ref name="variety2">{{cite web|url=https://variety.com/2018/film/news/cannes-lineup-includes-new-films-from-spike-lee-jean-luc-godard-1202751300/|title=Cannes Lineup Includes New Films From Spike Lee, Jean-Luc Godard|work=Variety|date=12 April 2018|access-date=12 April 2018|archive-date=21 April 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180421032959/http://variety.com/2018/film/news/cannes-lineup-includes-new-films-from-spike-lee-jean-luc-godard-1202751300/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/news.jsp?pageIndex=1&blbdComCd=601006&seq=4791&mode=VIEW&returnUrl=&searchKeyword=|title=LEE Chang-dong Returns to Cannes Competition with BURNING|date=April 23, 2018|website=Korean Film Biz Zone|access-date=May 21, 2018|archive-date=December 20, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181220230827/http://www.koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/news.jsp?pageIndex=1&blbdComCd=601006&seq=4791&mode=VIEW&returnUrl=&searchKeyword=|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Box office ===
''Burning'' grossed {{US$|719,772|long=no}} in the United States and Canada, and $6.1 million in other territories (including $4.2 million in South Korea), for a total worldwide gross of {{US$|6.8 million|long=no}}.<ref name="NUM"/>

On its opening day in South Korea, ''Burning'' attracted 52,324 viewers in total, ranking second at the box office behind ''[[Deadpool 2]]'' (248,904 viewers).<ref>{{cite web|title=KoBiz - Korean film, news, actor, movie, cinema, location & Korean Film Archive|url=http://koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/boxOffice_Daily.jsp?mode=BOXOFFICE_DAILY|website=Korean Film Biz Zone|access-date=2018-05-17|archive-date=2019-04-11|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190411030919/http://koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/news/boxOffice_Daily.jsp?mode=BOXOFFICE_DAILY|url-status=live}}</ref> Over the first weekend of its release, ''Burning'' received 220,717 admissions, placing third at the local box office.<ref>{{cite web|title=(LEAD) 'Deadpool 2' takes down 'Infinity War' at box office|url=http://english.yonhapnews.co.kr/kwave/2018/05/21/3001000000AEN20180521001351315.html|website=[[Yonhap News Agency]]|author=Shim Sun-ah|date=May 21, 2018|access-date=May 29, 2018|archive-date=May 29, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180529130421/http://english.yonhapnews.co.kr/kwave/2018/05/21/3001000000AEN20180521001351315.html|url-status=live}}</ref> By the end of its run, the film recorded 528,168 admissions in its home country.<ref>{{cite web |title=BURNING (2018) |url=http://www.koreanfilm.or.kr/eng/films/index/filmsView.jsp?movieCd=20173202 |website=Korean Film Biz Zone}}</ref>

Nick Pinkerton, writing in ''[[Artforum]]'', was more muted, writing that "''Burning'' is strewn with all sorts of information whose exact meaning and validity is impossible to determine... a film with such a diffident, often passive protagonist must generate its tensions and attractions elsewhere{{snd}}memorable supporting players, a tactile atmosphere, a complex sense of the social sphere, an emphatic emotionalism".<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.artforum.com/film/nick-pinkerton-on-lee-chang-dong-8217-s-burning-2018-77314|title=Mild Roast|last=Pinkerton|first=Nick|date=2018-10-25|website=www.artforum.com|language=en-US|access-date=2018-12-15|archive-date=2018-12-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181215121920/https://www.artforum.com/film/nick-pinkerton-on-lee-chang-dong-8217-s-burning-2018-77314|url-status=live}}</ref> Writing in the ''[[The Korea Times|Korea Times]]'', David Tizzard argued that Ben may function not as an autonomous character but as Jeong-su’s projected double—a "''[[Fight Club]]''–style" manifestation of his fantasies, resentments, and dissociated self. This reading suggests that Ben and Jeong-su form a single psychological entity, with their mirrored actions, shared habits, and overlapping memories implying two figures occupying one fractured consciousness.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tizzard |first=David |date=2025-11-22 |title=Ben doesn't exist: Rereading 'Burning' in the ruins of late capitalism - The Korea Times |url=https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20251122/ben-doesnt-exist-rereading-burning-in-the-ruins-of-late-capitalism |access-date=2025-11-23 |website=www.koreatimes.co.kr |language=en}}</ref>

The depiction of the female character Hae-mi in the film attracted some criticism. However, Adam Nayman from ''[[Film Society of Lincoln Center]]''<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.filmcomment.com/blog/film-comment-podcast-toronto-one/|title=The Film Comment Podcast: Toronto One|last=Nayman|first=Adam|date=2018-09-11|website=Film Society of Lincoln Center|language=en|access-date=2018-12-17|archive-date=2018-12-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181217202500/https://www.filmcomment.com/blog/film-comment-podcast-toronto-one/|url-status=live}}</ref> argued and emphasized for ''[[The Ringer (website)|The Ringer]]'' that the story is "told fully from Jongsu's point of view", so "it's fair to ask whether Lee Chang Dong is cultivating true audience solidarity or urging us to understand the story exclusively through the lens of his hero's prejudices: to see Haemi and Ben as idealized and demonized figures, respectively."<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theringer.com/movies/2018/10/26/18025994/burning-movie-review-steven-yeun|title='Burning' Is One of the Best Films of the Year|last=Nayman|first=Adam|date=2018-10-26|website=The Ringer|language=en|access-date=2018-12-17|archive-date=2018-12-18|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181218010500/https://www.theringer.com/movies/2018/10/26/18025994/burning-movie-review-steven-yeun|url-status=live}}</ref> Phoebe Chen of the ''Film Feminist Journal: Another Gaze'', analyzed that Haemi's existence had a grander purpose in the film, and wrote, "If you know ''Burning'' is adapted from a [[Haruki Murakami]] short story, however loosely, you know that Haemi will vanish. The trope of the missing woman is built into Murakami's narrative DNA: her vanishing will preoccupy the protagonist, neurotically at first, then fade over the months and years to a dull malaise", and "The mystery of Haemi's disappearance is technically 'solved', but becomes supplanted by one grander: the mystery of a world that tantalises with the hope of futurity while locking its millions of subjects in a cold impasse."<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.anothergaze.com/act-taking-lee-chang-dongs-burning/|title=The Act of Taking: Lee Chang Dong's 'Burning'|last=Chen|first=Phoebe|date=2018-11-14|website=Another Gaze|language=en|access-date=2018-12-18}}</ref> [[Alexandra Heller-Nicholas]] of ''[[Alliance of Women Film Journalists]]'' wrote that the character of Hae-mi is much more than simply a missing woman as a plot device, but one of the most powerful parts of a film that casually yet firmly addresses the pressures on women.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://awfj.org/blog/2018/10/02/tiff18-review-burning-alexander-heller-nicholas|title=TIFF18 Review: BURNING — Alexander Heller Nicholas|last=Nicholas|first=Alexandra Heller|date=2018-10-02|website=Alliance of Women Film Journalists|language=en|access-date=2018-12-28|archive-date=2018-12-31|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181231092424/https://awfj.org/blog/2018/10/02/tiff18-review-burning-alexander-heller-nicholas/|url-status=live}}</ref>

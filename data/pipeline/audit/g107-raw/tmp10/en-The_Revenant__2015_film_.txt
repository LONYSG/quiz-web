'''''The Revenant''''' is a 2015 American [[Epic film|epic]] [[Western film]] directed by [[Alejandro González Iñárritu|Alejandro G. Iñárritu]]. The screenplay by [[Mark L. Smith]] and Iñárritu is based in part on [[Michael Punke]]'s 2002 novel ''[[The Revenant (novel)|The Revenant]]'', which describes [[frontier]]sman [[Hugh Glass]]'s experiences in 1823. The film is a remake of the film ''[[Man in the Wilderness]]'' (1971).<ref name="Slow-Paced Suffering">{{cite web|title=‘The Revenant’ Is Slow-Paced Suffering Leo DiCaprio Stars in Remake of an Epic Western|url=https://www.independent.com/2016/01/13/revenant-is-slow-paced-suffering//|work=[[Santa Barbara Independent]]|accessdate=26 August 2022|date=13 January 2016}}</ref> The film stars [[Leonardo DiCaprio]] as Glass, alongside [[Tom Hardy]], [[Domhnall Gleeson]] and [[Will Poulter]].

In August 2001, [[Akiva Goldsman]] purchased Punke's manuscript. Iñárritu signed on to direct ''The Revenant'' in August 2011. In April 2014, after several delays due to other projects, Iñárritu confirmed that he was beginning work on it and that DiCaprio had been cast in the lead role. [[Principal photography]] began in October 2014. Location and crew concerns delayed production from May to August 2015.

''The Revenant'' premiered at [[TCL Chinese Theatre]] in Los Angeles, California, on December 16, 2015. It had a [[limited release]] on December 25 and a [[wide release]] on January 8, 2016 by [[20th Century Fox]]. It was a commercial success, grossing $533 million worldwide and becoming Iñárritu's highest-grossing film. It received critical acclaim, with praise for DiCaprio's performance, Iñárritu's direction and [[Emmanuel Lubezki]]'s cinematography.<ref name="Stephanie Zacharek 2016, p54">Stephanie Zacharek, TIME magazine, January 18, 2016, p54.</ref><ref name="MoraskiCBS_151225"/><ref name="newyorkerreview">{{cite magazine|last1=Lane|first1=Anthony|title=Wilder West "The Hateful Eight" and "The Revenant."|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|date=December 27, 2015|url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/01/04/wilder-west|access-date=February 8, 2016|archive-date=February 1, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160201104123/http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2016/01/04/wilder-west|url-status=live}}</ref>

It won three [[Golden Globe Awards]] and five [[BAFTA Awards]], including Best Film at both ceremonies. At the [[88th Academy Awards]], the film received 12 nominations, including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] and [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]] (Hardy). It won the Academy Awards for [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] (Iñárritu, his second consecutive in that category), [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]] (DiCaprio, his first after 5 previous nominations), and [[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] ([[Emmanuel Lubezki]], his third consecutive in that category). DiCaprio also won the [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama|Golden Globe Award]], the [[Screen Actors Guild Award for Outstanding Performance by a Male Actor in a Leading Role|Screen Actors Guild Award]], the [[BAFTA Award for Best Actor in a Leading Role|BAFTA Award]], and the [[Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Actor|Critics' Choice Award]] for Best Actor.

==Plot==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, summaries should be 400-700 words. -->
Fur trapper [[Hugh Glass]] watches as American soldiers burn down a [[Native Americans in the United States|Native American]] village on the [[Great Plains]].

Years later, in  1823, during the [[Arikara War]], Glass guides Captain [[Andrew Henry (fur trader)|Andrew Henry]]'s [[trapping|trappers]] through the territory of the present-day [[The Dakotas|Dakotas]]. While he and his half-[[Pawnee people|Pawnee]] son, Hawk, are hunting, the company's camp is attacked by an [[Arikara]] war party which is seeking to recover its chief's abducted daughter, Powaqa. Many of the trappers are killed during the fight, and the rest of them escape onto a boat. Guided by Glass, the survivors begin a trek to [[Fort Kiowa]] on foot because Glass believes traveling down the [[Missouri River]] will make them vulnerable. After docking, the crew stash their pelts near the shore.

While scouting game, Glass is mauled and left near death by a [[grizzly bear]]. Fearing another Arikara attack, trapper  John Fitzgerald argues that they must [[Euthanasia|mercy-kill]] Glass and keep moving. Henry agrees, but he is unable to pull the trigger. Instead, he offers to pay anyone who will stay with Glass and bury him after he dies. When the only volunteers are Hawk and the young [[Jim Bridger]], Fitzgerald, wanting the money to recoup his losses from the abandoned pelts, also agrees to stay.

After the others leave, Fitzgerald attempts to smother Glass but is stopped and intervened by Hawk. Fitzgerald stabs Hawk to death as Glass watches helplessly. The next morning, Fitzgerald convinces Bridger, who is unaware of Hawk's murder, that the Arikara are approaching and they must abandon Glass. At first Bridger protests, but he ultimately follows Fitzgerald after the latter leaves Glass half-buried alive in a makeshift grave. Bridger leaves behind a canteen on which he had engraved a spiral symbol. The next morning, Fitzgerald admits to Bridger that there are no pursuing Arikara. They reach the fort, and Fitzgerald falsely reports to Henry that Hawk vanished and Glass died. Bridger is complicit in the lie about Glass's death, but remains unaware of Hawk's murder.

Glass starts an arduous journey through the wilderness, bringing Bridger's canteen with him. He performs a crude cauterization of his wounds and eludes the pursuing Arikara by jumping into river rapids. He encounters Pawnee refugee Hikuc, who tells Glass that "revenge is in the [[Great Spirit|Creator]]'s hands." The men share bison meat and travel together. As a storm approaches, Hikuc constructs a [[sweat lodge]] for the feverish Glass to shelter in. After a hallucinogenic experience in the lodge, Glass emerges to discover that [[French Canadians|French Canadian]] [[Voyageurs|hunters]] have [[Lynching|lynched]] Hikuc. Glass infiltrates the hunters' camp and sees their leader, Toussaint, raping Powaqa. Glass frees her; she castrates Toussaint and flees while Glass kills several hunters and recovers Hikuc's horse, leaving Bridger's canteen behind. The following morning, Glass is ambushed by the Arikara and driven over a cliff on his horse. He survives the night by disemboweling the dead horse and sheltering inside its carcass.

A French survivor staggers into Fort Kiowa, and Bridger recognizes the man's spiral-engraved canteen. Believing that it was stolen, Henry organizes a search party to find Hawk. Fitzgerald, realizing that Glass is alive, empties the outpost's safe and flees, intending to reach [[Texas]]. The search party finds the exhausted Glass. Furious, Henry orders the arrest of Bridger upon returning to the fort, but Glass vouches for him, saying Bridger was deceived by Fitzgerald, who murdered Hawk. Glass and Henry set out in pursuit of Fitzgerald.

After the two men split up, Fitzgerald ambushes, kills, and scalps Henry. Glass props up Henry's corpse on his horse to act as a decoy, taking its place himself to ambush Fitzgerald and shoot him in the shoulder. He pursues Fitzgerald to a riverbank, where they engage in a brutal fight. Glass is about to kill Fitzgerald, but he spots a band of Arikara downstream. He remembers Hikuc's words and pushes Fitzgerald downstream into the hands of the Arikara. Their chief, Elk Dog, kills Fitzgerald by stabbing and then scalping him, and the Arikara (who have found Powaqa) spare Glass. Glass retreats into the mountains, where he is visited by his wife's spirit.

==Production==
===Development===
[[File:Alejandro González Iñárritu 2014.jpg|thumb|Director [[Alejandro González Iñárritu|Alejandro G.Iñárritu]]]]
Development of ''The Revenant'' began in August 2001, with producer [[Akiva Goldsman]] acquiring the rights to [[Michael Punke]]'s then-unpublished manuscript.<ref name="Goldsman"/> [[David Rabe]] had written the film's script.<ref name="DiCaprioCast"/> The production was picked up by [[Park Chan-wook]], with [[Samuel L. Jackson]] in mind to star. Park later left the project.<ref name="Park"/><ref name="HillcoatGone"/> The development stalled until 2010 when [[Mark L. Smith]] wrote a new adaptation of the novel for [[Steve Golin]]'s [[Anonymous Content]]. In May 2010, Smith revealed that [[John Hillcoat]] was attached to direct the film and that [[Christian Bale]] was in negotiation to star.<ref name="BaleTalks"/> Hillcoat left the project in October 2010.<ref name="HillcoatGone"/> [[Jean-François Richet]] was considered to replace him, but [[Alejandro González Iñárritu|Alejandro G.Iñárritu]] signed on to direct in August 2011.<ref name="HillcoatGone"/><ref name="InarrituOn"/> Goldsman was also confirmed to be producing with Weed Road Pictures.<ref name="InarrituOn"/> In November, [[Regency Enterprises|New Regency Productions]] joined to produce with Anonymous Content, and [[20th Century Fox]] was confirmed to be distributing the film.<ref name="EllisonTalks"/><ref name="NewRegency"/> Days later, Iñárritu stated that he was seeking [[Leonardo DiCaprio]] and [[Sean Penn]] for the two lead roles.<ref name="DiCaprioPenn"/>

Once Iñárritu agreed to direct, he began working with Smith on script rewrites. In an interview with ''Creative Screenwriting'', Smith admitted that during this process, he was unsure if Iñárritu would even be able to film some of the sequences they wrote. He recalled, "He would have some ideas and I would say, 'Alejandro, we can't pull this off. It's not going to work', and he would say, 'Mark, trust me, we can do this.' In the end, he was right."<ref>{{cite web|url=http://creativescreenwriting.com/i-think-he-wanted-to-toss-me-off-the-cliff-mark-l-smith-on-the-revenant-and-martyrs/|title="I think he wanted to toss me off the cliff." Mark L. Smith on The Revenant and Martyrs|last=McKittrick|first=Christopher|date=January 27, 2016|publisher=Creative Screenwriting|access-date=January 27, 2016|archive-date=March 15, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160315000439/http://creativescreenwriting.com/i-think-he-wanted-to-toss-me-off-the-cliff-mark-l-smith-on-the-revenant-and-martyrs/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The project was put on hold in March 2012, as New Regency hired Iñárritu to direct an adaptation of ''Flim-Flam Man'', Jennifer Vogel's non-fiction book about her criminal father.<ref name="FlimFlam"/> Penn was also under consideration for the lead role in that film.<ref name="PennFlim"/> In December 2012, Iñárritu announced that his next film would be ''[[Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance)]]'', a comedy-drama about an actor who once played a famous superhero. He ended up winning the Academy Awards for Best Director and Best Original Screenplay, with the film winning Best Picture. Filming took place in March 2013.<ref name="Birdman"/> ''The Revenant'' was scheduled to begin production right after ''Birdman'' [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]].<ref name="Worldview"/>

A production budget of $60 million was granted, with $30 million funded by New Regency. [[Brett Ratner]]'s [[RatPac-Dune Entertainment]], a joint venture between Ratner's RatPac Entertainment and Fox's former financing partner, Dune Entertainment, also funded the film.<ref name="EllisonTalks"/> [[Worldview Entertainment]], which also co-financed ''Birdman'', was originally set to fund the film but backed out in July 2014 due to the departure of CEO [[Christopher Woodrow]].<ref name="EllisonTalks"/><ref name="Worldview"/><ref name="FoxFunding"/> New Regency approached Fox for additional funding, but Fox declined, citing the [[pay-or-play]] contracts made for both DiCaprio and [[Tom Hardy]], which would require that the actors be paid regardless of whether the film is completed.<ref name="FoxFunding"/> [[Annapurna Pictures]]' [[Megan Ellison]] entered negotiations to finance the film shortly after.<ref name="EllisonTalks"/><ref name="FoxFunding"/> The Chinese company [[Guangdong Alpha Animation and Culture Company]] partially financed the film.<ref name="financing"/>

===Filming===
[[Principal photography]] for ''The Revenant'' began in October 2014.<ref name="Budget"/> A planned two-week break from filming in December was extended to six weeks which forced Tom Hardy to drop out of ''[[Suicide Squad (2016 film)|Suicide Squad]]''. In February 2015, Iñárritu, who shot the film using [[sunlight|natural lighting]], stated that production would last "until the end of April or May", as the crew is "shooting in such remote far-away locations that, by the time we arrive and have to return, we have already spent 40% of the day".<ref name="LongerFilming"/><ref name="NineMonths"/><ref name="FilmingEndMarch"/> Ultimately, principal photography wrapped in August 2015.{{citation needed|date=April 2020}}

The waterfall scenes were filmed at the Kootenai Falls near [[Libby, Montana]]. Though the initial plan was to film the last scenes in Canada, the weather was ultimately too warm, leading the filmmakers to locations near the Río Olivia in [[Tierra del Fuego Province, Argentina|Tierra del Fuego]], [[Argentina]] with snow on the ground, to shoot the ending.<ref name="Budget"/><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/travel/destinations/north-america/galleries/the-revenant-filming-locations-canada/revenant17/|title=The incredible filming locations for The Revenant|newspaper=The Telegraph|date=February 15, 2016|access-date=April 4, 2018|archive-date=January 26, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180126070646/http://www.telegraph.co.uk/travel/destinations/north-america/galleries/the-revenant-filming-locations-canada/revenant17/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Crew members often complained about difficult filming, with many quitting or being fired. [[Mary Parent]] was then brought in as a producer.<ref name="Budget"/> Iñárritu stated that some of the crew members had left the production, explaining that "as a director, if I identify a violin that is out of tune, I have to take that from the orchestra." On his experience, DiCaprio stated: "I can name 30 or 40 sequences that were some of the most difficult things I've ever had to do. Whether it's going in and out of frozen rivers, or sleeping in animal carcasses, or what I ate on set. [I was] enduring freezing cold and possible hypothermia constantly."<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.yahoo.com/movies/leonardo-dicaprio-on-fighting-a-1281529422913590.html|title=Leonardo DiCaprio on Fighting a Bear in 'The Revenant' and Film vs. TV|author=Zakarin, Jordan|date=October 19, 2015|work=yahoo.com|access-date=December 28, 2015|archive-date=January 5, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160105145312/https://www.yahoo.com/movies/leonardo-dicaprio-on-fighting-a-1281529422913590.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/leonardo-dicaprio-jennifer-lawrence-joy|title=Leonardo DiCaprio Could Have Starred Opposite Jennifer Lawrence in Joy|author=Miller, Julie|magazine=Vanity Fair|date=December 10, 2015|access-date=December 28, 2015|archive-date=December 22, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222171330/http://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/12/leonardo-dicaprio-jennifer-lawrence-joy|url-status=live}}</ref> Hardy lightly wrestled Iñárritu on set to release tension while during filming.<ref>{{cite web | title=Tom Hardy Really Did Wrestle the Revenant Director on Set | website=[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]] | date=October 30, 2015 | url=https://www.vanityfair.com/hollywood/2015/10/tom-hardy-the-revenant-wrestling }}</ref> During the scene where Hugh and Hawk prayed, both DiCaprio and Lockwood had their eyes closed due to the cold weather condition.<ref>{{Cite magazine |last=Rapkin |first=Mickey |date=December 4, 2017 |title=Inside 'The Revenant' |url=https://www.mensjournal.com/entertainment/inside-the-revenant-leo-dicaprio-on-the-toughest-movie-hes-ever-made-20151211 |access-date=March 21, 2026 |magazine=Men's Journal}}</ref>

Iñárritu had stated that he originally wanted to film chronologically, a process that would have added $7 million to the production budget.<ref name="InSequence"/> Later he confirmed that it was shot in sequence, though Hardy had stated that it was impossible due to weather conditions.<ref name="InarrituDefends"/><ref name="NotInSequence"/> The terrain of the filming locations was not true to the actual Hugh Glass mauling story, which really occurred in modern-day [[South Dakota]].

In July 2015, it was reported that the budget had ballooned from the original $60 million to $95 million, and by the time production wrapped it had reached $135 million.<ref name="TheWrap"/>

===Music===
{{Main|The Revenant (soundtrack){{!}}''The Revenant'' (soundtrack)}}
The [[Film score|musical score]] was composed by Japanese musician [[Ryuichi Sakamoto]] and German electronic musician [[Alva Noto]] with additional music composed by [[Bryce Dessner]].<ref>{{cite web|last1=Henry|first1=Dusty|last2=Geslani|first2=Michelle|title=The National's Bryce Dessner composing soundtrack for Leonardo DiCaprio's The Revenant|url=https://consequence.net/2015/10/the-nationals-bryce-dessner-composing-soundtrack-for-leonardo-dicaprios-the-revenant/|website=[[Consequence of Sound]]|access-date=December 6, 2015|date=October 20, 2015|archive-date=November 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151123213022/http://consequenceofsound.net/2015/10/the-nationals-bryce-dessner-composing-soundtrack-for-leonardo-dicaprios-the-revenant/|url-status=live}}</ref> The main body of the score was recorded at the Seattlemusic Scoring Stage in the Bastyr Chapel in greater Seattle, Washington by musicians of the [[Northwest Sinfonia]]. Sakamoto conducted these sessions. Bryce Dessner's portion of the score was performed by the 25-piece Berlin-based orchestra known as "s t a r g a z e" under conductor [[André de Ridder]].<ref>{{cite web|last1=Gordon|first1=Jeremy|title=The National's Bryce Dessner and Alva Noto Joined Ryuichi Sakamoto on The Revenant Score|url=https://pitchfork.com/news/61726-the-nationals-bryce-dessner-and-alva-noto-joined-ryuichi-sakamoto-on-the-revenant-score/|website=[[Pitchfork Media]]|publisher=[[Condé Nast]]|access-date=December 6, 2015|date=October 21, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151204054608/http://pitchfork.com/news/61726-the-nationals-bryce-dessner-and-alva-noto-joined-ryuichi-sakamoto-on-the-revenant-score/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name="Pitchfork2">{{cite web|last1=Minsker|first1=Evan|title=The National's Bryce Dessner Shares "Imagining Buffalo" From The Revenant Soundtrack|url=https://pitchfork.com/news/62350-the-nationals-bryce-dessner-shares-imagining-buffalo-from-the-revenant-soundtrack/|website=Pitchfork Media|publisher=Condé Nast|access-date=December 6, 2015|date=December 2, 2015|archive-date=December 5, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151205142909/http://pitchfork.com/news/62350-the-nationals-bryce-dessner-shares-imagining-buffalo-from-the-revenant-soundtrack/|url-status=live}}</ref> Additional licensed music includes "Become Ocean", the Pulitzer Prize and Grammy Award-winning work of [[John Luther Adams]] as recorded by the [[Seattle Symphony]] with conductor [[Ludovic Morlot]] and an excerpt of "Jetsun Mila" from French musician and composer [[Éliane Radigue]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.soundtrack.net/movie/the-revenant-2015/|title=The Revenant (2015) - Soundtrack.Net|access-date=February 7, 2016|archive-date=February 2, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160202211633/http://www.soundtrack.net/movie/the-revenant-2015/|url-status=live}}</ref> A soundtrack album was released online on December 25, 2015, and on CD on January 8, 2016. [[Milan Records]] released a vinyl pressing of the soundtrack in April 2016.<ref name="Pitchfork2"/>

The score by Sakamoto and Noto was ruled ineligible for the [[Academy Award]] for [[Academy Award for Best Original Score|Best Original Score]] at the [[88th Academy Awards|2016 Oscars]] as it was deemed that it was "assembled from the music of more than one composer".<ref name="Indiewire">{{cite web|url=http://blogs.indiewire.com/thompsononhollywood/the-revenant-is-not-eligible-to-compete-for-the-oscar-for-original-score-20151216|title=Why 'The Revenant' Was Not Eligible to Compete for the Oscar for Original Score|date=December 19, 2015 |publisher=Indiewire.com|access-date=February 19, 2016|archive-date=March 1, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160301172913/http://blogs.indiewire.com/thompsononhollywood/the-revenant-is-not-eligible-to-compete-for-the-oscar-for-original-score-20151216|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Documentary===
The movie was accompanied by a 44-minute documentary, named ''A World Unseen'', highlighting the process of making the production. ==Themes==
In the ''[[Los Angeles Review of Books]]'', critic [[Wai Chee Dimock]] compared ''The Revenant''{{'}}s themes with those addressed in the literary works of [[James Fenimore Cooper]], particularly ''[[The Last of the Mohicans]]''. Dimock argues that the film reinterprets the concept of "[[half-breed]]s" from a derogatory idea that Cooper despised to an aesthetic way in which to see the world. She compared both works' protagonists—Glass and [[Natty Bumppo|Hawk-eye]]—as literary foils, with Glass living an inversion of the latter's biography and perspective.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://lareviewofbooks.org/essay/half-and-half-inarritu-remixes-james-fenimore-cooper/|title=Half-and-Half: Iñárritu remixes James Fenimore Cooper|first=Wai Chee | last=Dimock|work=[[Los Angeles Review of Books]]|date=February 16, 2016|access-date=October 12, 2016}}</ref>

In the documentary ''A World Unseen'', the director stated that for ''The Revenant''{{'}}s main themes, he revisits the issues and concerns of intense parental and filial relations, which are readily recognizable as a recurrent theme from his previous works. Regarding the theme of revenge, he mentioned that the approach of vengeance in the film needs to be significantly tempered by anyone who would want to see vengeance as either an effective or useful moral to be applied in life. In the end, he said, there can only be a disappointment and lack of fulfillment for anyone who looks to revenge as providing a higher purpose for living or a life-defining purpose.<ref name="20th Century FOX"/><ref name="Gelbart"/>

The film has been discussed in relation to [[Native Americans in film|Indigenous representation]] and as a rare example of a Hollywood depiction of the [[Native American genocide in the United States]]. M. Elise Marubbio states that the film "revises and critiques the grand narratives of [[American exceptionalism]] born of the frontier, [...] undercuts the [Western] genre’s tendency towards [[white privilege]] through multi-lingual narratives, and centers a counter-narrative focused on Indigenous families and women," while also critiquing Iñárritu for relying on stereotypes in his [[Native Americans in film|portrayal of Native women]].<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Marubbio |first1=M. Elise |title=Native American Women as Palimpsestic Apparitions in Alejandro Gonzales Iñárritu’s ''The Revenant'' |journal=European Journal of American Studies |date=2022 |volume=17 |issue=2 |page=1 |doi=10.4000/ejas.18235 |access-date=31 May 2026 |url=https://journals.openedition.org/ejas/18235 }}</ref> Reactions to the film from Native commentators were mixed and complex, with Leo Killsback saying that the film "sets a new bar in filmmaking as it achieves what most films fail to do—it fairly represents Indians,"<ref>{{cite web |last1=Killsback |first1=Leo |title=The Revenant is a Game-Changer |url=https://ictnews.org/archive/the-revenant-is-a-game-changer/ |website=ICT News |access-date=31 May 2026 |date=14 January 2016}}</ref> while Gyasi Ross criticizes the film's centering on Glass's character, saying that the film's story falls into "the same '[[white savior]]' garbage pile that has permeated pretty much any mainstream movie that includes Natives as major characters."<ref>{{cite web |last1=Ross |first1=Gyasi |title=''The Revenant'' Is Ultimately the Same Old White Savior Stuff for Native People |url=https://www.huffpost.com/entry/revenant-review-native-people_b_9005464 |website=Huffington Post |access-date=31 May 2026 |date=18 January 2016}}</ref> Bob Goulais criticized the film for opening with a portrayal of violence committed by Native Americans, saying, "There were plenty of arrows, brutal beatings and even some scalping. ==Historical accuracy==

It has been suggested the real Glass had such a relationship, but there's no firm evidence—and no evidence that he had any children. ... As for the ending, it has been changed in one significant way: in real life, nobody got killed."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2016/jan/20/reel-history-the-revenant-leonardo-dicaprio|title=How historically accurate is The Revenant?|newspaper=[[The Guardian]]|date=January 20, 2016|access-date=December 13, 2016|archive-date=November 30, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161130022522/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2016/jan/20/reel-history-the-revenant-leonardo-dicaprio|url-status=live}}</ref>

The filmmakers made a special point of emphasizing the importance of historical issues of ethnicity approached in the movie and represented in the mixed ethnic background of Hugh Glass's son portrayed in the film (portrayed as half Pawnee by Glass's wife) as relating to his own life and his identification with ethnic concerns. The director has referred to having encountered constant xenophobia and stated that: "These constant and relentless xenophobic (comments) have been widely spread by the media without shame, embraced and cheered by leaders and communities around the US. The foundation of all this is so outrageous that it can easily be minimized as an [[SNL]] [[sketch (comedy)|sketch]], a mere entertainment, a joke ... I debated with myself, if I should bring up this uncomfortable subject tonight but in light of the constant and relentless xenophobic comments that have been expressed recently against my Mexican fellows, it is inevitable."<ref>{{cite news|last1=Evans|first1=Greg|title='The Revenant' Director Alejandro González Iñárritu Says "Xenophobic" Rhetoric Is No Joke|url=https://deadline.com/2015/11/alejandro-gonzalez-inarritu-saturday-night-live-the-revenant-1201614717/|access-date=May 1, 2016|website=Deadline Hollywood|date=November 8, 2015|language=en-US|archive-date=May 10, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160510150451/http://deadline.com/2015/11/alejandro-gonzalez-inarritu-saturday-night-live-the-revenant-1201614717/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Bruce Bradley of ''[[True West Magazine]]'' pointed out several anachronisms hinted in the film such as John Fitzgerald talking about his father having friends in the [[Texas Ranger Division|Texas Rangers]] and about going to Texas and joining the army himself. However, in 1823, Texas was still [[Mexican Texas|part of Mexico]] and therefore would not have had an American army, let alone the Texas Rangers. Another anachronism involved Hugh Glass discovering a mountain of buffalo skulls while working his way to Fort Kiowa despite the buffalo slaughter not occurring until the 1870s.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Bradley |first1=Bruce |title=13 Major Facts The Revenant Got Wrong |url=https://truewestmagazine.com/article/13-major-facts-the-revenant-got-wrong/ |website=True West Magazine |access-date=January 3, 2023}}</ref>

==Reception==
===Box office===

Reviewers cited in a ''[[CBS News]]'' survey of critics highly praised DiCaprio's performance, referring to it as an "astonishing testament to his commitment to a role" and as an "anchoring performance of ferocious 200 percent commitment".<ref name=MoraskiCBS_151225>{{cite web | author = Moraski, Lauren | date = December 25, 2015 | title = "The Revenant" Reviews: What Critics Are Saying About Leonardo DiCaprio's Latest Film | url = https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-revenant-reviews-what-critics-are-saying-about-leonardo-dicaprio-latest-movie/ | access-date = December 28, 2015 | work = CBS News | archive-date = December 27, 2015 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20151227201455/http://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-revenant-reviews-what-critics-are-saying-about-leonardo-dicaprio-latest-movie/ | url-status = live }}</ref> [[Peter Travers]] of ''[[Rolling Stone]]'' called DiCaprio's acting "a virtuoso performance, thrilling in its brute force and silent eloquence".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-revenant-20151222?page=2|title=Page 2 of 'The Revenant' Movie Review - Rolling Stone|first=Peter|last=Travers|date=December 22, 2015|magazine=Rolling Stone|access-date=December 28, 2015|archive-date=February 28, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180228223852/https://www.rollingstone.com/movies/reviews/the-revenant-20151222?page=2|url-status=dead}}</ref> Writing for ''[[Vulture (website)|Vulture]]'', [[David Edelstein]] called the film a "tour de force" and "[b]leak as hell but considerably more beautiful", but noted it had "traditional masculinity instead of a search for what illuminates man's inhumanity to man".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Edelstein |first=David |date=2015-12-11 |title=The Revenant Is a Brutal Feat of Strength |url=https://www.vulture.com/2015/12/movie-review-the-revenant.html |access-date=2026-03-22 |website=Vulture |language=en}}</ref>

[[Justin Chang]] of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' wrote Iñárritu "increasingly succumbs to the air of grim overdetermination that has marred much of [his] past work" and it was "an imposing vision... but also an inflated and emotionally stunted one".<ref name=MoraskiCBS_151225/> Stephanie Zacharek, writing for ''Time'' magazine, gave a positive review to the film stating: "Inarritu may have fashioned ''The Revenant'' as the ultimate endurance test, but as Glass, DiCaprio simply endures. Bon appetit."<ref name="Stephanie Zacharek 2016, p54"/> [[Richard Brody]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' was critical of the film, and said that [[Emmanuel Lubezki]]'s images were mere "pictorial ornament[s] to [[Alejandro G. Iñárritu]]'s bland theatrical stagings".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Gonzalez |first=Ed |date=2016-02-18 |title=Oscar 2016 Winner Predictions: Cinematography |url=https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/oscar-2016-winner-predictions-cinematography/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190307025639/https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/oscar-2016-winner-predictions-cinematography/ |archive-date=March 7, 2019 |access-date=2026-03-22 |website=Slant Magazine |language=en-US}}</ref> A critic for ''[[Santa Barbara Independent]]'' considered the remake of ''[[Man in the Wilderness]]'' (1971) to be too much slow paced, leaving the audience cold.<ref name="Slow-Paced Suffering"/>

===Accolades===
{{main|List of accolades received by The Revenant{{!}}List of accolades received by ''The Revenant''}}
[[File:Leonardo DiCaprio visited Goddard Saturday to discuss Earth science with Piers Sellers (26105091624) cropped.jpg|thumb|[[Leonardo DiCaprio]]'s performance was widely acclaimed and ultimately won him the [[Academy Award for Best Actor]], the first of his career after five previous nominations.]]
''The Revenant'' has received numerous award nominations and wins, particularly for DiCaprio's performance, Iñárritu's direction and Lubezki's cinematography. At the [[88th Academy Awards|88th Annual Academy Awards]], Iñárritu won [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]] for the second consecutive time, [[Emmanuel Lubezki]] won [[Academy Award for Best Cinematography|Best Cinematography]] for the third consecutive time, and DiCaprio won his first award for [[Academy Award for Best Actor|Best Actor]]. Hardy lost the award for [[Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor|Best Supporting Actor]] to [[Mark Rylance]] for ''[[Bridge of Spies (film)|Bridge of Spies]]'', and the film itself lost [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] to ''[[Spotlight (film)|Spotlight]]''.<ref name="oscar-nominations-2016">{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/oscar-nominations-2016-full-list-855670|title=Oscar Nominations: The Complete List|work=THR|date=January 14, 2016|access-date=August 28, 2016|archive-date=September 12, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190912213503/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/oscars-nominations-complete-list-855398|url-status=live}}</ref>

At the [[73rd Golden Globe Awards]], it won three awards: [[Best Motion Picture – Drama]], [[Golden Globe Award for Best Director|Best Director]], and [[Golden Globe Award for Best Actor – Motion Picture Drama|Best Actor – Drama]].

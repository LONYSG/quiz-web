'''''Pan's Labyrinth''''' ({{langx|es|'''El laberinto del fauno'''|lit=The Labyrinth of the Faun|links=no}}) is a 2006 Spanish-language [[dark fantasy]] film<ref>{{Cite book|title=Future Folk Horror: Contemporary Anxieties and Possible Futures|editor-first=Simon|editor-last=Bacon|chapter=A Multi-contextual Analysis of the Future of Folk Horror in Guillermo del Toro's ''Pans Labyrinth''|first=John R.|last=Meyers|location=Lanham|year=2023|publisher=[[Lexington Books]]|chapter-url=https://books.google.com/books?id=sEHLEAAAQBAJ&pg=PA175|page=175|isbn=9781666921243 |access-date=30 October 2023|archive-date=11 November 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231111072958/https://books.google.com/books?id=sEHLEAAAQBAJ&pg=PA175|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Quarterly |first=Film |date=2007-06-01 |title=Pan's Labyrinth |url=https://filmquarterly.org/2007/06/01/pans-labyrinth/ |access-date=2025-01-03 |website=Film Quarterly |language=en-US}}</ref> produced, written, and directed by [[Guillermo del Toro]]. The film stars [[Ivana Baquero]], [[Sergi López (actor)|Sergi López]], [[Maribel Verdú]], [[Doug Jones (actor)|Doug Jones]], and [[Ariadna Gil]]. The film is an international co-production between Spain and Mexico.<ref name="mcu">(78% Spanish production, 22% Mexican production) {{cite web|url=http://www.mcu.es/comun/bases/cine/Anuarios/2006/P111905.pdf|title=EL LABERINTO DEL FAUNO|access-date=21 June 2015|archive-date=4 March 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160304090106/http://www.mcu.es/comun/bases/cine/Anuarios/2006/P111905.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref>

The story takes place in [[Francoist Spain]] in the summer of 1944. The narrative intertwines this real world with a mythical world centered on an overgrown, abandoned [[labyrinth]] and a mysterious [[faun]] with whom the protagonist, Ofelia, interacts. Ofelia's stepfather, Captain Vidal, hunts down the [[Spanish Maquis]] who resist the Francoist regime, while Ofelia's pregnant mother grows increasingly ill. Ofelia meets several strange and magical creatures who become central to her story, leading her through the trials of the old labyrinth garden. The film employs make-up, [[animatronics]], and [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]] effects to bring life to its creatures.

Del Toro stated that he considers the story to be a [[parable]],<ref>{{cite web | url = https://www.austinchronicle.com/screens/2007-01-12/435204/| title = Once Upon a Time in Spain | first = Marc | last = Savlov | date = 2007-01-12 | work = Austin Chronicle | quote = Pan's Labyrinth tries to be a parable, and I believe that the parable works across time–it is as pertinent today as it would be in 1944 in Spain.}}</ref> influenced by [[fairy tales]]. It addresses and continues themes related to his 2001 film ''[[The Devil's Backbone]]'',<ref name="SF">{{cite news |last=Spelling |first=Ian |date=25 December 2006 |title=Guillermo Del Toro and Ivana Baquero escape from a civil war into the fairytale land of ''Pan's Labyrinth'' |work=[[Science Fiction Weekly]] |url=http://www.scifi.com/sfw/interviews/sfw14471.html |url-status=dead |access-date=27 January 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080609075453/http://www.scifi.com/sfw/interviews/sfw14471.html |archive-date=9 June 2008 |df=dmy}}</ref> to which ''Pan's Labyrinth'' is a [[spiritual successor]], according to del Toro in his [[Audio commentary|director's DVD commentary]]. 

''Pan's Labyrinth'' premiered on 27 May 2006 at the [[2006 Cannes Film Festival|Cannes Film Festival]], where it received a 22-minute-long [[standing ovation]], the longest in the festival's history. The film was theatrically released by [[Warner Bros. Pictures]] in Spain on 11 October and in Mexico on 20 October. It garnered widespread critical acclaim, with praise towards its visual and makeup effects, direction, screenplay, cinematography, musical score, set design, and cast performances. It grossed $83 million at the worldwide box office and won numerous awards, including three [[Academy Awards]], three [[BAFTA Award]]s including [[BAFTA Award for Best Film Not in the English Language|Best Film Not in the English Language]], the [[Ariel Award for Best Picture]], and the [[Hugo Award for Best Dramatic Presentation#Long Form|Hugo Award for Best Dramatic Presentation, Long Form]]. ''Pan's Labyrinth'' has since been considered to be Del Toro's ''[[Masterpiece|magnum opus]]'', one of the best fantasy films ever made, as well as one of the greatest films of the 2000s and the 21st century.<ref>{{cite news | url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/oct/21/pans-labyrinth-toro-fantasy | title=Pan's Labyrinth: No 24 best sci-fi and fantasy film of all time | newspaper=The Guardian | date=21 October 2010 | last1=O'Neill | first1=Phelim | access-date=18 June 2023 | archive-date=18 June 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230618225320/https://www.theguardian.com/film/2010/oct/21/pans-labyrinth-toro-fantasy | url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://screenrant.com/pans-labyrinth-guillermo-del-toro-best-movie/ | title=Pan's Labyrinth is Still Guillermo del Toro's Best Movie | website=[[Screen Rant]] | date=2 January 2017 | access-date=18 June 2023 | archive-date=18 June 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230618225320/https://screenrant.com/pans-labyrinth-guillermo-del-toro-best-movie/ | url-status=live |last=Canfield|first=Jared}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://variety.com/lists/guillermo-del-toro-films-ranked-worst-best/ | title=Guillermo del Toro Films Ranked — from Worst to Best | date=17 December 2021 | access-date=18 June 2023 | archive-date=18 June 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230618225314/https://variety.com/lists/guillermo-del-toro-films-ranked-worst-best/ | url-status=live |website=Variety|last=Nordine|first=Michael}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-movies-21st-century/ | title=Hollywood Reporter Critics Pick the 50 Best Films of the 21st Century (So Far) | website=[[The Hollywood Reporter]] | date=6 April 2023 | access-date=18 June 2023 | archive-date=18 June 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230618235113/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/lists/best-movies-21st-century | url-status=live }}</ref>

A sequel, titled ''3993'', was conceived by del Toro but ultimately cancelled. A novelization by del Toro and [[Cornelia Funke]] was published in 2019.

==Plot==
{{long plot|section|date=October 2026}}
In a [[fairy tale]], Princess Moanna, daughter of the king of the [[underworld]], visits the human world, where the sunlight blinds her and erases her memory. She becomes mortal and eventually dies. The king believes that eventually her spirit will return to the underworld, so he builds labyrinths around the world which act as portals, in preparation for her return.

In 1944 [[Francoist Spain]], ten-year-old Ofelia travels with her pregnant mother Carmen to meet Captain Vidal, her new stepfather. Vidal, a [[Civil Guard (Spain)|Civil Guard]] officer of the [[Armed Police Corps]] and devotee of [[Falangism]], has been assigned to hunt down the [[Spanish Maquis]]. A [[Phasmatodea|large stick insect]], which Ofelia believes to be a fairy, leads Ofelia into an ancient stone [[labyrinth]], but she is stopped by Vidal's housekeeper Mercedes, who is secretly supporting her brother Pedro and other members of the Maquis. That night, the insect appears in Ofelia's bedroom, where it transforms into a fairy and leads her through the labyrinth. There, she meets a [[faun]], who believes she is the [[reincarnation]] of Princess Moanna. He gives her a book and tells her she will find in it three tasks to complete in order for her to acquire immortality and return to her kingdom. During a dinner with several important figures, Vidal announces that he will stockpile food and supplies in the granary in order to weaken the Maquis and their collaborators. The local population can only access supplies through [[ration cards]]. Vidal secures the granary with a [[padlock]] which Mercedes falsely assures him has only one key.

Ofelia completes the first task — retrieving a key from the belly of a giant toad — but becomes worried about her mother, whose condition is worsening. The faun gives Ofelia a [[mandrake]] root to place under Carmen's bed and regularly supply with blood, which seems to ease Carmen's illness. Accompanied by three fairy guides and equipped with a piece of magic chalk, Ofelia completes the second task — retrieving a dagger from the lair of the Pale Man, a child-eating monster. Despite the faun's warning not to consume anything there, she gives in to her temptation and eats two grapes, awakening the Pale Man. He devours two of the fairies but Ofelia escapes. Infuriated at her disobedience, the faun refuses to give Ofelia the third task and abandons her.

Vidal's ruthlessness becomes more evident. He interrogates and kills two local farmers (who only hunted rabbits). The Maquis attack nearby as a distraction, and steal supplies from the granary using a duplicate key. Vidal notices that the lock was opened. Vidal interrogates and [[tortures]] a captive. Doctor Ferreiro then [[Euthanasia|euthanises]] the captive and Vidal kills Ferreiro. He later catches Ofelia tending to the mandrake root, and Carmen destroys the root as he directs. She immediately develops painful contractions and dies giving birth to Vidal's son.

Vidal grows suspicious of Mercedes, due to the unlocked door. Mercedes tries to escape with Ofelia, but they are caught. Ofelia is locked in her bedroom, while Mercedes is taken to be interrogated. Mercedes frees herself, and slashes Vidal’s mouth open before escaping to rejoin the Maquis.

The faun, having changed his mind, returns and tells her to bring her newborn brother into the labyrinth as a prerequisite for the third task. Ofelia with the baby flee into the labyrinth, pursued by Vidal, as the Maquis launch an attack on a Civil Guard outpost. Ofelia meets the faun, who says be needs to draw  a tiny amount of the baby's blood with the dagger, explaining that completing the third task and opening the portal to the [[underworld]] requires the blood of an innocent. Ofelia says she would rather not return than harm her brother. Vidal finds her talking to the faun, whom he cannot see. Vidal takes the baby from Ofelia's arms before fatally shooting her.

Vidal returns to the labyrinth's entrance with the baby, where they are surrounded by the Maquis, including Mercedes and Pedro. Knowing that he will be killed, he hands the baby to Mercedes, asking that his son be told the time of his father's death. Mercedes replies that his son will not even know his name, before Vidal is shot dead. Mercedes enters the labyrinth and comforts the dying Ofelia. Drops of Ofelia's blood fall down the centre of the spiral stone staircase onto an altar. 

Ofelia, well dressed and uninjured, appears in a golden throne room. The King of the underworld tells her that, by choosing to spill her own innocent blood rather than that of another, she passed the final test. The faun praises Ofelia for her choice, addressing her as "Your Highness". Her mother, the Queen of the underworld, invites Ofelia to sit next to her father and rule at his side as the people around them applaud. Back in the stone labyrinth, Ofelia smiles as she dies.

The epilogue concludes the tale of Princess Moanna, stating that she ruled the underworld with kindness and justice for many centuries, and left little traces of her time in the human realm, "visible only to those who know where to look."

Del Toro said he was nervous about casting the lead role, and that finding the 10-year-old Spanish actress was purely accidental. (The film was shot from June–October 2005, when she was 11.) "The character I wrote was initially younger, about 8 or 9, and Ivana came in and she was a little older than the character, with this curly hair which I never imagined the girl having. ==Production==

===Influences===
The original Spanish title ''El laberinto del fauno'' refers to the [[Faun|fauns]] of [[Roman mythology]], while the English, [[German language|German]] and [[French language|French]] titles refer specifically to the faun-like [[Greek mythology|Greek deity]] [[Pan (mythology)|Pan]]. However, del Toro has stated that the faun in the film is not Pan.<ref name="SF"/>  The idea for ''Pan's Labyrinth'' came from [[Guillermo del Toro]]'s notebooks, which he says are filled with "doodles, ideas, drawings and plot bits". He had been keeping these notebooks for twenty years. At one point during production, he left the notebook in a taxi in [[London]] and was distraught, but the cabbie returned it to him two days later. Though he originally wrote a story about a pregnant woman who falls in love with a faun,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.ebar.com/arts/art_article.php?sec=film&article=288|access-date=20 July 2007|title=The world of the labyrinth|publisher=Bay Area Reporte|date=4 January 2007|last=Lamble|first=David|archive-date=28 September 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070928051357/http://www.ebar.com/arts/art_article.php?sec=film&article=288|url-status=dead}}</ref> Sergi López said that del Toro described the final version of the plot a year and a half before filming. López said that "for two hours and a half he explained to me all the movie, but with all the details, it was incredible, and when he finished I said, 'You have a script?' He said, 'No, nothing is written'". López agreed to act in the movie and received the script one year later; he said that "it was exactly the same, it was incredible. In his little head he had all the history with a lot of little detail, a lot of characters, like now when you look at the movie, it was exactly what he had in his head".<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.canmag.com/nw/6255-sergi-lopez-pans-labyrinth-interview|title=Sergi Lopez on ''Pan's Labyrinth''|access-date=27 January 2007|date=2 January 2007|publisher=CanMag|last=Topel|first=Fred|archive-date=24 January 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070124023210/http://www.canmag.com/nw/6255-sergi-lopez-pans-labyrinth-interview|url-status=usurped}}</ref>

Del Toro got the idea of the faun from childhood experiences with "[[lucid dream]]ing". He stated on ''[[Charlie Rose (talk show)|Charlie Rose]]'' that every midnight, he would wake up, and a faun would gradually step out from behind the grandfather's clock.<ref>''Pan's Labyrinth'' DVD, U.S.</ref> Originally, the faun was supposed to be a classic half-man, half-goat faun fraught with beauty. But in the end, the faun was altered into a goat-faced creature almost completely made out of earth, moss, vines, and tree bark. He became a mysterious, semi-suspicious relic who gave both the impression of trustworthiness and many signs that warn someone to never confide in him at all.

Del Toro has said the film has strong connections in theme to ''[[The Devil's Backbone]]'' and should be seen as an informal sequel dealing with some of the issues raised there. [[Fernando Tielve]] and Íñigo Garcés, who played the protagonists of ''The Devil's Backbone'', make cameo appearances as unnamed guerrilla soldiers in ''Pan's Labyrinth''. Some of the other works he drew on for inspiration include [[Lewis Carroll]]'s ''Alice'' books, [[Jorge Luis Borges]]' ''[[Ficciones]]'', [[Arthur Machen]]'s ''[[The Great God Pan]]'' and ''[[The White People]]'', [[Edward Plunkett, 18th Baron Dunsany|Lord Dunsany]]'s ''The Blessing of Pan'', [[Algernon Blackwood]]'s ''Pan's Garden'' and [[Francisco Goya]]'s works. In 2004, del Toro said: "''Pan'' is an original story. Some of my favourite writers (Borges, Blackwood, Machen, Dunsany) have explored the figure of the god Pan and the symbol of the labyrinth. These are things that I find very compelling and I am trying to mix them and play with them."<ref>{{Cite web |date=24 November 2004 |title=View topic - Answers Archive (August 2004 to Present) |url=http://www.deltorofilms.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=28 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927050559/http://www.deltorofilms.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=28 |archive-date=2007-09-27 |access-date=2026-06-14 |website=DelToroFilms.com}}</ref> It was also influenced by the illustrations of [[Arthur Rackham]].<ref name="panspeople">{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/film/2006/nov/17/2|title=Pan's people|date=17 November 2006|access-date=9 July 2007|work=[[The Guardian]]|location=London|first=Guillermo|last=del Toro|archive-date=29 August 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070829040243/http://arts.guardian.co.uk/filmandmusic/story/0,,1949245,00.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

Del Toro wanted to include a fairy tale about a dragon for Ofelia to narrate to her unborn brother. The tale involved the dragon, named Varanium Silex, who guarded a mountain surrounded by thorns, but at its peak is a blue rose that can grant immortality. The dragon and the thorns ward off many men though, who decide it is better to avoid pain than to be given immortality. Although the scene was thematically important, it was cut short for budget reasons.<ref>{{cite web|author=Guillermo del Toro|author-link=Guillermo del Toro|title=Svnt Dracones |publisher=[[John Howe (illustrator)|John Howe]]'s official website |date=15 October 2008 |url=http://www.john-howe.com/blog/2008/10/16/forging-more-dragons/ |access-date=15 October 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140503160317/http://www.john-howe.com/blog/2008/10/16/forging-more-dragons/ |archive-date=3 May 2014 |url-status=dead |df=dmy }}</ref>

There are differing ideas about the film's religious influences. Del Toro himself has said that he considers ''Pan's Labyrinth'' "a truly profane film, a layman's riff on Catholic dogma", but that his friend [[Alejandro González Iñárritu]] described it as "a truly Catholic film". Del Toro's explanation is "once a Catholic, always a Catholic," however he also admits that the Pale Man's preference for children rather than the feast in front of him is intended as a criticism of the Catholic Church.<ref>Guillermo Del Toro, Pan's Labyrinth: Inside the Creation of a Modern Fairy Tale (Harper Design, 2016)</ref> Additionally, the priest's words during the torture scene were taken as a direct quote from a priest who offered communion to political prisoners during the Spanish Civil War: "Remember my sons, you should confess what you know because God doesn't care what happens to your bodies; He already saved your souls."<ref>{{Cite book |last=Toro |first=Guillermo del |url=https://books.google.com/books/about/Guillermo_Del_Toro_s_Pan_s_Labyrinth.html?id=vTxmCwAAQBAJ&source=kp_book_description |title=Guillermo del Toro's Pan's Labyrinth: Inside the Creation of a Modern Fairy Tale |last2=Nunziata |first2=Nick |last3=Vaz |first3=Mark Cotta |date=2017-05-16 |publisher=HarperCollins |isbn=978-0-06-243390-9 |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://intersecting.weebly.com/blog/pans-labyrinth-2006|title=Pan's Labyrinth|website=Intersecting|access-date=4 May 2017|archive-date=24 February 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170224052205/http://intersecting.weebly.com/blog/pans-labyrinth-2006|url-status=live|date=July 18, 2015}}</ref>

In regard to whether or not the fantasy underworld was real or a product of Ofelia's imagination, del Toro stated in an interview that, while he believes it is real, the movie "should tell something different to everyone. It should be a matter of personal discussion". He then mentioned there were several clues in the movie indicating the underworld was indeed real.<ref name=pansinterview/>

The film was shot in a [[scots pine]] forest situated in the [[Sierra de Guadarrama|Guadarrama mountain range]], Central [[Spain]]. [[Guillermo Navarro]], the [[Cinematographer|director of photography]], said that "after doing work in Hollywood on other movies and with other directors, working in our original language in different scenery brings me back to the original reasons I wanted to make movies, which is basically to tell stories with complete freedom and to let the visuals really contribute to the telling of the story".<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.ascmag.com/magazine_dynamic/January2007/PansLabyrinth/page1.php |title=Fear and Fantasy |access-date=17 June 2007 |date=January 2007 |publisher=[[American Cinematographer]] |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070511033220/http://www.ascmag.com/magazine_dynamic/January2007/PansLabyrinth/page1.php |archive-date=11 May 2007 |url-status=dead |df=dmy |last=Calhoun|first=John}}</ref>

The pale man's eyes on his hands are a feature shared by the Japanese mythological monster the [[Tenome]] (a name which means "hand eyes").

===Effects===
''Pan's Labyrinth'' employs some [[computer-generated imagery]] in its effects, but it mostly uses complex make-up and [[animatronics]]. The giant toad was inspired by ''[[The Maze (1953 film)|The Maze]]''. Del Toro himself performed the noises. The mandrake root is a combination of animatronics and CGI. Del Toro wanted the fairies "to look like little monkeys, like dirty fairies", but the animation company had the idea to give them wings made of leaves.<ref name="USAToday">{{cite news|url=https://www.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2007-01-02-pans-labyrinth-main_x.htm|access-date=20 July 2007|date=14 March 2007|title=Surprises lurk inside ''Pan's Labyrinth''|work=[[USA Today]]|last=Wloszczyna|first=Susan|archive-date=23 May 2011|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110523100746/http://www.usatoday.com/life/movies/news/2007-01-02-pans-labyrinth-main_x.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Jones spent an average of five hours sitting in the makeup chair as his team of [[David Martí]], [[Montse Ribé]] and Xavi Bastida applied the makeup for the Faun, which was mostly [[latex]] foam. The last piece to be applied was the pair of horns, which weighed ten pounds and were extremely tiring to wear. The legs were a unique design, with Jones standing on 20-cm-high lifts (8&nbsp;in), and the legs of the Faun attached to his own. His lower leg was eventually digitally erased in post-production. The Faun's flapping ears and blinking eyes were remotely operated by David Martí and Xavi Bastida from DDT {{Not a typo|Efectos}} Especiales while on set. Del Toro told Jones to "go rock star... like a glam rocker. But less [[David Bowie]], more [[Mick Jagger]]".<ref name="USAToday"/>

The Captain's room, as shown in the scene where Captain Vidal is shaving, is supposed to resemble his father's watch, which del Toro says represents his troubled mind.{{citation needed|date=August 2020}}

A bout of weight loss on del Toro's part inspired the physical appearance of the saggy-skinned Pale Man.<ref>Cruz, Gilbert. "10 Questions: Filmmaker Guillermo del Toro on the politics of horror movies, living in self-imposed exile and owning a man cave". ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' magazine. 5 September 2011. page 80.</ref> In order to see while performing the part, Doug Jones had to look out of the character's nostrils, and its legs were attached to Jones over the green leotard which he wore.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://chud.com/interview-doug-jones-pans-labyrinth/|access-date=27 May 2019|title=INTERVIEW: DOUG JONES (PAN'S LABYRINTH)|date=18 February 2007|last=Fischer|first=Russ|archive-date=27 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190527051220/https://chud.com/interview-doug-jones-pans-labyrinth/|url-status=live|work=Chud}}</ref>

===Subtitles===
The film uses subtitles for its translation into other languages, including English. Del Toro wrote them himself, because he was disappointed with the subtitles of his previous Spanish-language film, ''[[The Devil's Backbone]]''. In an interview, he said that the subtitles of ''The Devil's Backbone'' were "for the thinking impaired" and "incredibly bad". He spent a month working with two other people, and said that he did not want it to "feel like... watching a subtitled film".<ref>{{Cite web|author=World Entertainment News Network|date=2007-02-13|title=Labyrinth Director Wrote His Own Subtitles|url=https://www.contactmusic.com/news/labyrinth-director-wrote-his-own-subtitles_1021894|access-date=2023-02-16|website=Contactmusic.com|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080520082522/http://www.contactmusic.com/news.nsf/article/labyrinth%20director%20wrote%20his%20own%20subtitles_1021894 |archive-date=20 May 2008|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Marketing ==
After ''[[Mimic (film)|Mimic]]'', Del Toro wanted his outreach to go beyond Hollywood film-making as he felt was too secure and restrictive what he was "allowed" to make from the perspective of producers. Though American producers offered to double the budget to make ''Pan's Labyrinth'' in English and shot in the US, Del Toro still refused, stating the film would have become a "[[Eurotrash (term)|Euro-trash]] production,"<ref>{{Cite news |last=Rodriguez |first=Rene |date=2007-01-16 |title=Director keeps Hollywood out of "Pan's Labyrinth" |url=https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/director-keeps-hollywood-out-of-pans-labyrinth/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240526023850/https://www.seattletimes.com/entertainment/director-keeps-hollywood-out-of-pans-labyrinth/ |archive-date=2024-05-26 |access-date=2025-06-23 |work=The Seattle Times |language=en-US}}</ref> and a result it ended up as an independent project. Though challenging to promote outside of Spanish speaking countries with a budget of only [[United States dollar|USD]] 19 million, it earned $30 million within just two months of its U.S. opening to become the highest grossing Spanish-language film ever released in the U.S.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2007/film/news/pan-s-breaks-out-of-art-niche-1117960060/|title=‘Pan’s’ breaks out of art niche|first=Ian|last=Mohr|date=February 24, 2007|website=Variety}}</ref>

[[Bob Berney]], the president of [[Picturehouse (company)|Picturehouse]], was in charge of promotional material for distribution in the US. He commented the target audiences were young males through TV commercials and later a panel discussion at [[San Diego Comic-Con]]<ref>{{Cite news |title=Comic-Con: Jason Statham, Rosario Dawson, and more |url=https://ew.com/article/2006/07/21/comiccon/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240518232807/https://ew.com/article/2006/07/21/comiccon/ |archive-date=2024-05-18 |access-date=2025-06-23 |work=EW.com |language=en|last=Nisha|first=Gopalan|date=21 July 2006}}</ref> and Latino communities through TV spots, posters, newspaper ads in Spanish, and mainstream radio stations such as [[KROQ-FM|KROQ]].<ref>{{Cite news |first=Gina|last=McIntyre |date=2007-02-06 |title=Del Toro's 'Labyrinth' gains Oscar momentum |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/del-toros-labyrinth-gains-oscar-129533/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250118074837/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/del-toros-labyrinth-gains-oscar-129533/ |archive-date=2025-01-18 |access-date=2025-06-23 |work=The Hollywood Reporter |language=en-US}}</ref>

Pictures]] acquiring the Spanish and Latin American distribution rights to the film.<ref>{{cite news|last=de la Fuente|first=Anna Marie|title='Labyrinth' snakes through WB Int'l|url=https://variety.com/2005/film/markets-festivals/labyrinth-snakes-through-wb-int-l-1117923019/|date= 17 May 2005|work=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|access-date=12 October 2025}}</ref> In January 2006, [[Picturehouse (company)|Picturehouse]] had acquired the North American distribution rights to the film for $6 million.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://variety.com/2006/film/markets-festivals/picturehouse-in-labyrinth-1117936689/|title=Picturehouse in 'Labyrinth'|first=Anna Marie de la|last=Fuente|date=24 January 2006|website=Variety}}</ref> Its first premiere in an English-speaking country was at the [[London FrightFest Film Festival]] on 25 August 2006. On 24 November 2006 it had its first general English release in the United Kingdom; that month it was also released in France, [[Serbia]], [[Belgium]], Italy, Russia, [[Singapore]] and South Korea. The film was released in the United States on 15 May 2007 from [[New Line Home Entertainment]] in both single-disc and double-disc special edition versions, featuring an additional [[DTS (sound system)|DTS-ES]] audio track not present on the UK version. === Commercial performance ===
{{Anchor|Box office}}
During its limited first three weeks at the United States box office, the film made $5.4 million. As of 2021, it has grossed $37.6 million in North America and $46.2 million in other territories, for a total of $83.9 million worldwide.<ref name="bom" /> In Spain, it grossed almost $12 million, and it is the fifth highest grossing foreign film in the United States.<ref name="bom" />

In the United States, it has generated $55 million from its DVD sales and rentals.<ref name="bom" /><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.the-numbers.com/dvd/charts/annual/2007.php|title=Top-Selling DVD Titles in the United States 2007|website=The Numbers|access-date=4 May 2017|archive-date=5 October 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131005095235/http://www.the-numbers.com/dvd/charts/annual/2007.php|url-status=live}}</ref>

In the United Kingdom, it was 2011's eighth best-selling foreign-language film on physical [[home video]] formats.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2012 |title=BFI Statistical Yearbook 2012 |pages=116 |publisher=[[British Film Institute]] (BFI) |url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/uk-film-council-statistical-yearbook-2010.pdf |access-date=21 April 2022 |archive-date=23 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220423012731/https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/uk-film-council-statistical-yearbook-2010.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> It was later the UK's tenth best-selling foreign-language film on physical home video formats in 2012.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2013 |title=BFI Statistical Yearbook 2013 |pages=138 |publisher=[[British Film Institute]] (BFI) |url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-statistical-yearbook-2013.pdf |access-date=23 April 2022 |archive-date=1 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220501001158/https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-statistical-yearbook-2013.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> On UK television, it was 2013's second most-watched foreign-language film, with 200,700 viewers on [[Channel 4]].<ref>{{Cite report |url=https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-statistical-yearbook-2014.pdf |title=Statistical Yearbook 2014 |date=2014 |publisher=[[British Film Institute]] (BFI) |pages=141 |access-date=25 April 2022 |url-status=dead |archive-date=1 May 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220501005443/https://www2.bfi.org.uk/sites/bfi.org.uk/files/downloads/bfi-statistical-yearbook-2014.pdf }}</ref>

===Comparisons to other films===
====Spanish films====
Del Toro himself has indicated similarities with ''[[The Spirit of the Beehive]]'', filmed in Francoist Spain, which juxtaposes issues related to the Civil War with horror film.<ref name="timeout" /><ref name="outsidetheframe">{{cite web|url=http://www.thephoenix.com/OutsideTheFrame/PermaLink.aspx?guid=8ae649e6-a17f-478b-993a-a6f0f791a936 |title=Outside The Frame: Guillermo Del Toro Interview Part 1 |access-date=28 January 2007 |date=11 January 2007 |publisher=ThePhoenix.com |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080119233841/http://thephoenix.com/OutsideTheFrame/PermaLink.aspx?guid=8ae649e6-a17f-478b-993a-a6f0f791a936 |archive-date=19 January 2008 |df=dmy }}</ref>
At least one critic has made a connection to a second Spanish film, ''[[Cría Cuervos]]'' (1975, [[Carlos Saura]]), again made while Franco was still in power. Doug Cummings (Film Journey 2007) identifies the connection between ''Cria Cuervos'', ''Spirit of the Beehive'' and ''Pan's Labyrinth'': "Critics have been summarily referencing ''Spirit of the Beehive'' (1973) in reviews of ''Pan's Labyrinth'', but Saura's film–at once a sister work to Erice's classic in theme, tone, even shared actress ([[Ana Torrent]])–is no less rich a reference point."<ref name="film journey">{{cite web|last=Cummings |first=Doug |title=Cria cuervos |url=http://www.filmjourney.org/2007/03/31/cria-cuervos/#more-619 |archive-date=14 April 2013 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130414182044/http://www.filmjourney.org/2007/03/31/cria-cuervos/%23more-619 |url-status=dead|work=Film Journey |access-date=23 August 2010 |date=31 March 2007 }}</ref>

====Non-Spanish films====
In a 2007 interview, del Toro noted the striking similarities between his film and [[Walt Disney Pictures]]' ''[[The Chronicles of Narnia (film series)|The Chronicles of Narnia]]'': both films are set around the same time, have similar child-age principal characters, mythic creatures (particularly the fauns), and themes of "disobedience and choice". Says del Toro: "This is my version of that universe, not only 'Narnia', but that universe of children's literature."<ref name="del-toro-narnia">{{cite news|url=http://www.starbeacon.com/heat/local_story_363121523|archive-url=https://archive.today/20120911011135/http://www.starbeacon.com/heat/local_story_363121523|url-status=dead|archive-date=11 September 2012|last=Nuckols|first=Ben|title=Del Toro crafts a harrowing fairy tale|access-date=28 March 2007|date=29 December 2006|publisher=[[Star Beacon]]}}</ref> In fact, del Toro was asked to direct ''[[The Chronicles of Narnia: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe]]'' but turned it down for ''Pan's Labyrinth''.<ref name="del-toro-narnia" />
In addition to ''Narnia'', ''Pan's Labyrinth'' has also been compared to films such as ''[[Labyrinth (1986 film)|Labyrinth]]'', ''[[MirrorMask]]'', ''[[Spirited Away]]'' and ''[[Bridge to Terabithia (2007 film)|Bridge to Terabithia]]''.<ref name="timeout">{{cite web|first=Ben|last=Walters|date=21–28 November 2006|url=https://www.timeout.com/film/83674.html|title=Pan's Labyrinth Film Review|work=[[Time Out (magazine)|Time Out]]|access-date=24 January 2007|archive-date=21 January 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070121173319/http://www.timeout.com/film/83674.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.canmag.com/nw/6778-bridge-terabithia-review|last=Topel|first=Fred|date=15 February 2007|title=Bridge to Terabithia a Pan's Labyrinth for Kids|access-date=23 February 2007|archive-date=22 February 2007|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070222035036/http://www.canmag.com/nw/6778-bridge-terabithia-review|url-status=usurped}}</ref>

== Cancelled sequel ==
In November 2007, del Toro confirmed that a sequel, titled ''3993'', was in production.<ref>{{cite web|date=8 November 2007|last=Turek|first=Ryan|title=Guillermo Del Toro's 3993 Details... – ComingSoon.net|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/horror/news/708222-guillermo-del-toros-3993-details|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161119120752/http://www.comingsoon.net/horror/news/708222-guillermo-del-toros-3993-details|archive-date=19 November 2016|access-date=4 May 2017}}</ref> Del Toro scrapped the project after deciding to direct ''[[Hellboy II: The Golden Army]]''.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 January 2017 |title=Pan's Labyrinth: 15 Things You Never Knew |url=https://www.cbr.com/pans-labyrinth-things-you-never-knew/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181107104130/https://www.cbr.com/pans-labyrinth-things-you-never-knew/ |archive-date=7 November 2018 |access-date=2018-11-06}}</ref>

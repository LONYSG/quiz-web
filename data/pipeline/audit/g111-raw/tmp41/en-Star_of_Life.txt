{{Short description|Emergency medical service symbol}}
[[File:Star of life2.svg|thumb|The Star of Life represents [[emergency medical services]] such as [[ambulances]].]]
The '''Star of Life''' is a symbol used to identify [[emergency medical services]]. It features a [[blue]] [[heavy asterisk|six-pointed star]], outlined by a white border. The middle contains a [[Rod of Asclepius]] – an ancient symbol of medicine. The Star of Life can be found on ambulances, medical personnel uniforms, and other objects associated with emergency medicine or first aid. Elevators marked with the symbol indicate the lift is large enough to hold a stretcher. [[Medical identification tag|Medical bracelets or tags]] sometimes use the symbol to indicate that the bearer has a medical condition that emergency services may need to know.

The Star of Life is widely used around the world, but like many international symbols, it has not been adopted everywhere. In some countries, its use is restricted to authorized personnel.

== History ==
[[File:Emergency_Medical_Services_Flag.svg  |thumb|Generic [[emergency medical services]] flag]]
The Star of Life originated in the United States. In 1963, the [[American Medical Association]] (AMA) designed the Star of Life as a "universal" symbol for medical identification. The AMA did not trademark or copyright the symbol, stating it was being "freely offered" to manufacturers,<ref>{{cite journal |author=(Editorial) |date=1964 |title=[American Heart] Association Acts to Promote Medical Identification Symbol |journal=Circulation |volume=30 |issue=1 |pages=7 |doi=10.1161/01.CIR.30.1.7 |doi-access=free }}</ref> and also was for use on cards carried by persons with a medical condition.<ref>{{cite journal |url=http://archpedi.jamanetwork.com/article.aspx?articleid=500942 |title=Universal Medical Identification Symbol |journal=Am J Dis Child |date=1964 |volume=107 |number=5 |page=439 |doi=10.1001/archpedi.1964.02080060441001|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

By way of a 1964 resolution,{{citation needed|date=January 2022}} it was adopted by the [[World Medical Association]] "as the universal emergency medical information symbol."<ref>{{Cite press release|title=Maucher Jenkins files an invalidation action for DBRD against the BKS "Star of Life" trademarks at the German PTO |date=4 December 2020 |url=https://www.maucherjenkins.com/commentary/german-pto-trade-mark-invalidation-action-star-of-life-rescue-sign |access-date=2 January 2022 |website=Maucher Jenkins |language=en |first=Michael |last=Nielen}} (Note that Maucher Jenkins is representing one party in the trademark dispute.)</ref> The Star of Life was promoted by the [[American Red Cross]] and rapidly adopted worldwide.<ref>{{cite journal |author=Dukelow, Donald |date=1966 |title=Emergency Medical Identification Symbol |journal=School Health |volume=36 |issue=1 |pages=20–21 |doi=10.1111/j.1746-1561.1966.tb05512.x|pmid=5174672 }}</ref>

In 1970, when the American Medical Association's Committee on Emergency Medical Services formed the [[National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians]] (NREMT),<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.webpal.org/SAFE/aaarecovery/7_medicine/Medicine%20-%20Severe/Developing%20Emergency%20Health%20Services.pdf |title=Developing Emergency Medical Services |publisher=American Medical Association |accessdate=17 February 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.nremt.org/rwd/public/document/history |title=History of the National Registry of EMTs |publisher=National Registry of EMTs |accessdate=17 February 2020}}</ref> the AMA chose the Star of Life to designate nationally certified Emergency Medical Services personnel. In 1973, the NREMT filed for a trademark for the Star of Life logo, under the category of a [[collective membership mark]]. This version featured a Star of Life enveloped by a circle. NREMT's trademark was granted in 1975, but "was not renewed and therefore has expired."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=72454410&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch |title=USPTO Trademark Search of US Serial #72454410 |publisher=U.S. Patent and Trademark Office |date=September 9, 1975 |accessdate= 17 February 2020}}</ref> NREMT subsequently registered a [[service mark]] featuring a Star of Life and the words "NATIONAL REGISTRY." This logo's trademark remains active.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=78453390&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch |title=USPTO Trademark Search of US Serial #78453390 |publisher=U.S. Patent and Trademark Office |access-date= 17 February 2020}}</ref>

Prior to the use of the Star of Life, ambulances in the United States commonly displayed a [[safety orange]]-colored cross on a square background. This was only a slight variation from the inverted [[Swiss flag]] (<big><span style="color:red">✚</span></big>) used by the [[International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement]]. In 1973, the [[American Red Cross]] complained that the traditional orange cross too closely resembled their logo of a red cross on a white background, the usage of which is restricted by the [[Geneva Conventions]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.state.sd.us/dps/EMS/EMS_Main/Star.htm |archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20070711171553/http://www.state.sd.us/dps/ems/EMS_Main/Star.htm |archivedate=2007-07-11 |title=Star of Life - The EMS Symbol |publisher=South Dakota EMS |access-date=2007-06-29}}</ref> Dr. Dawson Mills, Chief of the EMS Branch, [[National Highway Traffic Safety Administration]] in the United States, asked the National Registry of EMTs for permission to use the star as the "national identifier for Emergency Medical Services" in the United States,"<ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=718rAwAAQBAJ&q=%22Red+Cross%22+NHTSA+Ambulance+Dawson+Mills&pg=PT45|title=Prehospital Care Pearls and Pitfalls|year=2012|publisher=People's Medical Publishing House|isbn=9781607951711|accessdate=17 February 2020}}</ref> and in 1977 reported to Congress that it had become the national standard.<ref>{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ucsbaluyES0C |title=Department of Transportation and Related Agencies Appropriations for 1977 Hearings Before a Subcommittee of the Committee on Appropriations, House of Representatives, Ninety-fourth Congress, Second Session. Page 224 |year=1976 |publisher=United States Congress}}</ref>

Leo R. Schwartz, Chief of the EMS Branch, [[National Highway Traffic Safety Administration]] in the United States, modified the Star of Life by adding the six main tasks of Emergency Medical Services and changing the color from red (used by the [[American Medical Association|AMA]]) to blue.<ref>[https://northern.vaems.org/index.php/about-us/star-of-life "Star of Life'] - Northern Virginia EMS Council</ref> The "blue Star of Life" was recommended for adoption by the United States Department of Health, Education and Welfare on October 25, 1973,<ref>{{cite report |title=Star of Life Emergency Medical Care Symbol |version=DOT HS 803 721 |publisher=United States Department of Transportation, NHTSA |date=January 1979}}</ref> and was registered as a [[certification mark]] on February 1, 1977 in the name of the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration.<ref>{{cite web |date=February 1, 1977 
|url=https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=1058022&caseSearchType=US_APPLICATION&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch|title=USPTO Trademark Search of US Serial #1058022 |publisher=U.S. Patent and Trademark Office |access-date=17 July 2019}}</ref>  Both NREMT's version and the US government's modification omit the white outline around the edge common on many of today's ambulances.

The US government's registration lists uses of the Star of Life, including "emergency medical care" and "emergency medical care vehicles." The US government's registration does not list any international classes of trademark.<ref>{{cite web |date=February 1, 1977 |url=http://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=73033491&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch |title=USPTO Trademark Search of US Serial #73033491 |publisher=U.S. Patent and Trademark Office |access-date=17 July 2019}}</ref>

Federal standards dictate requirements ambulances in the US must satisfy in order to display the Star of Life.<ref>{{Cite web |publisher=US General Services Administration|date=August 1, 2007 |title=Federal Specification for the Star-of-Life Ambulance |url=https://www.ok.gov/health2/documents/KKK-A-1822F%20%2007.01.2007.pdf |website=U.S. General Services Administration|page=3}}</ref> The federal government has given states additional authority to manage the symbol.<ref>{{Cite web|date=June 1995|title="Star of Life"|url=https://www.ems.gov/vgn-ext-templating/ems/sol/HS808721.pdf|url-status=dead|website=US Department of Transportation National Highway Traffic Safety Administration|access-date=2021-12-29|archive-date=2017-11-07|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171107204923/https://www.ems.gov/vgn-ext-templating/ems/sol/HS808721.pdf}}</ref> Private ambulance operators like [[American Medical Response|AMR]] have trademarked logos featuring an embedded Star of Life.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Trademark Status & Document Retrieval US Serial #75134611 |url=https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=75134611&caseSearchType=US_APPLICATION&caseType=SERIAL_NO&searchType=statusSearch |access-date=2021-12-29 |publisher=US Patent and Trademark Office |date=July 15, 1996}}</ref>

== Symbolism ==
[[Image:Star of life parts.svg|thumb|Six points on the Star of Life]]
The six branches of the star represent the six main tasks executed by rescuers all through the emergency chain:<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.angelfire.com/co/fantasyfigures/staroflife.html|title=Star of Life|access-date=2007-06-29}}</ref>

# '''Detection''': The first rescuers on the scene, usually untrained civilians or those involved in the incident, observe the scene, understand the problem, identify the dangers to themselves and the others, and take appropriate measures to ensure their safety on the scene (environmental, electricity, chemicals, radiation, etc.).
# '''Reporting''': The call for professional help is made and dispatch is connected with the victims, providing emergency medical dispatch.
# '''Response''': The first rescuers provide [[first aid]] and immediate care to the extent of their capabilities.
# '''On scene care''': The EMS personnel arrive and provide immediate care to the extent of their capabilities on-scene.
# '''Care in transit''': The EMS personnel proceed to transfer the patient to a [[hospital]] via an ambulance or helicopter for specialized care. They provide medical care during the transportation.
# '''Transfer to definitive care''': Appropriate specialized care is provided at the hospital.

== Global usage ==
===Africa===
Egyptian Ministry of Health ambulances display the Star of Life on one rear door, and a red crescent on the other.<ref>{{Cite web|title=300 new ambulances for Egypt. - Wietmarscher Ambulanz- und Sonderfahrzeug GmbH|url=https://www.was-vehicles.com/en/company/news/300-new-ambulances-for-egypt.html|access-date=2021-12-30|website=www.was-vehicles.com}}</ref>

=== Asia ===
==== East Asia ====
Ambulances in Taiwan, mainland China, Hong Kong, and Macau display the Star of Life. The symbol is also widely used in Japan, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Singapore. It is less common in South Korea, where ambulances display a green cross and green lights.

==== <span class="anchor" id="West Asia"></span> West and Central Asia ====
[[File:A United Hatzalah ambucycle.jpg|thumb|An ambulance motorcycle from Israel's [[United Hatzalah]], combining the [[Star of David]] with the Star of Life.]]
Most ambulances in Turkey do not use the Star of Life. Ambulances in Iran commonly display the Star of Life.  National ambulances in the United Arab Emirates do not display the Star of Life, instead showing an [[Electrocardiography|EKG]] graphic on the sides and rear. In Saudi Arabia, the [[Red Crescent Society]] answers the emergency line, and provides service in vehicles bearing the red crescent emblem.

In Israel, [[Magen David Adom]] displays a red [[Star of David|Jewish star]], sometimes shown with a Star of Life. The Jewish star is paired with the [[Protocol III|red crystal]] in times of conflict. Israel's other ambulance operator, [[United Hatzalah]], has a logo based on both stars. "The Star of Life is a universal symbol of emergency medical care. The Star of David is our national symbol. Combining these two elements reminds us of the messages that we...focus on." said United Hatzalah's president.{{Attribution needed|date=May 2025}}

In [[Palestine]], the [[Palestine Red Crescent Society]] provides ambulance service in vehicles displaying a red crescent, alongside private operators who often display the Star of Life.

====<span class="anchor" id="India"></span> South Asia====
Indian Automotive Industry Standard AIS-125 is the National Ambulance Code of India. This document is managed jointly by the Automotive Research Association and the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways. It requires ambulances display several distinctive markings including a reflective [[Battenburg markings|Battenburg]] pattern, the word "AMBULANCE", and the emergency telephone number. The standard states "Displayed on the upper half of the left side should be a 'Star of Life' symbol, with a size of 40 × 40 cm... Displayed on the left back window should be a 'Star of Life' symbol, with a size of 85% of the window".<ref>{{Cite web|date=June 2013|title=Constructional and Functional Requirements for Road Ambulances (National Ambulance Code)|url=http://www.nisc.gov.in/PDF/AIS_125.pdf|website=The Automotive Research Association of India, on behalf of Automotive Industry Standards Committee, under Central Motor Vehicle Rules – Technical Standing Committee set-up by Ministry of Road Transport and Highways (Department of Road Transport and Highways), Government of India}}</ref>

=== Central and South America ===
The Star of Life is used in many Spanish speaking nations, where it is known as {{lang|es|La Estrella de la vida}}.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Historia de la Estrella de La Vida |url=https://www.lifeguard.cl/files/Estrella-de-la-Vida.pdf |website=Revista del Hospital J. M. Ramos Mejía}}</ref> In Argentina, the {{ill|Sistema de Atención Médica de Emergencia|es}} (SAME), the capital district ambulance service, uses a green and slightly rounded version of the Star of Life in their logo. In [[Brazil]], the Star of Life is known by its Portuguese name ''{{Lang|pt|Estrela da Vida}}''. A red Star of Life is incorporated into the national emergency service's visual identity standards.<ref>{{Cite web |title=SAMU 192 Manual De Identidade Visual |url=https://bvsms.saude.gov.br/bvs/publicacoes/manual_identidade_visual_samu.pdf  |access-date=Dec 29, 2021 |website=Sistema Único de Saúde}}</ref>  Brazil's [[Brazilian National Standards Organization|ABNT]] Standard NBR 14561 for ambulance design makes direct reference to being based on the American Star of Life vehicle. Ambulances which do not comply with the Brazilian standard are prohibited from displaying the Star of Life or the word “RESGATE” (rescue).<ref>{{Cite book |title=Veículos para atendimento a emergências médicas e resgate |publisher=ABNT – Associação Brasileira de Normas Técnicas |year=2000 |series=NBR 14561. Projeto 24:302.07-002:1999 |location=Rio de Janeiro |page=1}}</ref>

=== Australia and New Zealand ===
[[File:Malteserkreuz.svg|thumb|upright 0.5|Cross of the [[Order of Saint John (chartered 1888)|Order of St. John]]]]
A few patient transport providers like Ambulance Service Australia use the Star of Life. However, it is far more common to see the [[Maltese cross|Maltese Cross]] in this region.

===Europe===
The European Union's ambulance design standard [[CEN 1789]] in section A1 ''Recognition and Visibility of Ambulances'' states:<blockquote>With the exception of Red Cross societies or where the "Star of life" is locally registered, a blue reflective "Star of life" emblem (minimum size 500 mm) together with reflective letters, numerals or a symbol identifying the organization and the vehicle, should be applied to the roof of the ambulance...a blue reflective "Star of life" emblem should [also] be applied to the sides and rear of the ambulance.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Final Draft prEN 1789 "Medical vehicles and their equipment - Road ambulances" |url=http://www.helse-midt.no/Helse-Midt/Ambulanse/Overtakelse/Ambulansestandard_EN_1789.pdf |access-date=2021-12-29 |publisher= CEN/TC 239 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141024062237/http://www.helse-midt.no/Helse-Midt/Ambulanse/Overtakelse/Ambulansestandard_EN_1789.pdf |archive-date=24 October 2014 |url-status=dead}}</ref></blockquote>

In [[Portugal]], the Star of Life is referred to by the Portuguese name {{lang|pt|Estrela da Vida}}. In March 1977, the then National Ambulance Service of Portugal (the present [[Emergency medical services in Portugal|INEM, National Medical Emergency Institute]]) filed a trademark registration on the symbol, which was granted in 1981. The INEM continues to hold a trademark registration in the country, which is used to certify that vehicles are "in accordance with INEM standards" and personnel have "proper preparation". It may also be used on maps and road signs "to indicate the location or access to qualified emergency medical care services".<ref>{{Cite web|date=2017|title=The Star of Life - SYMBOL OF INEM |url=https://www.inem.pt/2017/05/26/logotipo-inem/ |website=Instituto Nacional de Emergência Médica}}</ref>

[[Belgium|Belgian]] EMTs use blue stars; nurses, doctors, and ambulance drivers wear other colors.<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-01-11 |title=Dringende geneeskundige hulpverlening: verhoogde herkenbaarheid van voertuigen en personeel voor meer veiligheid  |trans-title=Urgent medical assistance: increased recognizability of vehicles and personnel for more safety |website=Presscenter.org |url=http://www.presscenter.org/nl/pressrelease/20170327/dringende-geneeskundige-hulpverlening-verhoogde-herkenbaarheid-van-voertuigen- |access-date=2022-01-02 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180111165051/http://www.presscenter.org/nl/pressrelease/20170327/dringende-geneeskundige-hulpverlening-verhoogde-herkenbaarheid-van-voertuigen-|archive-date=2018-01-11}}</ref> In the [[Netherlands]], the Star of Life is widely used. The Dutch government owns a trademark on the symbol, alongside the paint scheme used on emergency vehicles.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Striping & Star of Life: beschermde beeldmerken |trans-title=Striping & Star of Life: protected logos |language=nl |url=https://www.ambulancezorg.nl/themas/public-affairs/huisstijl-ambulancezorg/striping-star-of-life|access-date=2022-01-02|website=www.ambulancezorg.nl}}</ref>

After [[Latvia]] became independent in 1918, ambulance services in the 1920s and 1930s were provided by the [[Latvian Red Cross]] and thus displayed the [[Red Cross symbol]]. During the [[Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic|Soviet control of Latvia]], that symbol continued to be used as a general symbol of medicine. As [[On the Restoration of Independence of the Republic of Latvia|restoration of independence]], the Riga Emergency Medical Service Station called on the government to adopt the Star of Life ({{Langx|lv|Dzīvības zvaigzne}}, sometimes ''sniegpārsla'' - 'snowflake') and the change was made with effect from 12 January 1995. It is currently used on vehicles, uniforms and medical service buildings.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2004-03-02 |title=Dzīvības zvaigzne: ātrai palīdzībai – 100 |trans-title=Star of Life: emergency medical service - 100 |url=https://www.apollo.lv/4684229/dzivibas-zvaigzne-atrai-palidzibai-100 |website=[[Apollo.lv]]}}</ref>

In [[Germany]], the symbol was registered in 1993 by industry association [[:de:Bundesverband eigenständiger Rettungsdienste und Katastrophenschutz|BKS]], an umbrella organization for private rescue services there.<ref name="#2032229">{{cite web| title=Registerauskunft {{!}} Registernummer: 2032229 (Marke eingetragen, Stand am: 08.08.2022) |date=12 March 1993 |access-date=8 August 2022 |website=Deutsches Patent und Markenamt |url=https://register.dpma.de/DPMAregister/marke/register/2032229/DE}}</ref> In December 2020, another ambulance industry association, [[:de:Deutscher Berufsverband Rettungsdienst|DBRD]], filed a challenge with the [[German Patent and Trade Mark Office]]. The challengers hired a law firm specializing in [[intellectual property]] rights to research the symbol's history. This led to allegations the Star of Life had already been in widespread public use since the 1960s, before being registered in Germany (or the US for that matter). The challengers claimed the trademark office seemed to have been not properly informed of these facts, which presumably could have led the original application to have been denied on [[public domain]] grounds. The [[European Union Intellectual Property Office]]'s 2014 rejection of a trademark on a minimally modified Star of Life was also cited.<ref>{{Cite web|title=DBRD geht gegen "Star of Life"-Regelung vor  |trans-title=DBRD takes action against "Star of Life" registration |url=https://www.rettungsdienst.de/news/dbrd-geht-gegen-star-of-life-regelung-vor-64488|access-date=2022-01-02|website=www.rettungsdienst.de}}</ref> {{as of|August 2022}}, BKS continues to hold German copyright on the symbol.<ref name="#2032229" />

In the [[United Kingdom]], some [[National Health Services|NHS]] ambulances in the UK display the Star of Life in addition to the [[Emergency medical services in the United Kingdom|local Ambulance Service]] emblem. These (latter) emblems have a pale gold six-spoked wheel with a Rod of Asclepius in the foreground. A crown and [[Maltese cross|Maltese Cross]], a common EMS emblem in [[Commonwealth of Nations|Commonwealth nations]], are included in the design.

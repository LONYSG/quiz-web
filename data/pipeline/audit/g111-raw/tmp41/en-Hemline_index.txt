{{Short description|Economic theory}}
The '''hemline index''' is a theory that suggests that skirt length ([[hemline]]s) rise or fall along with stock prices. The most common version of the theory is that skirt lengths get shorter in good economic times (1920s, 1960s)<ref>Claire Brayford, [http://www.express.co.uk/posts/view/34787/The-Hemline-Economy "The Hemline Economy"], ''Daily Express'', February 13, 2008</ref> and longer in bad, such as after the [[Wall Street crash of 1929|1929 Wall Street crash]]. However, the reverse has also been proposed with longer skirts signaling prosperity (1950s).<ref>{{Cite news|last=James|first=Andrea|date=April 25, 2008|title=Hemlines doing their part for the economy: tough times call for short skirts|work=Seattle Post-Intelligencer|url=http://www.seattlepi.com/business/360526_shortskirts25.html|access-date=March 20, 2011}}</ref> 

The theory is often incorrectly attributed to economist [[George W. Taylor (professor)|George Taylor]] in 1926.<ref>Tamar Lewin, The hemline index, updated, ''International Herald Tribune'', October 19, 2008</ref><ref>See also Henrietta Prast "Fashionomics", ''[[Wilmott Magazine]]'', June 2005, who cites Paul Nystrom in his 1928 monograph, ''The Economics of Fashion'' as the source of the theory.</ref> Taylor's 1929 thesis ''Significant post-war changes in the full-fashioned hosiery industry'', which identified skirt length as one factor that led to explosive growth in the hosiery industry during the 1920s, did not propose a hemline theory.<ref>{{Cite web|title=The Surprising Reason Why Skirt Lengths Have Changed Throughout History|url=https://www.instyle.com/fashion/clothing/what-is-the-hemline-index-real|access-date=2021-07-10|website=InStyle|language=en}}</ref> 

Non-peer-reviewed research in 2010 supported the correlation, suggesting that "the economic cycle leads the hemline with about three years".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://publishing.eur.nl/ir/repub/asset/20147/EI%202010-40.pdf |title=The hemline and the economy: is there any match? |author=Marjolein van Baardwijk |author2=Philip Hans Franses |publisher=publishing.eur.nl |date=2010 |access-date=2014-03-03|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120306100914/http://publishing.eur.nl/ir/repub/asset/20147/EI%202010-40.pdf|archive-date=2012-03-06}}</ref>

[[Desmond Morris]] revisited the theory in his book ''Manwatching''.<ref>https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/632396/1/424749.pdf pp. 86-89</ref>

{{short description|Period of twilight in the morning or evening}}
[[File:Sé Catedral de Bragança - Portugal (3663183339).jpg|thumb|Blue hour at the [[Old Cathedral of the Holy Name of Jesus, Bragança]] in Portugal]]
The '''blue hour''' (from [[French language|French]] {{lang|fr|l'heure bleue}};<ref>{{cite book |last=Verfaillie |first=Roland |title=L'heure Bleue |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=P56JAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA5 |page=5 |year=2011 |location=San Francisco |publisher=Purple Onion Press|isbn=9780978708559 }}</ref>{{efn|A similar [[calque]] is used in many languages.{{citation needed|date=March 2017}}}} {{IPA|fr|lœʁ blø|pron}}) is the period of [[twilight]] (in the [[dawn]] or [[dusk]], around the [[Twilight#Nautical twilight|nautical stage]]) when the [[Sun]] is several degrees below the [[horizon]]. During this time, the remaining [[sunlight]] takes on a mostly blue [[tints and shades|shade]]. This shade differs from the colour of the sky on a clear day, which is caused by [[Rayleigh scattering]].

The blue hour occurs when the Sun is far enough below the horizon so that the sunlight's blue wavelengths dominate due to the [[Chappuis absorption]] in the [[ozone layer]].<ref name="Hoeppe">{{cite book |first=Götz |last=Hoeppe |title=Why the Sky Is Blue: Discovering the Color of Life |publisher=[[Princeton University Press]] |location=[[Princeton, New Jersey|Princeton]] |year=2007 |isbn=978-0-691-12453-7 |pages=249–53 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=-7inyeQbBjQC&pg=PA238}}</ref> Since the term is [[colloquial]], it lacks an official definition such as [[dawn|nautical dawn]], [[nautical dusk]], or the three stages of twilight. Rather, blue hour refers to the state of [[diffuse sky radiation|natural lighting]] that usually occurs around the nautical stage of the twilight period (at dawn or dusk).<ref name=timeanddate />

The blue hour is shorter in regions near the [[equator]] due to the sun rising and setting at steep angles. In places closer to the poles, the illumination and twilight periods are longer as the sun rises and sets at shallower angles.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Wu |first1=Jennifer |last2=Martin |first2=James |title=Photography: Night Sky: A Field Guide for Shooting after Dark |date=13 March 2014 |publisher=Mountaineers Books |isbn=978-1-59485-839-0 |page=139 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=mS8QAwAAQBAJ&dq=photography+%22blue+hour%22+latitude&pg=PT139 |language=en}}</ref>

==Explanation and times of occurrence==
A still commonly presented but incorrect explanation for the blue hue is that after sunset and before sunrise the atmosphere receives and disperses only the sun's shorter, blue wavelengths, while the longer, reddish wavelengths are scattered away.<ref>{{cite web |title=What is the blue hour? Learn about this magic time between daylight and darkness |url=https://www.mnn.com/earth-matters/climate-weather/blogs/what-is-the-blue-hour-learn-about-this-magic-time-between |website=mnn.com |access-date=7 April 2019}}</ref> In fact, the blue hour occurs when the Sun is far enough below the horizon so that the sunlight's blue wavelengths dominate due to the [[Chappuis absorption]] caused by ozone.<ref name="Hoeppe"/>

When the [[sky]] is clear, the blue hour can be a colourful spectacle, with the [[diffuse sky radiation|indirect sunlight]] tinting the sky yellow, orange, [[Belt of Venus|red]], and [[Earth's shadow|blue]]. The deep blue of the sky overhead comes from [[ozone]] in the upper atmosphere absorbing red light, which differs from the [[scattering]] that makes the daytime sky blue.<ref name=timeanddate /><ref name="Hoeppe"/>

At mid-latitudes the blue hour usually lasts about 20 to 30 minutes, shortly after sunset and before sunrise, and its exact timing varies with location.<ref>{{cite web |last=Byrd |first=Deborah |title=What is the blue hour? |url=https://earthsky.org/earth/what-is-the-blue-hour |website=earthsky.org |date=30 August 2014 |access-date=29 October 2026}}</ref> Its duration depends on latitude and season: at about 40° north or south it lasts around 20 minutes in March, and in [[Oslo]] roughly 30 minutes at the same time of year, while during the [[polar night]] it can last for several hours.<ref name=timeanddate />

==Photography==
Many artists value this period for the quality of the [[hard and soft light|soft light]]. Although the blue hour does not have an official definition, the blue color spectrum is most prominent when the Sun is between 4° and 8° below the horizon.<ref name=timeanddate>{{cite web |title=The Blue Hour |url=https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/blue-hour.html |publisher=timeanddate.com |access-date=29 October 2026}}</ref> Photography planning applications use narrower or wider ranges, from 4°–6° to as much as 3°–10° below the horizon.<ref name="TPE twilight">{{cite web |title=Magical Gold and Blue: Confusion in the Twilight Zone |url=https://photoephemeris.com/en/articles/magical-gold-and-blue-confusion-in-the-twilight-zone |website=The Photographer's Ephemeris |date=16 May 2016 |access-date=29 October 2026}}</ref>

Photographers use blue hour for the tranquil mood it sets. The blue hour is well suited to subjects with artificial light sources, such as buildings, monuments, cityscapes, or bridges, because of the contrast between the blue sky and artificial light.<ref name=timeanddate />

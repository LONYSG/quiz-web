'''Nyepi''' ([[Balinese language|Balinese]]: ᬜᬾᬧᬶ), also known as '''Day of Silence''', is a [[Balinese people|Balinese]] holiday held every ''Isakawarsa'' ("new year") according to the [[Balinese saka calendar|Balinese calendar]], and it can be traced as far back as 78 A.D.<ref name="indo.com">[http://www.indo.com/culture/nyepi.html Nyepi: Bali's day of Silence] Culture, Bali & Indonesia (2009)</ref>

The observance includes maintaining silence, [[fasting]], and meditation for [[Balinese Hinduism|Balinese Hindus]]. The following day is celebrated as New Year's Day.<ref name="Hogue2006-03-24">{{cite news|title=In Bali, a holiday for the ears|last=Hogue|first=Thomas|date=24 March 2006|work=[[The New York Times]]|location=[[New York City|New York]]|issn=0362-4331|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2006/03/24/opinion/24iht-edhogue.html|access-date=7 March 2011}}</ref><ref name="Onishi2011-03-06">{{cite news|last=Onishi|first=Norimitsu|author-link=Norimitsu Onishi|title=Silence Befalls Bali, but Only for a Day|date=6 March 2011|work=[[The New York Times]]|location=[[New York City|New York]]|issn=0362-4331|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2011/03/07/world/asia/07indonesia.html| access-date=7 March 2011}}</ref>

==Background==
The word "Nyepi" originates from ''sepi'', meaning "silent". The origin of the observance is a celebration of the [[Mesha Sankranti|Hindu Solar New Year]] based on the [[Shaka era]], which began in 78 AD.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Marcella |first=Zindi |title=Sejarah Hari Raya Nyepi, Bermula dari Zaman India Kuno |url=https://www.detik.com/sumbagsel/berita/d-7232967/sejarah-hari-raya-nyepi-bermula-dari-zaman-india-kuno#:~:text=History%2520Hari%2520Raya%2520Nyepi%2520bermula,penjelasannya%2520yang%2520berhasil%2520dirangkum%2520detikSumbagsel. |website=detiksumbagsel |language=id-ID |trans-title=History of Nyepi Day, Starting from Ancient India |access-date=26 March 2025}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|last=Zulfahmi|first=Najhan|title=Tujuan Nyepi: Makna di Balik Tradisi Terbesar Umat Hindu di Indonesia|url=https://www.detik.com/bali/budaya/d-7224851/tujuan-nyepi-makna-di-balik-tradisi-terbesar-umat-hindu-di-indonesia|website=detikbali|language=id-ID|trans-title=The Purpose of Nyepi: The Meaning Behind the Greatest Tradition of Hindus in Indonesia|access-date=30 March 2025}}</ref>

==Rituals==
[[File:Tawur Kesanga in Samarinda.jpg|right|250px|thumb|''Tawur Kesanga'', a ritual procession on the eve of Nyepi. Children carry lit torches that are used to light bonfires to burn ''ogoh-ogoh'' statues.<ref name="indo.com" /><ref>{{Cite web |title= Bali Travel Stories |website= lonelyplanet.com |date= 15 February 2013 |url= https://www.lonelyplanet.com/indonesia/bali/articles |url-status=dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20131104002535/http://www.lonelyplanet.com/indonesia/bali/travel-tips-and-articles/76348 |archive-date= 4 November 2013}}</ref>]]
[[File:Ogoh-Ogoh---Ubud Football Field-Red one with kids.jpeg|200px|thumb|''[[Ogoh-ogoh]]'' being paraded in the Ngrupuk area during the Bhuta Yajna ceremony]]

Observed from 6 a.m. until 6 a.m. the following day, Nyepi is dedicated to self-reflection, fasting, and meditation. During this period, activities that may interfere with this purpose are prohibited, including work, travel, and entertainment or leisure activities. The use of fire and artificial lighting is restricted, resulting in minimal electricity use, and residents and visitors are required to remain indoors across the island of Bali.<ref name="pemprov">{{cite web|url=https://lovebali.baliprov.go.id/article/detail/1710312976791/nyepi,-balinese-new-year:-day-of-silence|title=Nyepi, Balinese New Year: Day of Silence|date=13 March 2024|access-date=3 January 2026|work=Government of Bali}}</ref>

As Bali's usually bustling streets and roads become empty during Nyepi, there is little or no noise from TVs and radios, and few signs of activity are visible inside homes. The only people to be seen outdoors are the [[pecalang]], traditional security men who patrol the streets to ensure the prohibitions are followed.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/bali-nyepi-new-year-day-of-silence|title=Everything travelers need to know about Bali's "Day of Silence"|first=Ian Lloyd |last=Neubauer|access-date=3 January 2026|date=3 March 2025|publisher=Lonely Planet}}</ref>

Although Nyepi is primarily a Hindu holiday, non-Hindu residents and tourists are not exempt from the restrictions. Although they are free to do as they wish inside hotels, no one is allowed onto beaches or streets, and the [[Ngurah Rai International Airport|only airport in Bali]] remains closed for the entire day. Tourists who violate these rules can face deportation or even prosecution.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Erviani |first=Ni Komang |date=27 March 2023 |title=Two Polish nationals deported from Bali for ignoring Nyepi strictures |url=https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2023/03/27/two-polish-nationals-deported-from-bali-for-ignoring-nyepi-strictures.html |access-date=22 May 2024 |work=The Jakarta Post}}</ref> A Swiss tourist was given a one-year prison sentence in 2026 for leaving his hotel and calling Nyepi "crazy" on social media. Judge Tjokorda Putra Budi Pastima said the tourist had "offended the Balinese people, hurt their faith[,] and provoked public outrage".<ref name="ap-2026-nyepi">{{cite news |title=Swiss tourist sentenced to a year in jail for insulting Bali's sacred Day of Silence |url=https://apnews.com/article/indonesia-swiss-tourist-trial-nyepi-348f1d4b701051f5d7abcf85eae0404c |agency=[[Associated Press]] |date=20 August 2026}}</ref>

On the afternoon and evening before Nyepi, accessibility on roads is limited. Many local roads are closed for evening parades, making it difficult to navigate by car or motorcycle. Most local restaurants close early, and many ATMs are switched off, with cash removed until the day following Nyepi.<ref>{{Cite web |title=We Visited Bali During Nyepi: Here's Everything You Need To Know |url=https://baliuntold.com/culture/nyepi/ |access-date=22 May 2024 |website=Bali Untold}}</ref> Electricity remains operational.<ref>{{cite web|title=Pertama Kalinya, Listrik Akan Tetap saat Nyepi di Nusa Penida|url=https://www.balipost.com/news/2025/03/23/448737/Pertama-Kalinya,Listrik-akan-Tetap...html|access-date=3 January 2026|date=23 March 2025|work=Bali Post|language=id|trans-title=For the First Time, Electricity Will Remain on During Nyepi in Nusa Penida}}</ref>

The only exceptions granted are for [[emergency vehicles]] responding to life-threatening conditions and women going into labour.<ref name="pelak">{{cite web|title=Pelaksanaan Hari Raya Nyepi di Indonesia|url=https://www.babadbali.com/canangsari/hkt-hari-nyepi-pelaks.htm|publisher=Babad Bali|access-date=17 March 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=The Balinese Day of Silence|author=Greg Rodgers|url=http://goasia.about.com/od/Events-and-Holidays/fl/The-Balinese-Day-of-Silence.htm|publisher=About.com|access-date=30 July 2015|archive-date=2 April 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160402095250/http://goasia.about.com/od/Events-and-Holidays/fl/The-Balinese-Day-of-Silence.htm|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On the day after Nyepi, known as ''Ngembak Geni'' ("relighting the fire"), social activity picks up again, as families and friends gather to ask forgiveness from one another and to perform religious rituals together. Fires and electricity are allowed once more, and cooking resumes.<ref name="pemprov" />
* The '''[[Melasti]] ritual''' is performed 3–4 days beforehand, within [[Pura (Balinese temple)|Hindu temples]] near the sea and beaches. It is meant to purify the sacred objects ''[[Murti|pratima]]'', ''arca'', and ''pralingga''{{Clarify|date=March 2025|reason=It should be clarified with these terms mean.}} and to acquire [[Sacred waters|sacred water]] from the sea.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Thousands flock to Bali's beaches for Melasti purification ritual – National |url=https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/03/04/thousands-flock-to-balis-beaches-for-melasti-purification-ritual.html |access-date=12 March 2024 |website=The Jakarta Post}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=28 February 2022 |title=What Is Melasti? The Hindu 'Purification Festival' Celebrated Ahead of Balinese New Year Nyepi Day |url=https://news.abplive.com/lifestyle/what-is-melasti-the-hindu-purification-festival-celebrated-ahead-of-balinese-new-year-nyepi-day-1516021 |access-date=12 March 2024 |website=news.abplive.com}}</ref>
* The '''Bhuta Yajna ritual''' is performed in order to vanquish negative elements and create balance with God, humankind, and nature. It is also meant to appease [[Batara Kala]], with offerings of live animal sacrifice. Around sunset, the ''pengrupukan'', or ''ngrupuk''<ref>{{cite web |title= Ogoh Ogoh Museum Mengwi |website= bali.com |url= https://bali.com/places/ogoh-ogoh-museum-mengwi/ |access-date= 28 May 2024 }}</ref> ceremony begins inside houses, with noisy banging of pots and pans and bamboo tubes along with burning of dried coconut leaf torches to drive out demons.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Nyepi in Bali – Experiencing the Balinese Day of Silence |url=https://bali.com/bali/travel-guide/culture/nyepi-balinese-new-year/ |access-date=13 March 2024 |website=Bali.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=  Roxanne Hoorn |title= Meet the creatures and creators behind Bali's parade for banishing evil spirits. On the night before the Balinese New Year, villagers bring monsters to life — and then burn them |website= atlasobscura.com |date= 1 May 2024 |url= https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/bali-new-year-monster-parade-ogoh-ogoh |access-date= 28 May 2024 }}</ref>

Most Balinese villages make ''[[ogoh-ogoh]]'', demonic statues made of richly painted bamboo, [[papier-mâché]], cloth, and [[tinsel]], symbolising negative elements, malevolent spirits, or even characters from Hindu mythology. After the ''ogoh-ogoh'' have been paraded around the village, they are burned in the cemeteries, although many are displayed in front of community halls for another month or more and sometimes even purchased by museums and collectors.<ref name="pemprov" />

[[File:Nyepi2012.jpg|250px|thumb|A deserted street during Nyepi]]

* The '''Nyepi rituals''' are performed as follows:<ref>{{Cite web |last=Daniswari |first=Dini|date=1 March 2022 |title=4 Pantangan saat Nyepi di Bali |trans-title=4 Taboos during Nyepi in Bali |url=https://denpasar.kompas.com/read/2022/03/01/184202978/4-pantangan-saat-nyepi-di-bali |access-date=13 March 2024 |website= denpasar.kompas.com |language=id}}</ref>
** Amati Geni: No fire or light, including no electricity
** Amati Karya: No working
** Amati Lelunganan: No travel
** Amati Lelanguan: No revelry/self-entertainment
* The '''Yoga/Brata ritual''' starts at 6:00&nbsp;a.m. and continues until 6:00&nbsp;a.m. the next day.
* The '''Ngembak Agni/Labuh Brata ritual''' is performed for all Hindus to forgive each other and to welcome the new days to come.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tradisi Ngembak Geni dan Maknanya Bagi Umat Hindu |trans-title=Ngembak Geni/Agni Tradition nand Its Meaning for Hindus |url=https://www.suara.com/news/2021/03/15/192823/tradisi-ngembak-geni-dan-maknanya-bagi-umat-hindu |access-date=13 March 2024 |website=suara.com |language=id}}</ref>
* The '''Dharma Shanti rituals''' are performed after all the Nyepi rituals are finished.<ref name="pelak" />

In 2024, the ''ogoh-ogoh'' parades were cancelled due to the [[2024 Indonesian general election|general election]] being so close to the date of the festival and the regional government fearing that the parade may be used to convey political themes. Only villages, in particular traditional ones, were allowed to hold parades.<ref>{{cite web |title= Tourists Disappointed There Will Be No Ogoh-Ogoh Competitions in Bali In 2024 |website= thebalisun.com |date= 16 January 2024 |url= https://thebalisun.com/tourists-disappointed-there-will-be-no-ogoh-ogoh-competitions-in-bali-in-2024/ |access-date= 28 May 2024 }}</ref>

==Related festivals==
Nyepi is related to festivals observed by Hindus in the [[Indian subcontinent]], although the dates are not the same, due to the use of different calendars. For example, the Hindus of [[Maharashtra]] term their new year ''[[Gudi Padwa]]'' (in [[Marathi language|Marathi]]: गुढी पाडवा); [[Sindh]]is celebrate the beginning of their calendar year as ''[[Cheti Chand]]''; [[Meitei people|Manipuris]] celebrate [[Sajibu Nongma Panba]]; and Hindus of [[Andhra Pradesh]] and [[Karnataka]] celebrate ''[[Ugadi]]''.

==Security==
Security is provided by [[hansip]], while the [[pecalang]] are redirected into security roles from their usual tasks such as traffic coordination; both types of security forces report to local village heads.<ref>{{Cite web | url=https://jakartaglobe.id/news/balinese-hindus-to-parade-7000-giant-puppets-ahead-of-nyepi-celebration/ |title = Balinese Hindus to Parade 7,000 Giant Puppets Ahead of Nyepi Celebration|work=Jakarta Globe|first=I Nyoman|last=Mardika|date=27 March 2017|access-date=3 January 2026}}</ref>

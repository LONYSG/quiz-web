'''Backmasking''' is a [[sound recording and reproduction|recording]] technique in which a message is recorded backward onto a track that is meant to be played forward.<ref>{{citation |url=https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/backmasking |title=backmasking |publisher=Merriam-Webster |access-date=2022-02-03}}</ref> It is a deliberate process, whereas a message found through phonetic reversal may be unintentional.

Artists have used backmasking for artistic, comedic and satiric effect, on both analogue and digital recordings. It has also been used to censor words or phrases for clean releases of [[Profanity|explicit]] songs.

In 1969, rumors of a backmasked message in the Beatles song "[[Revolution 9]]" fueled the "[[Paul is dead]]" urban legend.<ref name="salon">{{Cite book |url=https://www.salon.com/2017/06/24/what-exactly-lurks-within-the-backward-grooves-of-stairway-to-heaven/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170625005652/http://www.salon.com/2017/06/24/what-exactly-lurks-within-the-backward-grooves-of-stairway-to-heaven/ |url-status=live |archive-date=June 25, 2017 |title=[Led Zeppelin IV] |last=Erik |first=Davis |date=2005|publisher=Continuum |isbn=0-8264-1658-6 |location=New York, NY |language=en-US |oclc=57452450 |access-date=2018-08-14}}</ref> Since at least the early 1980s, Christian groups in the United States alleged that backmasking was being used by prominent [[rock music]]ians for [[satanism|Satanic]] purposes,<ref>{{cite news |last=Billiter |first=Bill |title=Satanic Messages Played Back for Assembly Panel |date=April 28, 1982 |work=The Los Angeles Times |page=B3 |url=https://www.newspapers.com/article/the-los-angeles-times-backmasking/206804322/ |via=[[Newspapers.com]]}}</ref> leading to record-burning protests and proposed anti-backmasking legislation by state and federal governments during the 1980s, as part of the [[Satanic panic]] movement of the time.

Many popular musicians were accused of including backmasked messages in their music. However, apparent backmasked messages may in fact be examples of [[pareidolia]] (the brain's tendency to recognize patterns in meaningless data), coincidental phonetic reversal,<ref name=salon/> or as deliberate responses to the allegations themselves.<ref>{{cite news |last=Macdonald |first=Fiona |date=21 October 2014 |title=The hidden messages in songs |publisher=BBC |url=https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20141003-the-hidden-messages-in-songs |quote=After Christian fundamentalists claimed that a line in the title track of their 1974 album Eldorado sounded like 'He is the nasty one – Christ you're infernal' when reversed, the Electric Light Orchestra inserted a deliberately backmasked segment into their next album.}}</ref>

==History==

===Development===
The backwards playing of records was advised as training for magicians by occultist [[Aleister Crowley]], who suggested in his 1913 book ''[[Magick (Book 4)]]'' that an [[adept]] "train himself to think backwards by external means", one of which was to "listen to phonograph records, reversed".<ref>{{cite book |last=Crowley |first=Aleister |author-link=Aleister Crowley |title=[[Magick (Book 4)]] |orig-date=1913 |publisher=Weiser |year=1997 |isbn=978-0-87728-919-7 |page=648}}</ref><ref name="salon" /> In the movie ''[[Gold Diggers of 1935]]'', the end of the dancing-pianos musical number, "The Words Are in My Heart," is filmed in [[reverse motion]], with the accompanying instrumental score incidentally being reversed.

[[File:Reel-to-reel recorder tc-630.jpg|thumb|[[Tape recorder]]s allow backward recording in [[recording studio]]s.]]

In 1959, a vocal group called The Eligibles released a record called "Car Trouble", which contains two nonsense passages. When reversed, they reveal the phrases "And you can get my daughter back by 10:30, you bum!" and (perhaps inevitably) "Now, lookit here, cats, stop running these records backwards!" Peaking at #107 on the [[Billboard magazine]] charts that summer, "Car Trouble" is believed to be the first hit record to contain backmasking.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I1oTsFlwwzQ |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/I1oTsFlwwzQ |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live|title=The Eligibles – Car Trouble|date=June 4, 2016 |via=www.youtube.com}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

[[The Beatles]], which began incorporating ''[[musique concrète]]''-inspired tape manipulation techniques into their recordings in the mid-1960s, were responsible for popularizing the concept of backmasking.<ref name="sullivan">{{cite journal |last=Sullivan |first=Mark |title='More Popular Than Jesus': The Beatles and the Religious Far Right |journal=Popular Music |date=October 1987 |volume=6 |issue=3 |pages=313–326 |doi=10.1017/S0261143000002348|s2cid=190680105 }}</ref> Singer [[John Lennon]] and producer [[George Martin]] both claimed that they had discovered the backwards recording technique during the recording of 1966's ''[[Revolver (Beatles album)|Revolver]]'' (specifically the album tracks "[[Tomorrow Never Knows]]" and "[[I'm Only Sleeping]]") and the single "[[Rain (Beatles song)|Rain]]".<ref>{{cite news |last=Mugan |first=Chris |title=Subliminal advertising: The voice within |newspaper=The Independent | location = London |date=October 13, 2006 |url=http://arts.independent.co.uk/music/features/article1847509.ece |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930155232/http://arts.independent.co.uk/music/features/article1847509.ece |archive-date=September 30, 2007 |access-date=2007-03-10}}</ref> Lennon stated that, while under the influence of [[Cannabis (drug)|marijuana]], he accidentally played "Rain" in reverse and enjoyed the sound. After sharing the results with the other Beatles, the effect was used on [[tape loop]]s and the guitar solo on "Tomorrow Never Knows" and later on Lennon's vocals in the [[Coda (music)|coda]] of "Rain".<ref name="stevens">{{cite book |last=Stevens |first=John |title=The Songs of John Lennon: The Beatles Years |year=2002 |publisher=Berklee Press |isbn=978-0-634-01795-7 |pages=149, 155–156}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Aldridge |first=Alan |title=The Beatles Illustrated Lyrics |year=1991 |publisher=Houghton Mifflin |isbn=978-0-395-59426-1 |page=135 |quote=On the end of 'Rain' you hear me singing it backwards. We'd done the main thing at EMI and the habit was then to take the song home and see what you thought a little extra gimmick or what the guitar piece would be. So I got home about five in the morning, stoned out of my head, I staggered up to my tape recorder and I put it on, but it came out backwards, and I was in a trance in the earphones, what is it, what is it. It's too much, you know, and I really wanted the whole song backwards almost, and that was it. So we tagged it on the end.<!-- This quote needs to attributed to the speaker, I assume it was Lennon but have not verified from source -->}}</ref> According to Martin, the band had been [[Recording studio as an instrument|experimenting with changing the speeds of and reversing]] the "Tomorrow Never Knows" tapes, and Martin got the idea of reversing Lennon's vocals and guitar, which he did with a clip from "Rain". Lennon then liked the effect and kept it.<ref name="ev">{{cite web |last=Olcott |first=Evan |title=Audio Reversal In Popular Culture |url=http://www.triplo.com/ev/reversal/ |publisher = Triplo Press | access-date=2006-08-26|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060821143244/http://www.triplo.com/ev/reversal/ |archive-date = August 21, 2006}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last1=Giuliano |first1=Geoffrey |first2=Vrnda |last2= Devi |title=Glass Onion: The Beatles in their own words |year=1999 |publisher=Da Capo Press |isbn=978-0-306-80895-1 |page=265 |quote=I'd introduced John to backwards music on 'Rain' when I took his voice and turned it 'round when he was out on a coffee break. When I played it for him, he flipped.<!-- this quote needs to attributed to the person who said it. Was it the author of the book, his co author or are they quoting someone? -->}}</ref> "Rain" was the first Beatles song to feature a backmasked message: "Sunshine... Rain... When the rain comes, they run and hide their heads" ({{Audio-nohelp|Rain backwards.ogg|listen}}; the last line is the first line of the song in reverse).<ref>{{cite book |last=Cross |first=Craig |title=The Beatles: Day-by-Day, Song-by-Song, Record-by-Record | publisher = iUniverse |year=2005 | location = New York |isbn=978-0-595-34663-9 |page=425}}</ref>

===Controversies===
The Beatles were involved in the spread of backmasking both as a recording technique and as the center of a [[controversy]]. The latter has its roots in an event in 1969, when [[WNIC|WKNR-FM]] DJ [[Russ Gibb]] received a phone call from a student at [[Eastern Michigan University]] who identified himself as "Tom". The caller asked Gibb about a rumor that Beatle [[Paul McCartney]] had died, and claimed that the Beatles song "[[Revolution 9]]" contained a backward message confirming the rumor. Gibb played the song backwards on his turntable, and heard the phrase "turn me on, dead man".<ref name="reeve">{{cite book |last=Reeve |first=Andru J |title=Turn Me On, Dead Man: The Beatles And The "Paul-Is-Dead" Hoax |publisher=AuthorHouse |pages=11–13}}</ref> Gibb began telling his listeners about what he called "The Great Cover-up",<ref>{{cite journal |last=Yoakum |first=Jim |title=The Man Who Killed Paul McCartney |journal=[[Gadfly Online|The Gadfly]] |date=May–June 2000 |url=http://www.gadflyonline.com/archive/MayJune00/archive-mccartney.html |access-date=2007-10-12| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20071011195426/http://gadflyonline.com/archive/MayJune00/archive-mccartney.html| archive-date= October 11, 2007 | url-status= live}}</ref> and listeners cited other alleged backmasked phrases, including "Paul is a dead man, miss him, miss him, miss him", on "[[I'm So Tired]]".<ref name="reeve" />

The "[[Paul is dead]]" rumor popularized the idea of backmasking in [[popular music]].<ref name="sullivan" /> After Gibb's show, many more songs were found to contain phrases that sounded like known spoken languages when reversed. Initially, the search was done mostly by fans of [[rock music]]. In the late 1970s,{{sfn|Blecha|2004|p=49}} during the rise of the [[Christian right]] in the United States,<ref name="nyt">{{cite news |last=Holden |first=Stephen |date=1983-03-27 |title=Serious Issues Underlie a New Album from Styx |work=New York Times |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1983/03/27/arts/serious-issues-underlie-a-new-album-from-styx.html |access-date=2007-03-13}}</ref> [[fundamentalist Christian]] groups began to claim that backmasked messages could bypass the [[Consciousness|conscious mind]] and reach the [[unconscious mind]], where they would be [[Subliminal stimuli|accepted subliminally]] by the listener.{{sfn|Poundstone|1986|pp=227–232}} In 1981, Christian DJ Michael Mills began stating on Christian radio programs that [[Led Zeppelin]]'s "[[Stairway to Heaven]]" contained hidden [[Satanic panic|Satanic messages]] that were heard by the unconscious.<ref name="davis">{{cite book |editor-first=David |editor-last= Barker |title=33 1/3 Greatest Hits |volume=1 |publisher=Continuum |year=2007 |isbn=978-0-8264-1903-3 |chapter=Led Zeppelin IV |last=Davis |first=Erik |pages=212–214}}</ref>

In 1982, the [[Trinity Broadcasting Network]]'s [[Paul Crouch]] hosted a show with self-described neuroscientist William Yarroll, who argued that rock stars were cooperating with the [[Church of Satan]] to place backmasked messages on records,{{sfn|Denisoff|1988|p=289}} and fundamentalist Christian pastor Gary Greenwald held public lectures on dangers of backmasking and at least one mass record-smashing event.<ref name="devil">{{cite journal |last1=Vokey |first1=John R. |first2=J. Don |last2= Read |title=Subliminal messages: Between the devil and the media |journal=[[American Psychologist]] |date=November 1985 |pages=1231–1239 |doi=10.1037/0003-066X.40.11.1231 |volume=40 |pmid=4083611 |issue=11}}</ref> During the same year, thirty North Carolina teenagers, led by their pastor, claimed that singers had been possessed by Satan, who used their voices to create backward messages, and held a record-burning at their church.<ref name="robinson">{{cite web |title=Backmasking on records: Real, or hoax? |last=Robinson |first=B. A |publisher=[[Ontario Consultants on Religious Tolerance]] |url=http://www.religioustolerance.org/chr_cul5.htm |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20010825094757/http://www.religioustolerance.org/chr_cul5.htm |url-status=usurped |archive-date=August 25, 2001 |access-date=2006-07-04}}</ref>

Allegations of demonic backmasking were also made by [[social psychology|social psychologists]], parents, and critics of rock music,<ref name="wsj">{{cite news |last=Searcey |first=Dionne |title=Behind the Music: Sleuths Seek Messages In Lyrical Backspin |publisher=The Wall Street Journal |date=2006-01-09 |url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB113677367081541303 |access-date=2007-02-28}}</ref> as well as the [[Parents Music Resource Center]],<ref name="ev2">{{cite journal |last=Olcott |first=Evan |title=Backwards Messages|journal=Music and Technology |publisher=Triplo Press |url=http://www.triplo.com/ev/column/04.html | access-date=2007-03-13 |archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20061122142845/http://www.triplo.com/ev/column/04.html |archive-date = 2006-11-22}}</ref> which accused Led Zeppelin of using backmasking to promote [[Satanism]].<ref>{{cite web|last=Cox |first=Edward |title=Popular music restrictions in America in the late 1980s/early 90s |publisher=edcox.net |url=http://www.edcox.net/research/pre/rockmusic.asp |access-date=2007-06-21 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927033409/http://www.edcox.net/research/pre/rockmusic.asp |archive-date=September 27, 2007}}</ref>

===Legislation===
One result of the furor was the firing of five radio DJs who had encouraged listeners to search for backward messages in their record collections.{{sfn|Blecha|2004|p=49}} A more serious consequence was legislation by the state governments of [[Arkansas]] and [[California]]. The 1983 California bill was introduced to prevent backmasking that "can manipulate our behavior without our knowledge or consent and turn us into disciples of the [[Antichrist]]".{{sfn|Blecha|2004|p=51}} Involved in the discussion on the bill was a [[California State Assembly]] Consumer Protection and Toxic Materials Committee hearing, during which "Stairway to Heaven" was played backwards, and William Yaroll testified.{{sfn|Denisoff|1988|p=290}} The successful bill made the distribution of records with undeclared backmasking an invasion of privacy for which the distributor could be sued.<ref name="devil" /> The Arkansas law, which referenced albums by [[the Beatles]], [[Pink Floyd]], [[Electric Light Orchestra]], [[Queen (band)|Queen]] and [[Styx (band)|Styx]],<ref name="nyt" /> and mandated that records with backmasking include a warning sticker, passed unanimously in 1983, but was vetoed by [[Bill Clinton]] and failed a second vote to override his veto.<ref name="devil" /> United States House Resolution 6363, introduced in 1982 by Representative [[Bob Dornan]], proposed mandating a similar label federally;{{sfn|Poundstone|1983|pp=200–214}} the bill was referred to the Subcommittee on Commerce, Transportation and Tourism and never passed.<ref>{{USBill|97|HR|6363}} from [[THOMAS]]</ref> Government action was also called for in the legislatures of Texas and Canada.<ref name="devil" />

[[File:Compact Disc.jpg|thumb|right|The [[compact disc]] made finding backward messages difficult, causing interest in backmasking to decline.]]

With the advent of [[compact disc]]s in the 1980s, but prior to the advent of sound editing technology for [[personal computer]]s in the 1990s, it became more difficult to listen to recordings backwards, and the controversy died down.<ref name="wsj" />

===Resurgence===
Although the backmasking controversy peaked in the 1980s, the general belief in subliminal manipulation became more widespread in the United States during the following decade,<ref name="brannon" /> with belief in Satanic backmasking on records persisting into the 1990s.<ref>{{cite book |editor-first= Kenneth J. |editor-last= Bindas |title=America's Musical Pulse: Popular Music in Twentieth-Century Society|publisher=Praeger |year=1992 |isbn=978-0-275-94306-6 |last=Racocy |first=Rudolf E. |chapter=Introduction: The Importance of Music to People |pages=xvi–xvii}}</ref> At the same time, the development of [[digital audio editor|sound editing software]] with audio reversal features simplified the process of reversing audio,<ref name="wsj" /> which previously could only be done with full fidelity using a professional tape recorder.{{sfn|Poundstone|1986|pp=227–232}} The [[Sound Recorder (Windows)|Sound Recorder]] utility, included with [[Microsoft Windows]] from [[Windows 95]] to [[Windows XP]], allows one-click audio reversal,<ref>{{cite web|title=Help with Windows Sound Recorder |publisher=Capital Community College |url=http://www.ccc.commnet.edu/dl/dl-help/help-SoundRecorder.htm |access-date=May 9, 2007| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070429121019/http://www.ccc.commnet.edu/DL/dl-help/help-SoundRecorder.htm| archive-date= April 29, 2007 | url-status= live}}</ref> as does popular [[Open-source software|open source]] sound editing software [[Audacity (audio editor)|Audacity]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Audacity: Features |url=https://audacity.sourceforge.net/about/features|publisher = SourceForge.net | access-date=May 9, 2007| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070504192124/http://audacity.sourceforge.net/about/features| archive-date= May 4, 2007 | url-status= live}}</ref> Following the growth of the [[Internet]], backmasked message searchers used such software to create websites featuring backward music samples, which became a widely used method of exploring backmasking in popular music.<ref name="wsj" />

In January 2014, the first backmasked video was released as part of a Grammy Awards promotional campaign. A customized video player allowed the user to watch a piece of film accompanied by a music soundtrack both forwards and backwards. The backwards content contained a hidden visual story and the words 'music unleashes you' embedded into the reversed audio track.

Backmasking has been used as a [[sound recording and reproduction|recording]] technique since the 1960s. In the era of [[magnetic tape sound recording]], backmasking required that the source [[reel-to-reel audio tape recording|reel-to-reel]] tape actually be played backwards, which was achieved by first being wound onto the original takeup reel, then reversing the reels so as to use that reel as the source (this would reverse the stereo channels as well).<ref name="radar" />

Backmasked words are unintelligible when played forward, but when played backwards are clear speech.<ref name="robinson" /> Listening to backmasked audio with most [[phonograph|turntables]] requires disengaging the drive and rotating the album by hand in reverse<ref name="seattle" /> (though some can play records backwards).{{sfn|Poundstone|1986|pp=227–232}} With [[magnetic tape]], the tape must be reversed and spliced back into the cassette.<ref name="seattle" /> [[Compact disc]]s were difficult to reverse when first introduced, but [[digital audio editor]]s, which were first introduced in the late 1980s and became popular during the next decade,<ref>{{cite journal |title=Portable Digital Audio Workstations (Buyer's Guide) |journal=Theatre Crafts International |date=February 1, 1997 |url=http://www.accessmylibrary.com/premium/0286/0286-9303362.html |access-date=June 5, 2007}}</ref> allow easy reversal of audio from digital sources.<ref name="wsj" />

=== Music ===
During the [[Judas Priest]] subliminal message trial, lead singer [[Rob Halford]] admitted to recording the words "In the dead of the night, love bites" backwards into the track "Love Bites", from the 1984 album ''[[Defenders of the Faith]]''. Asked why he recorded the message, Halford stated that "When you're composing songs, you're always looking for new ideas, new sounds."<ref>{{cite news |title=Judas Priest's lead singer testifies |newspaper=New York Times |date=August 1, 1990 |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1990/08/01/arts/judas-priest-s-lead-singer-testifies.html |access-date=2007-04-20 |agency= Associated Press}}</ref>

Backmasking has been used by [[heavy metal music|heavy metal]] bands to deliberately insert messages in their lyrics or imagery. Bands have utilized Satanic imagery for commercial reasons.{{sfn|Blecha|2004|p=52}} For example, [[thrash metal]] band [[Slayer]] included at the start of the band's 1985 album ''[[Hell Awaits]]'' a deep backmasked voice repeatedly chanting "join us".<ref>{{cite web|last=Jenson |first=K. Thor |date=September 17, 2008 |work=Backmasked Tracks |title=#5: Slayer – Hell Awaits |publisher=[[UGO Networks]] |url=http://www.ugo.com/music/backmasked-tracks/?cur=hell-awaits |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110615014957/http://www.ugo.com/music/backmasked-tracks/?cur=hell-awaits |archive-date=2011-06-15 |access-date=June 13, 2009}}</ref> [[Cradle of Filth]], another band that has employed Satanic imagery, released a song entitled "Dinner at Deviant's Palace", consisting almost entirely of unusual sounds and a reversed reading of the [[Lord's Prayer]].<ref name="hardrock">{{cite web |last=Corbin |first=Dean |title=Hard Rock/Metal/Punk |publisher=Backmask Online |url=http://www.backmaskonline.com/hardrock.html |access-date=2006-08-06|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060819193952/http://www.backmaskonline.com/hardrock.html |archive-date = August 19, 2006}}</ref>

Artists often use backmasking of sounds or instrumental audio to produce interesting sound effects.<ref name="radar">{{cite web |last=Molitorisz |first=Sacha |title=Out of the box |work=Radar Blog |publisher=Sidney Morning Herald |date=June 23, 2004 |url=http://blogs.smh.com.au/radar/archives/2004/06/out_of_the_box.html |access-date=April 15, 2007| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070509114207/http://blogs.smh.com.au/radar/archives/2004/06/out_of_the_box.html| archive-date= May 9, 2007 | url-status= dead}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last=Federlein |first=David |title=Reader comments: backwards satanic messages |work=The [[Skeptic's Dictionary]] |date=April 1, 2005 |url=http://skepdic.com/comments/backcom.html |access-date=August 26, 2006}}</ref> One such sound effect is the [[reverse echo]]. When done on tape, such use of backmasking is known as ''[[reverse tape effects]]''. Backmasking has been used for artistic effect by [[Missy Elliott]] ("[[Work It (Missy Elliott song)|Work It]]",<ref name="rap">{{cite web |last=Corbin |first=Dean |title=Rap/Hip Hop |publisher=Backmask Online |url=http://www.backmaskonline.com/rap.html |access-date=2006-08-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060311023706/http://www.backmaskonline.com/rap.html| archive-date = March 11, 2006}}</ref>), [[Jay Chou]] ("[[You Can Hear]]"<ref>{{cite web |title=Jay Chou FAQ |publisher=Jay-Chou.net |url=http://www.jay-chou.net/forums/faq.php?faq=new_faq_item2 |access-date=2006-06-21 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060628030930/http://www.jay-chou.net/forums/faq.php?faq=new_faq_item2 |archive-date=June 28, 2006 }}</ref>) [[At the Drive-In]] ("[[300&nbsp;MHz]]"<ref name="modernrock">{{cite web |last=Corbin |first=Dean |title=Modern Rock |publisher=Backmask Online |url=http://www.backmaskonline.com/modernrock.html |access-date=2006-08-06| archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20060526195316/http://www.backmaskonline.com/modernrock.html| archive-date = May 26, 2006}}</ref>), [[Klaatu (band)|Klaatu]] ("[[Anus of Uranus]]"/"[[Silly Boys]]",<ref>{{cite web |last=Bradley |first=David |title=Klaatu Identities and Beatles Rumors |url=http://www.klaatu.org/klaatu1.html |date=January 24, 2004 |work=The Official Klaatu Homepage |access-date=2009-06-13}}</ref>) and [[Lacuna Coil]] ("[[Self Deception]]"<ref>{{cite web|title=Lacuna Coil – Frequently Asked Questions |publisher=Emptyspiral.net – The Lacuna Coil Community |date=October 12, 2006 |url=http://emptyspiral.net/modules.php?name=Content&pa=showpage&pid=69 |access-date=2006-08-23 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070707120824/http://emptyspiral.net/modules.php?name=Content&pa=showpage&pid=69 |archive-date=July 7, 2007}}</ref>)

A related technique is to reverse an entire instrumental track. [[John Lennon]] originally wanted to do so with "[[Rain (The Beatles song)|Rain]]", but objections by producer [[George Martin]] and bandmate [[Paul McCartney]] cut the backward section to 30 seconds.<ref name="stevens" />  [[Danish people|Danish]] band [[Mew (band)|Mew]]'s 2009 album ''[[No More Stories...]]'' contains a track, "New Terrain", which, when listened to in reverse, reveals a new song, entitled "Nervous".<ref>{{cite web |title=New Terrain / Nervous |publisher=MewX |date=July 28, 2009 |url=http://www.mewx.info/wp/?p=464 |access-date=September 10, 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110721224418/http://www.mewx.info/wp/?p=464|archive-date=2011-07-21}}</ref> Soul duo [[Gnarls Barkley]] released a companion version of their album ''[[The Odd Couple (album)|The Odd Couple]]'', an instrumental album called ''elpuoc ddo eht'', consisting of the original album, fused into a single 38:44-long track, and reversed. This album can be legally obtained{{clarify|date=September 2021}} by owners of the original, as it is meant to complement it, and be a resource to samplers.

The [[B-side]] of the 1966 [[Napoleon XIV]] single "[[They're Coming to Take Me Away, Ha-Haaa!]]" is a reversed version of the entire forwards record, titled "!aaaH-aH ,yawA eM ekaT oT gnimoC er'yehT". The forward version reached #3 in the US charts and #4 in the UK.<ref>{{cite web |title= They're Coming To Take Me Away, Ha-haaa by Napoleon XIV |editor-last= Wiser |editor-first= Carl |work=Songfacts |publisher= songfacts.com |url= http://www.songfacts.com/detail.php?id=675 |access-date= August 13, 2007}}</ref>

In [["Weird Al" Yankovic]]'s "Nature Trail to Hell", from 1984's ''[["Weird Al" Yankovic in 3-D]]'', Yankovic's backmasked voice declares that "[[Satan]] eats [[Cheez Whiz]]".<ref name="wsj" /> Another early example can be found on [[the J. Geils Band]] track "No Anchovies, Please", from 1980s album ''[[Love Stinks (album)|Love Stinks]]''. The message, disguised as a foreign-sounding language spoken under the narration, is, "It doesn't take a genius to tell the difference between chicken shit and chicken salad."{{sfn|Poundstone|1986|pp=227–32}}

Turn back! Turn back! Turn back! Turn back!" ({{Audio-nohelp|Elofireonhighreverce.ogg|listen}}).{{sfn|Patterson|2004|pp=173–174}} In 1983, ELO released an entire album, ''[[Secret Messages]]'', in response to the controversy.<ref name="ftm" /> Among the many backmasked messages on the album are: "Welcome to the big show" (2x);{{sfn|Poundstone|1986|pp=227–232}} "Thank you for listening"; "Look out there's danger ahead"; "Hup two three four"; "Time After Time"; and "You're playing me backwards".<ref name="elo" /> Styx also released an album in response to allegations of Satanic backmasking:<ref name="kilroy" /> 1983's ''[[Kilroy Was Here (album)|Kilroy Was Here]]'', which deals with an allegorical group called the "Majority for Musical Morality" that outlaws rock music.<ref name="nyt" /> A sticker on the album cover contains the message, "By order of the Majority for Musical Morality, this album contains secret backward messages", and the song "Heavy Metal Poisoning" does in fact contain the backmasked [[Latin]] words "''[[Annuit cœptis]], [[Novus ordo seclorum]]''" ("[God] has favored our undertakings; a new order for the ages")—part of the [[Great Seal of the United States|Great Seal]] which encircles the pyramid on the back of the American [[United States one-dollar bill|dollar bill]].{{sfn|Poundstone|1983|pp=200–214}}

[[Iron Maiden]]'s 1983 album ''[[Piece of Mind]]'' features a short backwards message, included by the band in response to allegations of Satanism that were surrounding them at the time.<ref>{{cite web|last=Narinian|first=Vartan|work=The Iron Maiden FAQ Part 1 |title= 21: Somewhere in Time cover references/neato things |date= August 8, 2012 |url= http://www.faqs.org/faqs/music/iron-maiden-faq/part1/ |publisher=faqs.org}}</ref> Between the songs "[[The Trooper]]" and "Still Life" is inebriated drummer [[Nicko McBrain]] doing an impression of [[Idi Amin|Idi Amin Dada]]: "'What ho', sed de t'ing wid de t'ree bonce [said the thing with the three heads]. Some messages chastise or poke fun at the listener who is playing the song backwards. ===Censorship===
[[File:Zappa.jpg|thumb|right|[[Frank Zappa]] used backmasking to avoid censorship.]]

Backmasking has been used to avoid censorship. On Frank Zappa's track "[[Hot Poop]]", from ''[[We're Only in It for the Money]]'' (1968), the released version contains at the end of its side "A" the backmasked message "Better look around before you say you don't care. / Shut your fucking mouth 'bout the length of my hair. / How would you survive / If you were alive / shitty little person?" This profanity-laced verse, originally from the song "Mother People", was censored by Verve Records, so Zappa edited the verse out, reversed it, and inserted it elsewhere in the album as "Hot Poop" (though even in the backward message the word "fucking" is censored).<ref>{{cite web |title=We're Only In It for the Money |last=Pacholski |first=Luke |url=http://www.lukpac.org/~handmade/patio/vinylvscds/money.html |publisher=lukpac.org |access-date=2006-07-29| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20060718065134/http://www.lukpac.org/~handmade/patio/vinylvscds/money.html| archive-date= July 18, 2006 | url-status= live}}</ref> On the same album, a backwards message can also be heard in "Harry, You're a Beast" with Madge saying, "Don't come in me, in me" repeatedly before she starts crying. In at least one bootleg version of the album, these words are very clear.<ref>{{cite web|title=Frank Zappa – Bootleg (We're only in it for the money 1967) Parte2|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OAEyXHOY0qk |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211221/OAEyXHOY0qk |archive-date=2021-12-21 |url-status=live|website=YouTube|date=October 22, 2009|access-date=August 1, 2016}}{{cbignore}}</ref>

Another example is found in [[Roger Waters]]' 1992 album ''[[Amused to Death]]'', on which Waters recorded a backward message, possibly critical of film director [[Stanley Kubrick]], who had refused to let Waters sample a breathing sound from ''[[2001: A Space Odyssey (film)|2001: A Space Odyssey]]''.<ref>{{cite web |last=Simon |first=Michael |title=Amused To Death Trivia |publisher=Roger Waters International Fan Club |url=http://www.rogerwaters.org/atdtrivia.html |access-date=2007-03-17 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070208094658/http://www.rogerwaters.org/atdtrivia.html |archive-date=February 8, 2007 }}</ref> The message appears in the song "[[Perfect Sense Part 1]]", in which Waters' backmasked voice says, "Julia, however, in light and visions of the issues of Stanley, we have changed our minds. We have decided to include a backward message, Stanley, for you and all the other book burners."<ref>{{cite web |title=Roger Waters – Perfect Sense |publisher=[[UGO Networks]] |url=http://www.ugo.com/music/backmasked-tracks/?cur=perfect-sense |access-date=2009-06-13 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110615225203/http://www.ugo.com/music/backmasked-tracks/?cur=perfect-sense |archive-date=2011-06-15 }}</ref>

On the other hand, backmasking can be used to [[censorship|censor]] words and phrases deemed inappropriate on [[radio edit]]s and "[[Expurgation|clean]]" album releases.<ref name="nelson">{{cite web|last=Nelson |first=Chris |title=Sticker Ban Policy: Family Values Or Consumer Fraud? |publisher=[[VH1]] |date=1998-09-08 |url=http://www.vh1.com/news/articles/501064/19980908/story.jhtml |access-date=February 3, 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071001091203/http://www.vh1.com/news/articles/501064/19980908/story.jhtml |archive-date=October 1, 2007}}</ref> For example, [[the Fugees]]' clean version of the album ''[[The Score (Fugees album)|The Score]]'' contains various backmasked [[profanity|profanities]];<ref name="nelson" /> thus, when playing the album backwards, the censored words are clearly audible among the backward gibberish.<ref>{{cite web |author=mcc |publisher=[[Everything2]] |title=Music that sounds better backward than forward |url=http://everything2.com/index.pl?node_id=677630 |access-date=2006-08-26}}</ref> When used with the word "shit", this type of backmasking results in a sound similar to "ish". As a result, "[[wikt:ish|ish]]" became a [[euphemism]] for "shit".<ref>{{cite web |editor-last=Rader |editor-first= Walter |work=The Online Slang Dictionary |author= Emily M. |orig-date= November 12, 1997 |date= March 5, 2011 |url= http://onlineslangdictionary.com/definition+of/ish |title= Definition of ish: noun |access-date= 2014-01-14}}</ref>

In [[Britney Spears]]' 2011 song "[[Till the World Ends]]", Spears says "if you want this good shit". However, on the official version, "shit" is reversed, creating the "ish" sound; therefore, the official version says "if you want this good ish". Backmasking is also used to censor the word "[[joint (cannabis)|joint]]" in the video for "[[You Don't Know How It Feels]]" by [[Tom Petty]], resulting in the line "Let's roll another tnioj".<ref name="TomPettySun">{{cite web|url=https://www.baltimoresun.com/1995/07/05/some-lyrics-are-revised-when-listeners-read-between-the-lines-changing-their-tunes/ |title=Some lyrics are revised when listeners read between the lines Changing Their Tunes |first=J. D. |last=Considine |work=[[The Baltimore Sun]] |date=July 5, 1995 |access-date = September 7, 2010}}</ref>

But on side two, the side with the song (Snow Blind) it had a black label with a small hole cut out where you could place the eraser side of a pencil, and play the album backwards to hear the backward message.<ref name="kilroy">{{cite news |last=Hoekstra |first=Dave |title=Styx makes a statement |newspaper=Suburban Sun-Times | location = Chicago |date=April 1, 1983}}</ref> In 1983, the band released a concept album, ''[[Kilroy Was Here (album)|Kilroy Was Here]]'', satirizing the [[Moral Majority]].

===Subliminal persuasion===

====Fundamentalist Christian groups====
Various [[fundamentalist Christian]] groups have declared that Satan uses—or Satan-influenced musicians use—backmasked messages to subliminally alter behavior. Pastor Gary Greenwald claimed that subliminal messages backmasked into rock music induce listeners towards sex and drug use.{{sfn|Vokey|2005|p=258}} Minister Jacob Aranza wrote in his 1982 book ''Backward Masking Unmasked'' that rock groups "are using backmasking to convey Satanic and drug related messages to the subconscious."{{sfn|Blecha|2004|p=49}} Christian DJ Michael Mills argued in 1981 that "the subconscious mind is being successfully affected by the repetition of beat and lyrics—being affected through a subliminal message."<ref>Mills, Michael. [http://blog.wfmu.org/freeform/2007/01/365_days_1_the_.html Hidden and Satanic Messages In Rock Music]. Radio interview, 1981, blog.wfmu.org. [http://blogfiles.wfmu.org/DP/2007/01/001_Michael_Mills_01_Introduction.mp3 Introduction], at 1:22</ref> Mills has toured America warning Christian parents about subliminal messages in [[rock music]].<ref name="devil" />

Some Christian websites have claimed that backmasking is widely used for Satanic purposes.<ref name="robinson" /> The web page for Alabama group Dial-the-Truth Ministries argues for the existence of Satanic backmasking in "[[Stairway to Heaven]]", saying that the song contains the backward message, "It's my sweet Satan&nbsp;... Oh I will sing because I live with Satan."<ref name="watkins" />

====PMRC====

|The message [of a piece of heavy metal music] may also be covert or subliminal. Sometimes subaudible tracks are mixed in underneath other, louder tracks. These are heard by the subconscious but not the conscious mind. Sometimes the messages are audible but are backward, called backmasking. There is disagreement among experts regarding the effectiveness of subliminals. We need more research on that.<ref name="pmrc">{{citation |author= United States Senate |year= 1985 |url= http://www.joesapt.net/superlink/shrg99-529/ |title= Record Labeling: Hearing before the Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation |work= United States Senate, Ninety-ninth Congress, First Session on Contents of Music and the Lyrics of Records (September 19, 1985) |location= Washington, DC |publisher= U.S. Government Printing Office |pages= 118–25 |via= Joesapt.net |access-date= 2007-06-09 |postscript= .}}</ref>

{{blockquote

While listening to a normal forward message (often somewhat nonsensical), one is simultaneously being treated to a backwards message (in other words, the lyric sounds like one set of words going forward, and a different set of words going backwards). ====Court cases====
Serial killer [[Richard Ramirez]], on trial in 1988, stated that [[AC/DC]]'s music, and specifically the song "[[Night Prowler (song)|Night Prowler]]" on ''[[Highway to Hell (album)|Highway to Hell]]'', inspired him to commit murder.<ref name="watkins">{{cite web |last=Watkins |first=Terry |title=Rock Music: The Devil's Advocate |publisher=Dial-the-Truth Ministries |url=http://www.av1611.org/rock.html |access-date=2007-03-17}}</ref> [[Reverse speech]] advocate [[David John Oates]] claimed that "[[Highway to Hell (song)|Highway to Hell]]", on the same album, contains backmasked messages including "I'm the law", "my name is Lucifer", and "she belongs in hell".<ref>{{cite web |last=Von Ulrich |first=Meyerratken |url=http://www.reversespeech.com/esotera.htm |title=Esotera Magazine |website=Reverse Speech |editor=George, Michael |translator=Galbraith, Evan |access-date=2007-03-17 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061230184800/http://www.reversespeech.com/esotera.htm |archive-date=December 30, 2006}} Originally published in ''Esotera'' magazine (June 1997).</ref> AC/DC's [[Angus Young]] responded that "you didn't need to play [the album] backwards, because we never hid [the messages]. We'd call an album ''Highway to Hell'', there it was right in front of them."<ref>{{cite interview|last=Young |first=Angus |subject-link=Angus Young |last2=Young |first2=Malcolm |subject-link2=Malcolm Young |interviewer=Sylvie Simmons |title=AC/DC Celebrate Their Quarter Century |url=http://www.sylviesimmons.com/interviews.php?interview=ACDC |work=[[Mojo (magazine)|Mojo]] |date=December 2004 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20061217215707/http://www.sylviesimmons.com/interviews.php?interview=ACDC |archive-date=December 17, 2006}}</ref>

In 1990, British [[heavy metal music|heavy metal]] band [[Judas Priest]] was sued over a [[suicide pact]] made by two young men in [[Nevada]]. The lawsuit by their families claimed that the 1978 Judas Priest album ''[[Stained Class]]'' contained hidden messages, including the forward subliminal words "Do it" in the song "[[Better by You, Better than Me]]" (a cover version of a [[Spooky Tooth]] song), and various backward subliminal messages. The case was dismissed by the judge for insufficient evidence of Judas Priest's placement of subliminal messages on the record,{{sfn|Vokey|2005|p=261}} and the judge's ruling stated that "The scientific research presented does not establish that subliminal stimuli, even if perceived, may precipitate conduct of this magnitude. There exist other factors which explain the conduct of the deceased independent of the subliminal stimuli."<ref>{{cite web |last=Sophia |first=Cassiel |title=Subliminal Suicide? |publisher=Metareligion |url=http://www.meta-religion.com/Secret_societies/Conspiracies/Mind_Control/subliminal_suicide.htm |access-date=2007-03-17}}</ref> Judas Priest members commented that if they wanted to insert subliminal commands in their music, messages leading to the deaths of their fans would be counterproductive, and they would prefer to insert the command "Buy more of our records."<ref>Van Taylor, David. KNPB Channel 5 Public Broadcasting, 1982: ''Dream Deceivers: The Story Behind James Vance Vs. Judas Priest''.</ref>

===Skepticism===
[[Skepticism|Skeptic]] [[Michael Shermer]] says that the emergence of the "Paul is dead" phenomenon was caused by [[pareidolia]]. Shermer argues that the human brain evolved with a strong [[pattern recognition]] ability that was necessary to process the large amount of [[noise]] in man's environment, but that today this ability leads to false positives.<ref>{{cite news |last=Shermer |first=Michael |title=Turn Me On, Dead Man |date=May 2005 |work =Scientific American |url=http://www.scientificamerican.com/article.cfm?id=turn-me-on-dead-man|access-date=2012-01-25}}</ref> [[Stanford University]] psychology professor Brian Wandell postulates that the observance of backward messages is a mistake arising from this pattern recognition facility, and argues that subliminal persuasion theories are "bizarre" and "implausible."<ref name="seattle">{{cite news |last=Holguin |first=Jaime |title=Backmasking unmasked! Music site's in heavy rotation |date=February 27, 2006 |newspaper=Seattle Times |url=http://seattletimes.nwsource.com/html/artsentertainment/2002828774_backmasking27.html |access-date=2007-02-28| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070218091502/http://seattletimes.nwsource.com/html/artsentertainment/2002828774_backmasking27.html| archive-date= February 18, 2007 | url-status= dead}}</ref> Rumors of backmasking in popular music have been described as auditory [[pareidolia]].{{sfn|Zusne|Jones|1989|p=77}} James Walker, president of Christian research group [[Watchman Fellowship]], states that "You could take a Christian hymn, and if you played it backwards long enough at different speeds, you could make that hymn say anything you want to"; Led Zeppelin publicist [[BP Fallon]] concurs, saying "Play anything backwards, and you'll find something." Eric Borgos of audio reversal website talkbackwards.com<ref>{{cite web |url= http://www.talkbackwards.com/ |title= Backmasking and Reverse Speech |work= TalkBackwards.com |access-date= 2007-06-05}}</ref> states that "Mathematically, if you listen long enough, eventually you'll find a pattern",<ref name="wsj" /> while Jeff Milner<ref name="milner" /> recounts, "Most people, when I show them the site, say that they're not able to hear anything, until, of course, I show them the reverse lyrics."<ref>{{cite news |last=Hopper |first=Tristin |title=Student sets up "backmasking" website |date=January 26, 2006 |work=The Charlatan | location = Carleton University, Ottawa |url=http://www.charlatan.ca/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=16842&Itemid=27 |access-date=2007-04-20}}</ref>

Audio engineer Evan Olcott says that messages by artists including Queen and Led Zeppelin are coincidental phonetic reversals, in which the spoken or sung [[phoneme]]s form new combinations of words when listened to backwards.<ref name="ev" /> Olcott states that "Actually engineering or planning a phonetic reversal is next to impossible, and even more difficult when trying to design it with words that fit into a song."<ref name="ev2" />

In 1985, [[University of Lethbridge]] psychologists John Vokey and J. Don Read conducted a study using [[Psalm 23]] from the [[Bible]], [[Queen (band)|Queen]]'s "[[Another One Bites the Dust]]", and other sound passages made up for the experiment. Vokey and Read concluded that if backmasking does exist, it is ineffective. Participants had trouble noticing backmasked phrases when the samples were played forwards, were unable to judge the types of messages (Christian, Satanic, or commercial), and were not led to behave in a certain way as a result of being exposed to the backmasked phrases. Vokey concluded that "we could find no effect of the meaning of engineered, backward messages on listeners' behaviour, either consciously or unconsciously."{{sfn|Vokey|2005|p=260}} Similar results to Vokey and Read's were obtained by D. Averill in 1982.<ref>{{cite news |last=Averill |first=D. |date=September 12, 1982 |title=Did the Devil make you do it? |newspaper =Tulsa World |location = Oklahoma |page=17}} Cited in {{Harvnb|Zusne|Jones|1989|p=79}}</ref> A 1988 experiment by T.E. Moore found "no evidence that listeners were influenced,
consciously or unconsciously, by the content of the backward messages."<ref name="brannon">{{cite book |last1= Brannon |first1= Laura A. |last2= Brock |first2= Timothy C. |chapter= The Subliminal Persuasion Controversy: Reality, Enduring Fable, and Polonius's Weasel |editor1-last= Shavitt |editor1-first= Sharon |editor2-first= Timothy C. |editor2-last= Brock |title= Persuasion: Psychological Insights and Perspectives |publisher= Allyn & Bacon |year= 1994 |url= http://courses.umass.edu/psyc392a/pdf/brannon&brock.1994.pdf |isbn= 978-0-205-15143-1 |access-date= August 23, 2018 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120722092128/http://courses.umass.edu/psyc392a/pdf/brannon%26brock.1994.pdf |archive-date= July 22, 2012 }}</ref> In 1992, an experiment found that exposure to backward messages did not lead to significant changes in attitude.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Swart |first1=L. C. |first2=C. L. |last2= Morgan |title=Effects of subliminal backward-recorded messages on attitudes |journal=Perceptual and Motor Skills |date=December 1992 |pages=1107–13 |pmid=1484773 |doi=10.2466/PMS.75.8.1107-1113 |volume=75 |issue=3 Pt 2}}</ref> Psychology professor Mark D. Allen says that "delivering subliminal messages via backward masking is totally and ridiculously impossible".<ref>{{cite news|last=Glover |first=Melanie |newspaper=The California Aggie |title=Backmasking: Satan, marijuana and Cheez Whiz |date=January 19, 2006 |url=http://media.www.californiaaggie.com/media/storage/paper981/news/2006/01/19/ArtsEntertainment/Backmasking.Satan.Marijuana.And.Cheez.Whiz-1477952-page2.shtml |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070927234234/http://media.www.californiaaggie.com/media/storage/paper981/news/2006/01/19/ArtsEntertainment/Backmasking.Satan.Marijuana.And.Cheez.Whiz-1477952-page2.shtml |archive-date=September 27, 2007 |access-date=2007-03-01}}</ref>

The finding of backward Satanic messages has been explained as caused by the [[observer-expectancy effect]]. The ''[[Skeptic's Dictionary]]'' states that "you probably won't hear [backmasked] messages until somebody first points them out to you. Perception is influenced by expectation and expectation is affected by what others prime you for."<ref>{{cite book |last=Carroll |first=Robert Todd |author-link=Robert Todd Carroll |chapter= Backwards satanic messages (backmasking) |title= The Skeptic's Dictionary |edition= online |date=December 28, 2006 |url=http://www.skepdic.com/backward.html |access-date=2007-04-18| archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20070501094630/http://www.skepdic.com/backward.html| archive-date= May 1, 2007 | url-status= live}}</ref> In 1984, S. B. Thorne and P. Himelstein found that "when vague and unfamiliar stimuli are presented, [test subjects] are highly likely to accept suggestions, particularly when the suggestions are presented by someone with prestige and authority."<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Thorne |first1=Stephen B. |first2 =Philip | last2 = Himelstein |year=1984 |title=The Role of Suggestion in the Perception of Satanic Messages in Rock-And-Roll Recordings |journal=Journal of Psychology |volume=116 |issue=2 |pages=245–8|doi=10.1080/00223980.1984.9923643}} Cited in {{cite book |last=Hicks |first=Robert D. |title=In Pursuit of Satan: The Police And the Occult |publisher=Prometheus |year=1991 | location = Buffalo, NY |isbn=978-1-59102-219-0 |page=306}}</ref> Vokey and Read concluded from their 1985 experiment that "the apparent presence of backward messages in popular music is a function more of active construction on the part of the perceiver than of the existence of the messages themselves."<ref name="devil" />

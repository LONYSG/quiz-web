The '''Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling and Wake''' is an annual event held on the [[Bank holiday#In the United Kingdom|Spring Bank Holiday]] at [[Brockworth, Gloucestershire#Cooper's Hill|Cooper's Hill]] in [[Brockworth]], Gloucestershire, England.<ref name="Gu130527"/> Participants race down the {{convert|200|yd|adj=on}} long hill chasing a wheel of [[Double Gloucester cheese]]. Its earliest known written attestation was in 1836, though it is possibly even older.<ref name="BBCOrigins" /> Now people from a wide range of countries take part in the competition as well. ''[[The Guardian]]'' in 2013 called it a "world-famous event", with winners coming from Australia, Belgium, Canada, Egypt, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, and the United States.<ref name="Glo2022"/>

==Format==
[[File:Cheese Rolling 2006 -Two stragglers in the ladies race.jpg|thumb|upright=1.3|View down [[Brockworth, Gloucestershire#Cooper's Hill|Cooper's Hill]] during the 2006 race]]
From the top of Cooper's Hill, a {{convert|7-9|lb|0|abbr=off|lk=on|adj=on}} round of [[Gloucester cheese|Double Gloucester]] [[cheese]] is sent rolling down the hill, which is {{convert|287|m}} high, {{convert|200|yd}} long, and has a [[Grade (slope)|slope]] of around 50 percent.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.greatbritishlife.co.uk/magazines/cotswold/22607631.cheese-rolling-coopers-hill-gloucester/|title=Cheese Rolling on Cooper's Hill, Gloucester|date=4 February 2010|website=Great British Life}}</ref><ref name=":0" /> Competitors then start racing down the hill after the cheese.<ref name="BBC, 2013" />

The competitors aim to catch the cheese; however, it has around a one-second [[Head start (positioning)|head start]] and can reach high speeds, enough to knock over and injure a spectator. The first person over the finish line at the bottom of the hill wins the cheese, even though the cheese is never actually caught.<ref>{{cite web | title=Gloucestershire Cheese Rolling Everything you need to know | date=5 June 2025 | url=https://www.soglos.com/event/outdoors/gloucestershire-cheese-rolling/17943/ }}</ref> Multiple races are held during the day, with separate events for men and women. Participants in the downhill races must be over the age of 18.<ref name="BBC2025" />

[[File:Cheese Rolling 2006 - Ladies uphill race.jpg|thumb|upright|2006 Ladies Uphill Race]]
There are separate uphill races. Considerably less hazardous, these races are open to minors. In the past (e.g. 2014), the uphill race categories have been "[[Boy|boys]] 14 and under" and "[[Girl|girls]] 14 and under". In the most recent races in 2025, the [[Child|children]]'s uphill categories were "under 11" and "11 and over". The adult's uphill race was mixed.<ref name="BBC2025">{{cite web |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c230jj5m1yro|title=Your 2025 guide to Gloucestershire cheese rolling |date=16 May 2025 |website=BBC.co.uk}}</ref>

{{Cn span|The number of races held during the event has varied over the years, but there are usually four downhill races (three men's, one women's) and a number of uphill races (adults and children).|date=May 2026}}

In the 2013 competition, a foam replica replaced the cheese for [[Health and Safety Executive|safety]] reasons, but a real cheese wheel was restored the following year.<ref name="b929">{{cite news| title=Gloucestershire cheese-rolling champ 'doesn't really like cheese' | work=BBC News | date=26 May 2014 | url=https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-27575303 | access-date=8 May 2025}}</ref>

==History==
[[File:A view from the top of Cooper's Hill, Gloucestershire, on 3 October 2005.jpg|thumb|A view down [[Brockworth, Gloucestershire#Cooper's Hill|Cooper's Hill]], from the start point of the race to the finish, taken on 3 October 2005]]
{{Anchor|Origins}}

The first written evidence of cheese rolling is found in a message written to the Gloucester [[town crier]], published in [[Berrow's Worcester Journal]] on 9 June 1836.<ref name="BBCOrigins">{{Cite web |last=Knibbs |first=Steve |date=2026-05-21 |title=Cooper's Hill cheese rolling: origins, myths and history |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cz024gnm9z4o |access-date=2026-05-26 |website=[[BBC News]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> Despite this, the tradition is believed to be even older. The BBC states that there are "suggestions it began 10 years earlier" than the 1836 date.<ref name="BBCOrigins" /> National Geographic said that the "first recorded Gloucestershire Cheese-Roll happened in 1826, although the tradition is possibly older".<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Burch |first=James |date=2018-06-12 |title='King of the Hill' Sets Lifetime Record in British Cheese-Rolling Competition |url=https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/why-people-risk-their-lives-chasing-cheese |access-date=2026-05-26 |website=[[National Geographic]] |language=en}}</ref> The BBC stated in 2025 that "theories suggest it may have started at least 600 years ago".<ref>{{Cite web |last=Jenkins |first=Sammy |last2=Harcombe |first2=Chloe |date=2025-05-16 |title=Cheese Rolling 2025: Your guide to the Gloucestershire tradition |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c230jj5m1yro |access-date=2026-05-26 |website=[[BBC News]] |language=en-GB}}</ref> Dorothy Gladys Spicer stated in the 1954 book "Yearbook of English Festivals" that the tradition had "been famous for at least five hundred years."<ref name="YoEF">{{Cite book |last=Spicer |first=Dorothy Gladys |title=Yearbook of English Festivals |publisher=[[H. W. Wilson Company]] |year=1954 |location=New York |pages=238–239}}</ref>

The event previously took place each [[Whit Monday]],<ref name="YoEF" /> but was moved to the Spring Bank Holiday.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2024-02-16 |title=Cheese rolling contest and other traditional UK competitions |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/newsround/68307814 |access-date=2026-05-26 |work=[[Newsround]] |language=en-GB}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Lumley |first=Sarah |date=2026-05-24 |title=Everything you need to know ahead of annual cheese rolling race |url=https://www.gloucestershirelive.co.uk/whats-on/everything-you-need-know-ahead-10979706 |access-date=2026-05-26 |website=[[Gloucestershire Live]] |language=en}}</ref> According to Jean Jefferies, who has written a book about, and set up the original website for, the event:<ref name="BBCOrigins" /> "It seems originally the event took place at midsummer", was moved "at some point" to Whit Monday, and then moved to the Spring Bank Holiday in 1967.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |date=2005-05-10 |title=Roll that cheese in 2006! |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/gloucestershire/content/articles/2005/05/10/cheese_rolling_feature.shtml |access-date=2026-05-26 |website=[[BBC News]] |language=en-gb}}</ref>

Two possible origins have been proposed for the ceremony. First, it may have evolved from a requirement for maintaining grazing rights on the common.<ref name="Spicer">{{cite news |date=7 May 2009 |title=Cheese Rolling: A brief history |website=[[BBC News]] |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/gloucestershire/content/articles/2009/05/07/cheese_rolling_history_feature.shtml |access-date=30 May 2018}}</ref><ref name="Anon 2012">Anon. "Cheese Rolling on Coopers Hill, Exhibition", [[Gloucester City Museum & Art Gallery|Gloucester City and Folk Museums]], Gloucester, 14 July 2012.</ref> Second, there may be [[pagan]] origins for the custom of rolling objects down the hill. It is thought that bundles of burning brushwood were rolled down the hill to represent the birth of the New Year after winter. Connected with this belief is the traditional scattering of buns, biscuits and sweets at the top of the hill by the [[Master of Ceremonies]].<ref name="capture1997to2007">{{cite web|url=http://cheese-rolling.co.uk/past_events.htm |title=Previous years/cheese-rolling.co.uk|year=2007|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20071017104137/http://cheese-rolling.co.uk/past_events.htm |url-status=dead |archive-date=17 October 2007}}</ref> This is said to be a fertility rite to encourage the fruits of harvest.<ref name="Anon 2012" />

In 1982, a team of students from the [[University of Bristol]] filmed the 31 May event using film cameras, with one camera overcranked to produce [[slow motion]].<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nmR9r7mmrco |title=Cheese Rolling Day May 31, 1982 |date=19 May 2020 |language=en |publisher=Barbara Wyatt |place=[[Brockworth]] |orig-date=Recorded 31 May 1982 |people=Jamie Hartzell, Barbara Wyatt, Frank Passingham, Jonathan Fisher, Mike Dorrell, Michael Hicks, John Adams, Keesian Pender, Pru McEwen, Deborah Perkin, Nick Ferguson, Steve Lewis, Jo Moss, Roger Wilson}}</ref><ref name="hartzell">{{Cite web|url= https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nmR9r7mmrco|title=Cheese Rolling Day May 31, 1982 |website=youtube.com |date=18 May 2020 |access-date=12 November 2022}}</ref>

[[File:A_race_at_the_Cooper's_Hill_Cheese_Rolling_and_Wake,_on_Cooper's_Hill,_Brockworth,_Gloucestershire,_on_27_May_2013.jpg|thumb|A race on 27 May 2013]]

In 1993, sixteen people were injured, four of them seriously, during the event.<ref name="Anon 2012" /> In 2009, safety concerns were raised after 15,000 spectators arrived, when there was only space for around 5,000. These concerns led to the organisers cancelling the 2010 event.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Morris |first1=Steven |title=Gloucestershire's annual cheese rolling cancelled due to health and safety fears |url=https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2010/mar/12/gloucestershire-cheese-rolling-cancelled |work=The Guardian |date=12 March 2010}}</ref> Despite the cancellation, around 100 people attended and held an unofficial event.<ref>{{cite news |title='Unofficial' cheese rolling event |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/england/gloucestershire/8714229.stm |date=31 May 2010}}</ref>

In 2011, a new two-day ticketed event was proposed in order to address the safety concerns raised in previous years and to allow the event to continue operating.<ref>{{Cite web |date=13 January 2011 |title=Save the Gloucestershire Cheese Rolling campaign launched |url=http://www.SoGlos.com/sport-outdoor/30974/Save-the-Gloucestershire-Cheese-Rolling-campaign-launched |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110716102140/https://www.soglos.com/sport-outdoor/30974/Save-the-Gloucestershire-Cheese-Rolling-campaign-launched | url-status=dead |archive-date=16 July 2011 |access-date=3 July 2016 |website=soglos.com}}</ref> The proposals were received negatively due to the cost of tickets, and the proposed event was cancelled following the organisers receiving abuse.<ref>{{cite news |title=Gloucestershire's cheese rolling cancelled after abuse |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-12851174 |work=BBC News |date=24 March 2011}}</ref> Despite the cancellation, the event continued unofficially with around 200 people attending.<ref name="BBC 2011 cancellation">{{cite news|url=https://www.BBC.co.uk/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-13594733|title=Coopers Hill cheese-rolling fans hold unofficial race|website=bbc.co.uk|date=31 May 2011|publisher=[[BBC News online]]|access-date=31 May 2011}}</ref> The event has continued without official management or planning alongside the Council Safety Advisory Group.

In 2020 and 2021, the event was cancelled as a result of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Gloucestershire Cheese Rolling 2022 on Coopers Hill in Gloucester |url=https://www.soglos.com/sport-outdoor/27837/Gloucestershire-Cheese-Rolling |access-date=19 March 2022 |website=SoGlos |date=9 March 2022}}</ref> It returned on Sunday 5 June 2022, ending a two-year absence.<ref name="back2022">{{cite web |url=https://www.gloucestercheeserolling.co.uk/when-is-the-cheese-rolling-taking-place- |title=When is the Gloucestershire Cheese Rolling taking place in 2022? |website=gloucestercheeserolling.co.uk |year=2022}}</ref><ref name="Glo2020" />{{Efn|The spring bank holiday was moved to 2 June, due to the [[Platinum Jubilee of Elizabeth II]].<ref name="Guinness">{{Cite web |title=Guinness World Records - Most wins of a cheese-rolling race |url=https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/754825-most-wins-of-a-cheese-rolling-race |access-date=24 April 2024 |website=guinnessworldrecords.com |date=5 June 2022}}</ref>}}

The 2026 edition of the event marked the first time in the competition's recorded history that every men's downhill race was won by non-British competitors, with the winning runners hailing from [[Germany]], the [[Netherlands]], and the [[United States]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kendall |first=Ellie |last2=Clark |first2=Daniel |last3=Denton |first3=Maxine |date=2026-05-25 |title=Cheese rolling 2026 updates as iconic event takes place |url=https://www.gloucestershirelive.co.uk/whats-on/whats-on-news/cheese-rolling-2026-updates-iconic-10982268 |access-date=2026-06-22 |website=Gloucestershire Live |language=en}}</ref>

==Cheese==
[[File:Master of Ceremonies Rob Seex, at the Cooper's Hill Cheese-Rolling and Wake, circa 2006 (cropped).jpg|thumb|Photo from 2006 or earlier of [[master of ceremonies]] Rob Seex,<ref>{{cite news |title=Our Crazy County - Photos: Cheese Rolling 2009 |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/gloucestershire/content/image_galleries/cheese_rolling_2009_gallery.shtml |access-date=27 May 2026 |work=[[BBC]] |language=en-gb}}</ref> holding a [[Double Gloucester cheese]]]]
The cheese currently used in the event is {{convert|7–9|lb|adj=on|abbr=off|0}} [[Double Gloucester cheese|Double Gloucester]], a hard cheese traditionally made in a circular shape.<ref>{{cite web |title=Double Gloucester |url=http://www.BritishCheese.com/doublegloucester |website=BritishCheese.com |publisher=British Cheese Board |year=2013 |access-date=31 May 2013|url-status=live |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110726112327/http://www.britishcheese.com/doublegloucester |archive-date=26 July 2011}}</ref> Each is protected for the rolling by a wooden casing round the side, and it is decorated with ribbons at the start of the race. Formerly, three cheeses were presented by parishioners, and the cheeses were usually rolled by them. A collection is usually made now to purchase them, as well as sweets, and also to provide prize money.<ref name="Anon 2012" />

Since 1988, the cheese has been supplied by local cheesemaker Diana Smart and her son Rod from their [[Churcham]] farm.<ref name="BBC, 2013" /><ref>{{Cite web|url= https://www.thecheeseshed.com/from-the-mongers-mouth/smarts-great-cheese-keeps-rolling|title=Smarts: Great Cheese Keeps Rolling - The Cheese Shed|website=thecheeseshed.com}}</ref><ref name="Facts">{{cite web |url=http://Cheese-Rolling.co.uk/more_cheese_rolling_facts.htm |title=More Cheese Rolling facts and information |website=Cheese-Rolling.co.uk |access-date=20 October 2017 |url-status=dead |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20130526042830/http://www.cheese-rolling.co.uk/more_cheese_rolling_facts.htm |archive-date=26 May 2013}}</ref> In May 2013, a police inspector warned all of those involved in planning the event, including the 86-year-old cheesemaker, that they could be held responsible for any injuries.<ref name="BBC, 2013">{{Cite news |date=24 May 2013 |title=Cheese-maker warned against supplying Gloucester cheese-rolling |publisher=[[BBC News Online]] |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-22639675}}</ref> Diana Smart died in 2021.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.gloucestershirelive.co.uk/whats-on/whats-on-news/gloucestershire-pub-cheesemaker-who-play-9306885 | title=The pub named after a crazy tradition | date=26 May 2024 }}</ref>

In 2013, organisers of the event switched to a lightweight foam version of the cheese for safety reasons.<ref name="b929"/> In the second race of 2013, Australian Caleb Stalder managed to catch the fake cheese and claim victory despite being some way behind the leaders.<ref name="cheese2013">{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-22681708|title=Gloucestershire cheese-rolling takes place despite warning|website=BBC News|date=27 May 2013}}</ref> In 2014, the organisers returned to using real cheese.<ref name="b929" />

==Injuries==
Due to the steepness and uneven surface of Cooper's Hill, there are usually several injuries each year.<ref name="bbc2023">{{Cite web |last=Morgan |first=William |date=1 June 2023 |title=Cheese Rolling organisers asked to give detailed plan after six hospitalised |url=https://www.gloucestershirelive.co.uk/news/gloucester-news/cheese-rolling-2023-put-six-8487642 |access-date=1 April 2024 |website=Gloucestershire Live |language=en}}</ref> [[St John Ambulance]] have previously provided first aid cover at the event; however, this stopped in 2012 when the event was no longer being officially managed.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-18322060 |title=Unofficial Gloucestershire cheese-rolling attracts hundreds |work=BBC News |date=4 June 2012}}</ref> The lack of official medical provision on site has led to concerns from the [[local resilience forum]] about the safety of the event.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2 June 2023 |title=Gloucestershire cheese rolling event strains emergency services |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-england-gloucestershire-65776772 |access-date=1 April 2024 |work=BBC News |language=en-GB}}</ref>

Canadian competitor Delaney Irving won the ladies' race in 2023, despite finishing unconscious, and only learning of her victory in the medical enclosure.<ref name="ko">{{cite web|author=Nadeem Badshah |url=https://www.theguardian.com/food/2023/may/29/woman-wins-coopers-hill-cheese-rolling-race-despite-being-knocked-out |title= Woman wins UK cheese rolling race despite being knocked unconscious |work= theguardian.com |date=29 May 2023}}</ref> Six competitors were taken to hospital by ambulance for treatment following the event.<ref name="bbc2023" />

==Cheese-rolling in popular culture==
* '''1948''': ''Cheese Rolling on Cooper's Hill'', a painting by [[Charles March Gere]], is part of the [[Museum of Gloucester]] collection, and depicts the event.<ref name="gere">{{Cite web|url=https://www.visitgloucester.co.uk/things-to-do/history-and-heritage/cheese-rolling-in-gloucester|title=Cheese Rolling in Gloucester |website=visitgloucester.co.uk |access-date=12 November 2022 |archive-date=19 January 2022|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220119163642/https://www.visitgloucester.co.uk/things-to-do/history-and-heritage/cheese-rolling-in-gloucester}}</ref>

[[File:The Cheese Rollers, Shurdington (geograph 6190978).jpg|thumb|The Cheese Rollers Pub & Restaurant, Shurdington, 2019]]

* '''Early 1970s''': The New Inn pub was renamed 'The Cheese Rollers Bar & Restaurant' in the early 1970s.<ref name="pub" /> Located in the neighbouring village of [[Shurdington]], it is named after the event, and has a collection of previous cheese casings along with photographs and articles about the event.<ref name="pub">{{Cite web|url=https://www.gloucestershirepubs.co.uk/pubs/new-inn-cheese-rollers-inn-main-road-shurdington-gl51-5xj/|title=New Inn / Cheese Rollers Inn, Main Road, Shurdington GL51 5XJ |website=gloucestershirepubs.co.uk |date=8 May 2022 |access-date=30 June 2022}}</ref>

* '''1997''': The American author [[Thomas Pynchon]], in his novel ''[[Mason & Dixon]]'', describes the rolling of a fictitious "octuple Gloucester," a wheel 512 times more massive than ordinary, in the Gloucestershire village of [[Randwick, Gloucestershire|Randwick]].

* '''2006''': Comedian [[Bridget Christie]] performed a show about cheese rolling at the [[Edinburgh Festival Fringe]], inspired by seeing the Cooper's Hill event as a child.<ref name="m778">{{cite web |last=Christie |first=Bridget |title=Bridget Christie: Ritual Britain made me proud to be British |website=The Guardian |date=28 June 2021 |url=https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2021/jun/28/bridget-christie-ritual-britain-crypt-gallery-ben-edge-museum-of-folklore |access-date=27 May 2025}}</ref>

* '''2008''': Cheese rolling was featured in the first episode of the [[Five (channel)|Five]] television series ''[[Rory & Paddy's Great British Adventure]]'', broadcast on 13 August 2008, and was described as "the grandaddy of weird sports" by presenters [[Rory McGrath]] and [[Paddy McGuinness]].<ref name="gloc2008" />

* '''2009''': The video for the single [[Can You Give It?|"Can You Give It"]] by [[The Maccabees (band)|the Maccabees]] was filmed around the event, and featured [[Chris Anderson (cheese roller)|Chris Anderson]], the most successful known cheese roller.
* '''2010''': English author [[Terry Pratchett|Terry Prachett]] referenced cheese rolling in his urban fantasy novel, ''[[I Shall Wear Midnight]]''. Pratchett made the cheese roll a competition between the humans and the fairy folk known as the [[Nac Mac Feegle]] who used a sentient cheese, Horace, to win the race.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Pratchett |first=Terry |title=I Shall Wear Midnight |publisher=Doubleday |year=2010 |isbn=0385611072 |edition=1st |language=en}}</ref>

* '''2018''': The contest was the subject of the [[BBC One]] programme ''The Great Cheese Chase''.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b0bb6l40|title=The Great Cheese Chase - BBC One|website=BBC|access-date=6 August 2018}}</ref> The contest was also featured in the German reality show ''Joko gegen Klaas – Das Duell um die Welt'', in which former German footballer Thorsten Legat was expected to participate but ultimately refused to do so.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.prosieben.de/serien/joko-gegen-klaas-das-duell-um-die-welt|title="Das Duell um die Welt - Team Joko gegen Team Klaas" - neue Folge am 10. Februar 2024|date=4 October 2022|website=www.prosieben.de}}</ref>

* '''2019''': ''Let's Roll'' is a short film directed by Chris Thomas<ref>{{Cite AV media |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZJ0vXN9MeZo |title=LET'S ROLL: Cheese Rolling Short Film (Comedy) |date=8 May 2020 |last=Thomas |first=Chris |language=en |place=[[Brockworth]]}}</ref> about a teenage girl, Antonia (Amy Bowden), attempting to emulate her brother's success in cheese rolling. The film was screened at the BAFTA-qualifying Norwich and Edinburgh film festivals.<ref name="norwich">{{Cite web|url=https://www.norwichfilmfestival.co.uk/films/official-selection-2019/lets-roll/ |title=Let's Roll |year=2019|publisher=Norwich Film Festival}}</ref><ref name="ed">{{Cite web|url=https://www.edfilmfest.org.uk/2019/lets-roll |title=Let's Roll |year=2019|publisher=Edinburgh International Film Festival}}</ref>
